Match the law to a scenario — e.g. lifting a heavy box = Manual Handling Regs; wearing gloves with bleach = COSHH/PPER.

📋 Safety documents

Risk assessment — key terms

  • Hazard: something that could physically harm someone
  • Level of risk: low / medium / high — likelihood of harm
  • Control measure: action taken to reduce/avoid the hazard

A risk assessment must be reviewed regularly and updated when new risks appear.

Accident forms

Completed by law after any workplace accident. Must record: who was involved, description of accident/injury, what the hazard was, how it could have been prevented, further action taken (e.g. updating the risk assessment).

🦠 Food safety: HACCP

HACCP (Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point) is the food safety management system used to control risk at every stage:

StageHazardControl point
Receipt of foodDamaged/incorrect temperature deliveriesCheck high-risk food is 0–5°C, frozen −18 to −22°C; refuse if wrong
StorageCross-contamination, wrong temperature, out of dateCorrect shelf, FIFO stock rotation, log temperatures
PreparationCross-contamination, bacteria growthColour-coded boards, hand washing, check use-by dates
CookingPhysical/chemical/microbiological contaminationGood hygiene, no jewellery, food probe to 75°C core temp
ServingFood held too long / wrong temperatureHot food at 63°C max 2 hrs; cold food at 5°C or below; colour-coded tongs

3 types of food hazard

Chemical (e.g. cleaning products), Physical (e.g. metal/plastic), Microbiological (e.g. Salmonella bacteria).

📜 Food & the law

LawWhat it covers

Food Safety (General Food Hygiene) Regs 1995Anyone handling food "from field to plate" must do so hygienically — HACCP used throughout
Food Labelling Regulations 2006 / Food Hygiene Regs 2006Labels must show: name, ingredients, allergens (in bold), storage/cooking instructions, origin, weight, use-by/best-before date, manufacturer's name & address, nutrition info

🩺 Symptoms of food-induced ill health

Visible symptomsNon-visible symptoms
Diarrhoea, vomiting, pale/sweating/chills, bloating, weight lossNausea, stomach-ache/cramps, wind, constipation, painful joints, headache, weakness

Allergic / anaphylactic reaction

Visible: red skin, raised rash, vomiting, swelling of lips/eyes, difficulty breathing.
Non-visible: swelling of tongue/throat, nausea, abdominal pain.
Anaphylaxis is a severe, potentially fatal reaction — treated with an adrenaline injection (EpiPen).

🧤 Preventing food-induced ill health

Cross-contamination

Wash hands before/after handling raw meat or high-risk food; use colour-coded boards/knives; wash hands after the toilet, sneezing or touching rubbish.

Physical contamination

Avoid hair, plasters, jewellery, broken glass, packaging in food — follow hygiene rules, keep areas clean, check deliveries, wash fruit/veg, use tongs/gloves.

🔍 The Environmental Health Officer (EHO)

Appointed by local authorities to protect public health and enforce food safety law.

During an inspection, the EHO checks

Premises are clean, equipment is safe, pest control is in place, waste is disposed of properly, staff have food hygiene training, food is stored/cooked correctly with correct dates, and a HACCP plan exists.

The EHO can

  • Take photos and food samples
  • Check all records (temperatures, cleaning, training)
  • Remove hazardous food / close the premises if there's a health risk
  • Give evidence in court if a business is prosecuted

After inspection, the business gets a food hygiene rating (out of 5 — 5 is "very good"), published online and on a sticker.

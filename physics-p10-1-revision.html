Key Concepts

Force ↑ → Acceleration ↑

A bigger resultant force on the same mass produces a bigger acceleration. They are directly proportional.

Mass ↑ → Acceleration ↓

A larger mass is harder to accelerate. For the same force, doubling the mass halves the acceleration.

Resultant Force & Direction

Acceleration always acts in the same direction as the resultant force. If the force is in the same direction as motion → object speeds up. If opposite → object slows down (negative acceleration).

The Formula

F = m × a
resultant force (N) = mass (kg) × acceleration (m/s²)
a = F ÷ m
m = F ÷ a
Worked Example 1

Calculate the resultant force on an object of mass 6.0 kg with acceleration 3.0 m/s².

F = m × a
F = 6.0 × 3.0
F = 18.0 N
Worked Example 2

Calculate the acceleration of an object of mass 5.0 kg acted on by a resultant force of 40 N.

a = F ÷ m
a = 40 ÷ 5.0
a = 8.0 m/s²

Newton's Second Law

The acceleration of an object is proportional to the resultant force acting on it, and inversely proportional to its mass. The acceleration is always in the same direction as the resultant force.
✔ Exam Tip Always use the resultant force (the total/net force), not just one force, in F = ma. Make sure units are consistent: force in N, mass in kg, acceleration in m/s².
⚠ Common Mistake Don't confuse mass (kg) and weight (N). Mass is the amount of matter; weight is the gravitational force on that mass. Only mass goes into F = ma.

Speeding Up vs Slowing Down

🚀 Speeding Up

  • Resultant force is in the same direction as velocity
  • Acceleration is positive
  • Object gains speed

🛑 Slowing Down

  • Resultant force is in the opposite direction to velocity
  • Acceleration is negative
  • Object loses speed

Inertia Higher

Inertia is the tendency of an object to stay at rest, or to continue moving at constant velocity. A resultant force is needed to change an object's velocity.

Inertial mass is a measure of how difficult it is to change an object's velocity:
inertial mass = force ÷ acceleration
✔ Exam Tip Inertial mass is defined as the ratio F/a. It is a measure of resistance to acceleration — the greater the mass, the harder it is to accelerate.

Keywords

Resultant force Newton's 2nd Law Acceleration Mass Inertia Inertial mass Proportional
Resultant force — The single force that has the same effect as all the individual forces acting on an object combined. Also called the net force.
Newton's Second Law — The acceleration of an object is proportional to the resultant force and inversely proportional to its mass. Summarised as F = ma.
Acceleration — The rate of change of velocity of an object, measured in m/s². A vector quantity — it has both size and direction.
Mass — The amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms (kg). Mass does not change with location.
Inertia — The tendency of an object to remain at rest or continue moving at constant velocity unless acted on by a resultant force.
Inertial mass — A measure of how difficult it is to change an object's velocity. Defined as force divided by acceleration (F/a).
Proportional — When one quantity increases, the other increases by the same factor. F and a are directly proportional when mass is constant.

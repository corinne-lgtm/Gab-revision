B1.6 Diffusion

How diffusion takes place, why it is important in living organisms, and what affects the rate of diffusion.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Diffusion is the net movement of particles from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration — down a concentration gradient.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How diffusion takes place and why it is important in living organisms
  • What affects the rate of diffusion

What is diffusion?

Diffusion is the spreading out of particles of a gas, or of any substance in solution (a solute). This results in the net movement (overall movement) of particles from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration.

It happens because of the random movement of particles (molecules or ions). Particles bump into each other and move in all directions — but overall more move from high to low concentration than the other way.

net movement = particles moving in − particles moving out Net movement is always from HIGH concentration → LOW concentration
⚠️ Study tip: Particles move randomly, but the net movement is always from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration. This is called moving down a concentration gradient.

Factors affecting the rate of diffusion

Factor Effect on rate Why?
Concentration gradient Bigger difference → faster diffusion More particles move towards the low-concentration area; fewer moving back. Steeper gradient = faster net movement.
Temperature Higher temperature → faster diffusion Particles have more energy and move around more quickly — random movement speeds up, so diffusion is faster.
Surface area Larger surface area → faster diffusion More area over which diffusion can take place at the same time — much more substance moves in a given time.

Diffusion in living organisms

🍬

Glucose & food

Digested food products (e.g. glucose, amino acids) move from the gut into the bloodstream by diffusion — from high concentration in the gut to low concentration in the blood.

💨

Oxygen & CO₂ — lungs

Oxygen moves from the air in the lungs into the red blood cells (high → low concentration). CO₂ moves from blood into the air in the lungs in the opposite direction. This is called gas exchange.

🧪

Urea — waste

Urea is made from the breakdown of amino acids in the liver. It passes from liver cells into the blood plasma and is excreted by the kidneys.

🦋

Pheromones — moths

Male moths can track down a mate from up to 3 miles away because chemical molecules produced by the female diffuse through the air down a concentration gradient.

Surface area adaptations

🔬

Folded cell membranes

Individual cells may be adapted to increase their surface area by folding up the membrane to form microvilli. This greatly increases the area over which diffusion can take place — so much more substance moves in a given time.

Example: intestinal cells have microvilli (the "brush border") to absorb digested food rapidly.

🫁

Why surface area matters

The most common adaptation to increase diffusion rate is to increase the surface area of the cell membrane. By folding up the membrane of a cell or the tissue lining an organ, the area over which diffusion can take place is greatly increased.

This is also why the lungs have alveoli (air sacs) — massive surface area for gas exchange.

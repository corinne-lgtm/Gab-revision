AQA GCSE Biology · B9 Respiration

B9.3 Anaerobic respiration

Why less energy is transferred by anaerobic than aerobic respiration, what an oxygen debt is, and anaerobic respiration in different organisms.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Anaerobic respiration provides energy without oxygen — but is less efficient. In animals it produces lactic acid; in yeast it produces ethanol + CO₂.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • Why less energy is transferred by anaerobic than aerobic respiration
  • What is meant by an oxygen debt (Higher)
  • That anaerobic respiration takes place in different organisms

The equations

Animals (including humans):

glucose → lactic acid (+ energy transferred to environment)

Plants and yeast (fermentation):

glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide (+ energy transferred to environment)
⚠️ Key difference: Animals produce lactic acid; plants and yeast produce ethanol + CO₂. Both are less efficient than aerobic respiration — the glucose is incompletely broken down.

Muscle fatigue and oxygen debt (Higher)

When muscles work very hard, lactic acid builds up. This causes muscle fatigue — fibres stop contracting efficiently, muscles ache.

The build-up of lactic acid creates an oxygen debt — the amount of oxygen needed to convert the accumulated lactic acid back to glucose (in the liver). This process also produces CO₂ and water.

lactic acid + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
💡 This is why you keep puffing and panting after you stop exercising — your heart rate and breathing rate stay high to supply the extra O₂ needed to pay off the oxygen debt. The bigger the debt (more lactic acid), the longer you pant. A fit person pays off the debt faster because their heart and lungs are more efficient.

Anaerobic respiration in other organisms

🍞

Yeast (fermentation)

Yeast produces ethanol + CO₂ during anaerobic respiration — known as fermentation. Used globally to make:

  • Bread — CO₂ makes the dough rise
  • Alcoholic drinks — beer, wine, spirits
🥛

Bacteria

Some bacteria produce lactic acid during anaerobic respiration — used to make yoghurt from milk. The lactic acid curdles the milk proteins.

🌿

Plants

Plant cells also respire anaerobically in waterlogged or low-oxygen conditions — producing ethanol + CO₂ (like yeast), not lactic acid.

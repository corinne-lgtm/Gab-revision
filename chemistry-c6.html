Electrolysis

Electrolysis

Electrolysis means breaking down an ionic compound using electricity. The substance broken down is the electrolyte.

Cathode (−)

Connected to the negative terminal. It attracts positive ions (cations).

Anode (+)

Connected to the positive terminal. It attracts negative ions (anions).

Why solid ionic compounds do not conduct

StateCan the ions move?Conducts?
Solid ionic compoundNo — ions are fixed in a giant ionic lattice.No
Molten ionic compoundYes — melting frees the ions.Yes
Aqueous ionic solutionYes — dissolved ions can move through the water.Yes
Key idea: electrolysis needs mobile ions. It is the ions — not free electrons moving through the liquid — that carry charge through the electrolyte.

Textbook example · molten zinc chloride

ZnCl₂(l) → Zn(s) + Cl₂(g)

When zinc chloride is solid, its ions cannot move. Once it is molten, Zn²⁺ moves to the cathode and Cl⁻ moves to the anode. Zinc metal forms at the cathode and chlorine gas forms at the anode.

From molten to aqueous

Dissolving an ionic compound in water also frees its ions. But water introduces extra ions, so predicting the products can become more complicated.

CuBr₂(aq) → copper at the cathode + bromine at the anode

In aqueous copper(II) bromide, Cu²⁺ moves to the cathode and Br⁻ moves to the anode. The detailed competition rules for aqueous solutions are developed in C6.2 and C6.4.

C6.2

Changes at the electrodes

Higher: turn gain/loss of electrons into balanced half equations.

Learn

Cathode = reduction

Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb

A positive ion gains electrons. Reduction Is Gain.

Anode = oxidation

2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻

A negative ion loses electrons. Oxidation Is Loss.

OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss; Reduction Is Gain — of electrons.

The effect of water in aqueous solutions

Water supplies H⁺ and OH⁻, so there is competition at the electrodes.

C6.3

The extraction of aluminium

Why aluminium needs electrolysis, why cryolite helps, and why the anodes wear away.

Learn

Why electrolysis?

Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot reduce aluminium oxide.

Why cryolite?

Aluminium oxide has a very high melting point. Dissolving it in molten cryolite lowers the operating temperature, reducing energy use.

Why replace anodes?

Oxygen forms at the positive carbon electrodes and reacts with hot carbon to form CO₂. The anodes gradually burn away.

A two-question decision rule

1 · Cathode (−)

Metal ion or H⁺?

If metal is more reactive than hydrogen → H₂.
If less reactive → metal.

2 · Anode (+)

Halide ion present?

Yes → halogen.
No → usually O₂ from OH⁻.

Brine: concentrated sodium chloride solution

cathode: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂
anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻

Na⁺ and OH⁻ remain in solution, so sodium hydroxide solution is also formed.

01 Atomic structure & bonding

8 marks

1.1 A magnesium atom has atomic number 12 and mass number 24. State the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons. [3 marks]

Marking points: 12 protons; 12 neutrons; 12 electrons.

1.2 Explain how a magnesium atom forms Mg²⁺. [2 marks]

Marking points: It loses two electrons; this gives a 2+ ion.

1.3 Explain why magnesium oxide has a high melting point. [3 marks]

Marking points: Giant ionic lattice; strong electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions; lots of energy needed to overcome them.

02 Periodic table

8 marks

2.1 Explain why Group 1 metals become more reactive down the group. [3 marks]

Marking points: Outer electron is further from nucleus; more shielding; weaker attraction, so electron is lost more easily.

2.2 Chlorine displaces bromine from potassium bromide. Write the word equation and explain why the reaction occurs. [3 marks]

Marking points: chlorine + potassium bromide → potassium chloride + bromine; chlorine is more reactive than bromine.

2.3 Predict one physical property trend down Group 0 and explain it. [2 marks]

Marking points: Boiling point increases; atoms get larger/more electrons so intermolecular attractions become stronger.

03 Quantitative chemistry

12 marks

3.1 Calculate Mr of CaCO₃. Ar: Ca=40, C=12, O=16. [2 marks]

Marking points: 40 + 12 + 48 = 100.

3.2 5.0 g of CaCO₃ is 0.050 mol. Use n = m/Mr to check this value. [2 marks]

Marking points: n=5.0/100=0.050 mol.

3.3 A solution contains 0.25 mol solute in 0.50 dm³. Calculate concentration in mol/dm³. [2 marks]

Marking points: 0.25/0.50 = 0.50 mol/dm³.

3.4 A student repeats a mass measurement: 4.82, 4.78, 4.80, 4.81 g. Calculate the mean and estimate uncertainty as half the range. [4 marks]

Marking points: Mean = 4.8025 g ≈ 4.80 g; range=0.04 g; uncertainty ≈ ±0.02 g.

3.5 Explain why mass may decrease during a reaction carried out in an open flask. [2 marks]

Marking points: A gaseous product can escape from the flask; total mass is conserved only if all products are included.

04 Chemical changes

12 marks

4.1 Put these metals in order of reactivity, highest first: copper, magnesium, zinc, silver. [2 marks]

Marking points: magnesium, zinc, copper, silver.

4.2 Write the products of hydrochloric acid + magnesium. [2 marks]

Marking points: magnesium chloride + hydrogen.

4.3 Describe how to make pure dry copper sulfate crystals from dilute sulfuric acid and excess copper oxide. [6 marks]

Marking points: Warm acid; add CuO until excess remains; filter excess solid; evaporate some water; cool to crystallise; filter/dry crystals.

4.4 Explain what oxidation and reduction mean in terms of electrons. [2 marks]

Marking points: Oxidation = loss of electrons; reduction = gain of electrons.

05 Electrolysis

10 marks

5.1 Molten lead bromide contains Pb²⁺ and Br⁻ ions. Name the product at each electrode. [2 marks]

Marking points: Cathode: lead. Anode: bromine.

5.2 Write the half equation for Pb²⁺ at the cathode. [2 marks]

Marking points: Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb.

5.3 Explain why solid lead bromide does not conduct electricity but molten lead bromide does. [3 marks]

Marking points: In solid, ions are fixed; when molten ions are free to move and carry charge.

5.4 Explain why aluminium is extracted by electrolysis rather than reduction with carbon. [3 marks]

Marking points: Aluminium is more reactive than carbon; carbon cannot reduce aluminium oxide; electrolysis is needed.

06 Energy changes

10 marks

6.1 State what happens to the temperature of the surroundings in an exothermic reaction. [1 marks]

Marking points: It increases.

6.2 On a reaction profile, what is activation energy? [2 marks]

Marking points: Energy difference from reactants to the top of the energy barrier; minimum energy for successful reaction.

6.3 Bond breaking needs 1200 kJ and bond making releases 1450 kJ. Calculate ΔE and state whether reaction is exothermic or endothermic. [3 marks]

Marking points: ΔE=1200−1450=−250 kJ; exothermic.

6.4 Describe a fair method to compare temperature changes for two reacting solutions. [4 marks]

Marking points: Measure fixed volumes/concentrations; record initial temperature; mix in insulated cup; record max/min temperature; keep variables same/repeat.

07 Synoptic practical & data

10 marks

7.1 A student measures gas volume every 30 s. Give the independent and dependent variables. [2 marks]

Marking points: Independent: time. Dependent: gas volume.

7.2 The gas volumes at 30,60,90,120 s are 104,160,198,221 cm³. Calculate mean rate from 30 to 120 s. [3 marks]

Marking points: (221−104)/(120−30)=117/90=1.30 cm³/s.

7.3 Give two ways to improve reliability of repeated measurements. [2 marks]

Marking points: Repeat readings; calculate a mean (and investigate anomalies).

7.4 Explain the difference between a random error and a systematic error. [3 marks]

Marking points: Random errors vary unpredictably and cause scatter; systematic error shifts results consistently in one direction, e.g. zero error.

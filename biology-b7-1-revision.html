Non-communicable diseases

Non-communicable diseases are diseases that cannot be passed from one individual to another. They are not infectious. They affect people as a result of their genetic makeup, lifestyle, and factors in their environment.

In 2012, 7 of the top 10 killer diseases globally were non-communicable — including coronary heart disease, stroke, COPD, lung cancer, and diabetes. Only 3 were communicable (pneumonia, HIV/AIDS, diarrhoeal diseases).

Examples: coronary heart disease, stroke, cancer, type 2 diabetes, COPD.

Risk factors for non-communicable diseases

🧬

Cannot change

  • Genes inherited from parents
  • Age
🚬

Lifestyle factors (can change)

  • Smoking
  • Lack of exercise
  • Overeating / poor diet
  • Drinking alcohol
☢️

Environmental factors

  • Ionising radiation
  • UV light from the sun
  • Second-hand tobacco smoke
  • Carcinogens in the environment
💡 We have the power to influence, change, or remove many of these risk factors — unlike genes or age.

Risk factors, correlations, and causal mechanisms

📊

Risk factor

An aspect of lifestyle or a substance in the body/environment that has been linked to an increased rate of disease. Does not prove cause.

e.g. smoking is a risk factor for lung cancer

↗️

Correlation

A relationship or pattern between two variables. If one goes up, the other tends to go up (or down). Does NOT prove that one causes the other.

e.g. rising global temperatures correlate with falling pirate numbers — but pirates don't cause climate change!

🔗

Causal mechanism

An explanation of how one factor causes another through a biological process. If proven, the link is real.

e.g. smoking → carcinogens inhaled into lungs → DNA mutations in lung cells → lung cancer. This is a proven causal mechanism.

⚠️ Key principle: A correlation suggests a link and is a useful starting point — but lots more research is needed to find a causal mechanism before we can say one thing causes another.

Human and financial costs of non-communicable diseases

👤

Human impact

  • Enormous impact on the individual and their family
  • Pain, reduced quality of life, premature death
  • Carer burden on family members
💰

Financial impact

  • If a wage-earner becomes ill, family loses income
  • Communities pay through taxes to support ill people
  • Families support informally at personal cost
  • Nations spend huge sums on treatment
  • Global economy suffers — especially when diseases affect working-age populations
💡 Non-communicable diseases affect far more people than communicable diseases — so they have the greatest effect at both human and economic levels globally.

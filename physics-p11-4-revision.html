Big Ideas
🔊

Sound = Vibration

Any vibrating object makes the air particles next to it vibrate, creating waves of compressions and rarefactions.

🌊

Longitudinal

Air particles vibrate along the direction of travel — parallel to energy transfer. This makes sound longitudinal.

🚫

No Vacuum Travel

Sound needs a medium. In a bell-jar experiment, the ringing fades as air is pumped out.

🌡️

Speed in Air

Speed of sound in air ≈ 340 m/s at 15°C. Speed increases with temperature.

Essential Formulas
v = f × λ
speed = frequency × wavelength
v in m/s · f in Hz · λ in m
Use for any wave — sound, water, string.
s = 2d / t
speed of sound from an echo
d = distance to wall · t = time delay
Multiply distance by 2 because sound travels there and back.
Remember: When measuring wavelength on a string with a single loop, the loop length L = half a wavelength, so λ = 2L. With 3 equal loops, one loop length = λ/2, so λ = ⅔ × total string length.
💡
Exam Tip In echo questions, always divide the total distance by 2 before calculating — or use s = 2d/t directly. Forgetting the factor of 2 is the most common mistake. Also check units: convert ms to s if needed.
Practical Methods

🎸 Waves on a String

Attach string to oscillator + pulley + weight. Find single loop (λ = 2L). Measure f from oscillator. Use v = f × λ. Increase f → more loops → check speed is constant.

🌊 Ripple Tank

Use ruler to create plane waves. Time wave from one end to other with stopwatch. Measure distance. Use speed = distance ÷ time. Move ruler faster → waves closer together (↑f).

🔔 Bell Jar (Vacuum)

Bell rings inside sealed jar. Pump out air progressively. Sound fades away → proves sound needs a medium. Bell still vibrates (light still seen) but no sound transmitted.

📢 Loudspeaker

Connect loudspeaker to signal generator. Loudspeaker vibrates → pushes air back and forth → creates compressions and rarefactions. Change frequency dial → change pitch of sound.
Sound vs Light

🔊 Sound Waves

  • Longitudinal (particles vibrate parallel to travel)
  • Need a medium (solid, liquid, gas)
  • Cannot travel through vacuum
  • ~340 m/s in air at 15°C
  • Speed increases with temperature

💡 Light / EM Waves

  • Transverse (particles vibrate perpendicular)
  • Do NOT need a medium
  • Travel through vacuum
  • 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum
  • Speed constant in vacuum
⚠️
Common Misconception Sound waves are NOT transverse. The particles move back and forth along the direction of travel (longitudinal), creating alternating compressions (high pressure) and rarefactions (low pressure).

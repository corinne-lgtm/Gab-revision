Section A — Multiple Choice (30 questions, 1 mark each)
Question 1 · P8 Forces in Balance
A book rests on a table. The two forces that form a Newton's Third Law pair with the table's normal reaction on the book are:
✅ Correct! Newton's Third Law: the book pushes down on the table with an equal and opposite force to the table's normal reaction on the book.
Question 2 · P8 Forces in Balance
A resultant force of 0 N means an object:
✅ Correct! Equilibrium means zero resultant force — the object can be stationary OR moving at constant velocity (Newton's 1st Law).
Question 3 · P8 Forces in Balance
A spring extends by 4 cm when a 20 N force is applied. What is its spring constant?
✅ Correct! F = ke → k = F/e = 20 ÷ 0.04 = 500 N/m. Always convert cm to m first!
Question 4 · P8 Forces in Balance
Which diagram correctly shows a vector triangle for three forces in equilibrium?
✅ Correct! For equilibrium, the vector triangle must close — arrows placed head-to-tail with the last arrow returning to the start.
Question 5 · P8 Forces in Balance
The moment of a force about a pivot is calculated using:
✅ Correct! M = F × d, where d is the perpendicular distance from the line of action of the force to the pivot.
Question 6 · P9 Motion
On a distance–time graph, what does the gradient represent?
✅ Correct! Gradient of a d–t graph = speed. A steeper gradient = faster speed.
Question 7 · P9 Motion
A car accelerates from 10 m/s to 30 m/s in 5 seconds. What is its acceleration?
✅ Correct! a = (v–u)/t = (30–10)/5 = 20/5 = 4 m/s²
Question 8 · P9 Motion
The area under a velocity–time graph represents:
✅ Correct! Area under a v–t graph = distance (or displacement if direction matters).
Question 9 · P9 Motion
Which equation links final velocity, initial velocity, acceleration and distance? (SUVAT)
✅ Correct! v² = u² + 2as links v, u, a and s (no time). This is on the AQA formula sheet.
Question 10 · P9 Motion
The gradient of a velocity–time graph gives:
✅ Correct! Gradient of a v–t graph = acceleration. A negative gradient = deceleration.
Question 11 · P10 Force and Motion
A 5 kg object accelerates at 3 m/s². What resultant force acts on it?
✅ Correct! F = ma = 5 × 3 = 15 N
Question 12 · P10 Force and Motion
A car of mass 1200 kg travelling at 20 m/s brakes to a stop in 4 s. What is the braking force?
✅ Correct! a = (0–20)/4 = –5 m/s². F = ma = 1200 × 5 = 6000 N (braking force is negative acceleration).
Question 13 · P10 Force and Motion
Momentum is defined as:
✅ Correct! p = mv. Momentum is measured in kg m/s and is a vector quantity.
Question 14 · P10 Force and Motion
The law of conservation of momentum states that in a closed system, the total momentum:
✅ Correct! In a closed system (no external forces), total momentum before = total momentum after any collision or explosion.
Question 15 · P10 Force and Motion
Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance. Which factor ONLY affects thinking distance?
✅ Correct! Thinking distance depends only on reaction time — affected by tiredness, alcohol, drugs, distractions. Braking distance depends on road/tyre/brake conditions and speed.
Question 16 · P11 Wave Properties
Wave speed is calculated using:
✅ Correct! v = fλ. Wave speed (m/s) = frequency (Hz) × wavelength (m).
Question 17 · P11 Wave Properties
In a transverse wave, particles oscillate:
✅ Correct! Transverse waves: oscillation is perpendicular to wave travel. Longitudinal waves: oscillation is parallel to wave travel.
Question 18 · P11 Wave Properties
Sound waves are an example of:
✅ Correct! Sound is a longitudinal wave — compressions and rarefactions travel parallel to the direction of energy transfer.
Question 19 · P11 Wave Properties
The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. This is the law of:
✅ Correct! Law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection, both measured from the normal to the surface.
Question 20 · P11 Wave Properties
A wave has a frequency of 200 Hz and a wavelength of 1.5 m. What is its speed?
✅ Correct! v = fλ = 200 × 1.5 = 300 m/s
Question 21 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum. What is this speed?
✅ Correct! Speed of light in vacuum = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (300,000,000 m/s).
Question 22 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
Which part of the EM spectrum has the shortest wavelength?
✅ Correct! Order (longest to shortest wavelength): Radio → Microwave → IR → Visible → UV → X-ray → Gamma. Gamma has the shortest wavelength and highest frequency.
Question 23 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
Which type of EM radiation is used for TV remote controls?
✅ Correct! TV remotes use infrared radiation (IR). Microwaves are used for cooking and satellite communication.
Question 24 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
Which type of radiation causes fluorescence in certain materials?
✅ Correct! UV radiation causes fluorescence — certain materials absorb UV and emit visible light. Also used in security marking.
Question 25 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
Which EM waves are used to communicate with satellites and why?
✅ Correct! Microwaves pass through the Earth's atmosphere and ionosphere, making them ideal for satellite communication.
Question 26 · P13 Electromagnetism
What does Fleming's Left-Hand Rule give the direction of?
✅ Correct! Fleming's Left-Hand Rule (motor effect): thuMb = Motion, First finger = Field, seCond finger = Current.
Question 27 · P13 Electromagnetism
The transformer equation is Vp/Vs = np/ns. A transformer has 200 primary turns and 1000 secondary turns. If the primary voltage is 230 V, what is the secondary voltage?
✅ Correct! Vs = Vp × (ns/np) = 230 × (1000/200) = 230 × 5 = 1150 V. This is a step-up transformer.
Question 28 · P13 Electromagnetism
Electromagnetic induction occurs when:
✅ Correct! EMF is induced whenever there is relative movement between a conductor and a magnetic field, or whenever the magnetic field through a coil changes.
Question 29 · P13 Electromagnetism
How can the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field be increased?
✅ Correct! Force = BIl. To increase force: increase B (stronger magnet), increase I (current), or increase l (length of conductor in field).
Question 30 · P13 Electromagnetism
Why is electricity transmitted at high voltage across the National Grid?
✅ Correct! P = I²R. Higher voltage → lower current for same power → less energy lost as heat (P = I²R). Step-up transformers increase voltage; step-down transformers reduce it before homes.
Section B — True or False (30 questions, 1 mark each)
Question 31 · P8 Forces in Balance
A free-body diagram shows only the forces acting ON an object, not forces the object exerts on other things.
✅ Correct! A free-body diagram shows only the forces acting ON the object. Forces the object exerts on others are NOT shown.
Question 32 · P8 Forces in Balance
If a spring is stretched beyond its elastic limit, it will return to its original length when the force is removed.
✅ Correct! Beyond the elastic limit, the spring is permanently deformed and will NOT return to its original shape.
Question 33 · P8 Forces in Balance
For a system of parallel forces to be in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments must equal the sum of anticlockwise moments.
✅ Correct! This is the principle of moments. For equilibrium: sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments about any point.
Question 34 · P8 Forces in Balance
The centre of mass of a symmetrical object always lies at its geometric centre.
✅ Correct! For uniform, symmetrical objects the centre of mass is at the geometric centre (e.g. centre of a uniform ruler).
Question 35 · P9 Motion
Speed is a scalar quantity, but velocity is a vector quantity.
✅ Correct! Speed has magnitude only (scalar). Velocity has both magnitude and direction (vector). A car travelling at 30 m/s north has a different velocity from one travelling 30 m/s south.
Question 36 · P9 Motion
A horizontal line on a velocity–time graph means the object is stationary.
✅ Correct! A horizontal line on a v–t graph means constant velocity (NOT stationary unless the line is on the x-axis at v = 0).
Question 37 · P9 Motion
Instantaneous speed can be found from a distance–time graph by drawing a tangent to the curve at that point.
✅ Correct! The gradient of the tangent to a d–t graph at a point gives the instantaneous speed at that moment.
Question 38 · P10 Force and Motion
A larger mass requires a smaller force to produce the same acceleration.
✅ Correct! F = ma, so for the same acceleration, a larger mass requires a LARGER force. Inertia is the tendency to resist changes in motion.
Question 39 · P10 Force and Motion
Crumple zones in cars increase the time of impact, which reduces the force experienced by passengers.
✅ Correct! F = Δp/Δt. Crumple zones increase Δt (time of impact), so the force F is reduced for the same change in momentum. This is why they save lives.
Question 40 · P10 Force and Motion
At terminal velocity, the driving force equals the resistive forces, so the resultant force is zero.
✅ Correct! At terminal velocity, weight = drag (for a falling object). Resultant force = 0, so acceleration = 0, meaning constant velocity.
Question 41 · P10 Force and Motion
Kinetic energy depends on the square of the velocity, so doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy.
✅ Correct! KE = ½mv². If v doubles, v² quadruples, so KE quadruples. This is why speed is so dangerous — doubling speed quadruples braking distance too.
Question 42 · P11 Wave Properties
Diffraction is most noticeable when the wavelength of a wave is much larger than the gap it passes through.
✅ Correct! Diffraction is greatest when the wavelength is approximately equal to (or larger than) the gap size. If the gap is much larger than the wavelength, little diffraction occurs.
Question 43 · P11 Wave Properties
When a wave moves from a less dense medium to a more dense medium, it speeds up.
✅ Correct! When a wave moves from a less dense to a more dense medium, it SLOWS DOWN and bends towards the normal (refraction).
Question 44 · P11 Wave Properties
The frequency of a wave changes when it crosses from one medium to another.
✅ Correct! When a wave refracts, the SPEED and WAVELENGTH change, but the FREQUENCY stays the same.
Question 45 · P11 Wave Properties
Ultrasound is used in foetal scanning because it is a form of ionising radiation that is safe at low doses.
✅ Correct! Ultrasound is used in foetal scanning because it is NON-ionising (it does not cause cell damage like X-rays). It is sound above 20,000 Hz.
Question 46 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
All electromagnetic waves are transverse waves that can travel through a vacuum.
✅ Correct! All EM waves are transverse and can travel through a vacuum at 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Unlike sound, they do not need a medium.
Question 47 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
Gamma rays can be used to sterilise medical equipment because they kill bacteria.
✅ Correct! Gamma rays are highly ionising and can kill living cells, including bacteria. They are used to sterilise surgical instruments.
Question 48 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
Radio waves have a higher frequency than X-rays.
✅ Correct! Radio waves have the LOWEST frequency (and longest wavelength) in the EM spectrum. X-rays have much higher frequency and shorter wavelength.
Question 49 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
Infrared radiation is detected by the skin as heat.
✅ Correct! Our skin detects infrared radiation as warmth. This is why IR cameras can detect body heat and why heaters emit IR.
Question 50 · P12 Electromagnetic Waves
Microwaves used in cooking heat food by causing water molecules to vibrate and heat up.
✅ Correct! Microwave ovens emit microwaves that are absorbed by water molecules, causing them to vibrate faster — this generates heat and cooks food.
Question 51 · P13 Electromagnetism
A solenoid carrying current produces a magnetic field similar in shape to that of a bar magnet.
✅ Correct! A current-carrying solenoid produces a uniform field inside and a dipole field outside — identical to a bar magnet, with north and south poles at each end.
Question 52 · P13 Electromagnetism
In a step-up transformer, the number of secondary turns is less than the number of primary turns.
✅ Correct! A step-up transformer INCREASES voltage, so the secondary coil has MORE turns than the primary (ns > np). Step-DOWN transformers have fewer secondary turns.
Question 53 · P13 Electromagnetism
The induced EMF can be increased by moving the magnet faster, using a stronger magnet, or using more turns of wire in the coil.
✅ Correct! All three methods increase the rate of change of magnetic flux, which increases the induced EMF (Faraday's law).
Question 54 · P13 Electromagnetism
An ideal transformer is 100% efficient, so the power input equals the power output.
✅ Correct! For an ideal (100% efficient) transformer: Vp × Ip = Vs × Is (power in = power out). Real transformers have small losses due to resistance in coils.
Question 55 · P13 Electromagnetism
Reversing the direction of the current in a motor causes it to spin in the opposite direction.
✅ Correct! Reversing the current (or reversing the magnetic field) reverses the force on the conductor, causing the motor to spin the other way.
Question 56 · P8 Forces in Balance
The unit of force is the Newton (N), which is equivalent to kg m/s².
✅ Correct! 1 N = 1 kg m/s², derived from F = ma. This is a key unit to know for AQA Physics.
Question 57 · P10 Force and Motion
Work done equals force multiplied by the distance moved in the direction of the force.
✅ Correct! W = Fd (work done in joules = force in newtons × distance in metres in the direction of force).
Question 58 · P11 Wave Properties
The required practical for waves involves measuring the speed of waves on water using a ripple tank.
✅ Correct! The AQA required practical uses a ripple tank to observe wave behaviour and measure wave speed using v = fλ. You measure frequency and wavelength from the ripple pattern.
Question 59 · P9 Motion
The required practical for motion involves using light gates or a motion sensor to measure acceleration.
✅ Correct! The AQA required practical on acceleration uses a trolley on a ramp with light gates or ticker tape to measure how force and mass affect acceleration (verifying F = ma).
Question 60 · P8 Forces in Balance
A parachutist reaches terminal velocity when their weight equals the air resistance acting on them.
✅ Correct! At terminal velocity, resultant force = 0, meaning weight (down) = air resistance (up). The parachutist continues at constant speed.

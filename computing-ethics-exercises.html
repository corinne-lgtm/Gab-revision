Question 2 [6 marks]

Describe three of the seven principles of the UK GDPR. For each principle, state its name and explain what it requires of an organisation.

Principle 1
Principle 2
Principle 3

Mark Scheme — Q2 [6 marks]

Award [1] for correct name + [1] for correct explanation, per principle. Max 6 marks (any 3 from below):

  • Lawful, fair & transparent — data must be collected legally, fairly, and subjects must be informed.
  • Purpose limitation — data may only be used for the specific purpose stated when collected.
  • Data minimisation — only collect the minimum data necessary for the purpose.
  • Accuracy — data must be kept accurate and up to date; inaccurate data must be corrected or deleted.
  • Storage limitation — data should not be kept longer than necessary for the stated purpose.
  • Integrity & confidentiality — data must be kept secure against loss, destruction or unauthorised access.
  • Accountability — organisations must be able to demonstrate compliance with all other principles.

Mark Scheme — Q5 [6 marks]

Award [1] for correct stage + [1] for developed impact. Max 6 (any 3 from below):

  • Manufacture: High energy and water use in chip fabrication contributes to carbon emissions and water scarcity

Question 6 [6 marks]

Compare the impact of the digital divide in a high-income country (such as the UK) with its impact in a low-income country. Discuss two differences in the causes or consequences.

Difference 1
Difference 2

Mark Scheme — Q6 [6 marks]

Award up to [3] per difference: [1] valid point for high-income, [1] valid contrasting point for low-income, [1] for development/consequence.

  • Infrastructure: In high-income countries, gaps tend to be rural (final-mile broadband); in low-income countries, large areas may have no fixed broadband infrastructure at all, though mobile leapfrogging is common.
  • Cost: In high-income countries, cost is a barrier for low-income households but devices exist; in low-income countries, a smartphone may cost several months' wages.
  • Consequences: In UK, divide affects access to employment/banking/education; in low-income countries, divide can mean exclusion from e-government services, medical information, and global economic participation.
  • Literacy: In high-income countries, digital literacy is taught in schools; in low-income countries, fewer trained teachers and devices mean fewer opportunities to develop skills.
Question 7 [8 marks]

Discuss the ethical implications of using artificial intelligence in criminal justice (for example, to predict the likelihood that someone will reoffend). In your answer, consider both potential benefits and risks.

This is an extended response question. Aim for a balanced argument of ~150–200 words covering multiple perspectives.

Mark Scheme — Q7 [8 marks]

Use the following levels:

  • [7–8] Balanced, well-structured answer covering multiple specific ethical issues on both sides; uses correct terminology (algorithmic bias, explainability, accountability, fairness); reaches a justified conclusion.
  • [5–6] Discusses both benefits and risks with some development; at least one side explored in depth; mostly accurate terminology.
  • [3–4] Some relevant points on at least one side; limited development; may be one-sided.
  • [1–2] One or two basic points with little development.

Indicative content:

  • Benefits: Faster processing of data; may reduce human bias in some cases; consistency; cost-effective for large volumes.
  • Risks: Algorithmic bias (training data reflects historical inequalities — may unfairly flag certain demographic groups); black box problem (decisions hard to challenge); privacy implications of data used; reduced human accountability; risk of self-fulfilling prophecy; erosion of presumption of innocence.
  • Ethical frameworks: Fairness / non-maleficence / accountability / right to appeal.
Question 8 [10 marks]

A tech company is planning to launch a new fitness app that collects users' heart rate, location and sleep data continuously. The data will be stored on cloud servers and may be shared with insurance companies.

Part (a) — Identify two potential privacy concerns raised by this app. [2 marks]
Part (b) — Explain how the Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR would apply to this scenario. Refer to at least two GDPR principles. [4 marks]
Part (c) — Discuss whether the sharing of users' data with insurance companies is ethical. Give a balanced argument. [4 marks]

Mark Scheme — Q8 [10 marks]

(a) [2 marks] — [1] per valid concern:

  • Sensitive health/biometric data could be breached, leading to identity theft or discrimination
  • Continuous location tracking could enable stalking or physical surveillance
  • Users may not understand or consent to their data being shared with third parties
  • Data used by insurers could lead to higher premiums or denial of cover

(b) [4 marks] — [1] per GDPR principle identified, [1] per developed application:

  • Purpose limitation: Sharing with insurers may go beyond the fitness purpose stated when data was collected — this would breach purpose limitation
  • Data minimisation: Continuous collection of all metrics may be excessive if a subset would serve the stated purpose
  • Lawful & transparent: Users must be clearly informed of data sharing; sharing with insurers requires explicit consent given it's sensitive health data
  • Integrity & confidentiality: Cloud storage must be secured against breaches

(c) [4 marks] — Levels:

  • [3–4] Balanced argument with developed points on both sides and reasoned conclusion
  • [1–2] One-sided or undeveloped points

Indicative points: FOR — users can opt in; insurers can price more fairly; may incentivise healthy behaviour. AGAINST — power imbalance; users may not understand implications; vulnerable groups harmed; mission creep beyond fitness tracking.

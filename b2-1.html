Biology · B2.1

Cell division

Follow a cell as it grows, copies its DNA and divides. Discover how two daughter cells receive the same genetic information.

01 · The information inside cells

DNA → chromosomes → genes

The nucleus contains chromosomes made of DNA. Each chromosome carries many genes, which are sections of DNA.

DNA

The molecule that carries genetic information.

Chromosome

A structure made of DNA that carries many genes.

Gene

A section of DNA that contains instructions for a particular characteristic or protein.

Human body cells: 46 chromosomes, normally arranged in 23 pairs. The chromosome number is maintained during mitosis.
02 · Interactive cell cycle

One cell. Three overall stages.

Select a stage to follow the genetic material. This is a simplified teaching model, not a microscope recording.

Exam boundary: AQA requires the three overall stages of the cell cycle. You do not need to memorise the individual phases of mitosis for Combined Science: Trilogy.
03 · Follow the chromosomes

Copy, separate, divide

Stage 1

Growth & replication

The cell grows and increases structures such as mitochondria and ribosomes. DNA replicates to form two copies of each chromosome.

Stage 2

Mitosis

One set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the cell. The nucleus divides.

Stage 3

Two daughter cells

The cytoplasm and cell membrane divide, forming two genetically identical cells.

Remember: DNA replication happens before mitosis. Mitosis is nuclear division; the final division of cytoplasm and membrane produces the two cells.
04 · Why does it matter?

Growth, repair and replacement

Growth

More cells allow a multicellular organism to grow and develop.

Repair

New cells replace damaged cells and help tissues repair.

Replacement

Many tissues continually replace worn-out cells, such as cells in the skin and digestive lining.

Cell cycles vary in length between cell types and conditions. Some specialised cells divide rarely or not at all. Mitosis also produces genetically identical cells in asexual reproduction.

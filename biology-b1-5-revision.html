AQA GCSE Biology · B1 Cell structure and transport

B1.5 Specialisation in plant cells

How root hair cells, photosynthetic cells, xylem and phloem are each adapted to carry out their specific functions.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Plants have very specialised cells — each adapted precisely for absorption, photosynthesis or transport.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How plant cells may be specialised to carry out a particular function
  • How the structure of root hair, photosynthetic, xylem and phloem cells relates to their function

Root hair cells

🌱
Root hair cell
Function: absorb water and mineral ions from the soil
📐 Long root hair extension
Greatly increases the surface area available for water to move into the cell — more contact with soil water.
💧 Large permanent vacuole
Speeds up the movement of water by osmosis from the soil across the root hair cell membrane.
⚡ Many mitochondria
Transfer the energy needed for active transport of mineral ions into the root hair cells against the concentration gradient.
⚠️ Root hair cells are always close to the xylem tissue, which carries water and mineral ions upward through the plant.

Photosynthetic cells

🍃
Photosynthetic cell
Function: carry out photosynthesis to make food for the plant
🌿 Chloroplasts
Contain chlorophyll that traps light energy needed for photosynthesis.
☀️ Position in layers
Usually positioned in continuous layers in the leaves and outer stem to absorb as much light as possible.
💧 Large permanent vacuole
Helps keep the cell rigid (by osmosis). Many of these rigid cells together help support the stem and keep the leaf spread out to capture more light.
⚠️ Cells in the trunk of a tree cannot photosynthesise — they are too deep inside to receive light.

Xylem cells

💧
Xylem cells
Function: transport water and mineral ions from roots to leaves and shoots
☠️ Cells die → hollow tubes
Xylem cells are alive when first formed, but lignin builds up in spirals in the cell wall. The cells die and form long hollow tubes — water moves easily from end to end.
🔩 Lignin spirals
Make the xylem cells very strong. They withstand the pressure of water moving up the plant and help support the plant stem.
⚠️ Xylem transport is one-way — upward from roots to leaves. Water moves up through the hollow tubes.

Phloem cells

🍬
Phloem cells
Function: transport dissolved food (sugars) made by photosynthesis around the plant
🧱 Sieve plates
Cell walls between phloem cells break down to form sieve plates — allow dissolved food to move freely up and down the tubes.
🤝 Companion cells
Phloem cells lose most of their internal structures but are kept alive by companion cells alongside them.
⚡ Mitochondria (companion cells)
The companion cells' mitochondria transfer the energy needed to move dissolved food up and down the plant.
⚠️ Unlike xylem, phloem transport is two-way — food can move up or down to where it is needed. Phloem cells do NOT become lignified.

Quick comparison

🌱 Root hair

Absorbs water + minerals. Long hair = big surface area. Many mitochondria for active transport.

🍃 Photosynthetic

Makes food. Chloroplasts trap light. Positioned in outer layers for max light.

💧 Xylem

Transports water upward. Hollow dead tubes with lignin spirals. Very strong.

🍬 Phloem

Transports sugars up/down. Sieve plates. Supported by companion cells.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • State one adaptation for each cell type + explain how it helps [2 marks each]
  • Suggest why a cell in the trunk cannot photosynthesise [2 marks]
  • Describe features to decide the function of an unknown plant cell [6 marks]

Top tip: Always link structure → function. "X has Y because this allows it to Z."

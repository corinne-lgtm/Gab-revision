Interpretation

1.
Interpretation A
“By the end of Elizabeth’s reign, the Queen’s authority was weaker because court rivalry had become harder to control. Essex’s rebellion exposed the dangers created by disappointed favourites and faction.”
Original revision interpretation written for this site.
How convincing is Interpretation A about the strength of Elizabeth’s authority at the end of her reign?
Explain your answer using contextual knowledge and what the interpretation says.

8 marks
How to write this answer well
  • Identify exactly what the interpretation claims about authority and court rivalry.
  • Test it with Essex’s rise, his loss of favour and monopoly, the 1601 rebellion, Cecil’s position and the rebellion’s limited support.
  • A convincing evaluation can agree with part of the interpretation while showing that the rebellion’s quick failure also demonstrated royal authority.
Revision marking points — not an official AQA mark scheme

Relevant points include patronage and faction; Essex’s financial and political problems; his failed rebellion in 1601; limited support; government control; Robert Cecil; and the distinction between tension at court and the wider strength of Elizabeth’s government.

Importance

2. Explain what was important about voyages of exploration and trade in Elizabethan England.

8 marks
How to write this answer well
  • Choose consequences for wealth, power and overseas ambitions.
  • Use named evidence: Drake’s circumnavigation, privateering, Hawkins, Raleigh/Roanoke, Muscovy/Eastland/East India companies.
  • Explain significance rather than simply retelling voyages. Include limits where useful: not every venture succeeded.
Revision marking points — not an official AQA mark scheme

Relevant points include profits and treasure, navigation and naval experience, challenge to Spain, expanding trade links, early trading companies, Raleigh’s colonisation attempts, Roanoke’s failure, and the beginnings of English involvement in the transatlantic slave trade through Hawkins.

Write an account

3. Write an account of how relations between England and Spain became more hostile by 1588.

8 marks
How to write this answer well
  • Keep a clear sequence and show escalation.
  • Use religion, the Netherlands, English support for Dutch rebels, Drake/privateering, 1585 intervention and the 1587 Cádiz raid.
  • Do not end with a list. Explain why each development made open conflict more likely.
Revision marking points — not an official AQA mark scheme

Relevant points include Protestant–Catholic tension; Dutch revolt and Spanish repression; English money and volunteers; sea beggars; attacks on Spanish shipping; William of Orange’s assassination; Leicester’s expedition in 1585; Drake’s raids; and preparations for the Armada.

Historic Environment · 2027

4. “The main weakness of the Spanish Armada was its plan to link with Parma’s army.” How far does a study of the Spanish Armada support this statement?
Explain your answer. Refer to the Spanish Armada and your contextual knowledge.

16 marks
How to write this answer well
  • Use the Armada as a historic environment: location and movement matter.
  • Explain why communication and coordination with Parma around Calais were difficult, then compare this with English attacks, naval technology/tactics and the return route.
  • Judge whether the link-up problem was the main weakness or one part of a wider failure.
Revision marking points — not an official AQA mark scheme

Relevant points include the plan to escort Parma’s invasion force; communication and anchorage problems; Calais and fire ships; Gravelines; English gunnery/manoeuvrability; the Armada being forced into the North Sea; supplies, damage and weather on the return.

Health and safety in hospitality and catering

Learn → Quiz → Exam Practice. Your quiz score is saved on this device.

Lesson 1 of 5

Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974

Employers must take reasonably practicable steps to protect workers and others affected by the business. Employees must take reasonable care and cooperate with safety procedures.

Lesson 2 of 5

COSHH

The Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations require hazards from harmful substances such as cleaning chemicals to be assessed and controlled: label products, follow instructions, store securely and use protection if needed.

Lesson 3 of 5

RIDDOR

Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations set requirements for reporting certain serious work-related incidents to the relevant enforcing authority. Not every minor accident is reportable.

Lesson 4 of 5

Risk assessments and accident forms

Identify hazards, who could be harmed, the likelihood and severity of harm, and appropriate control measures. Record workplace accidents promptly with facts, injuries, witnesses and actions taken.

Lesson 5 of 5

HACCP and food safety responsibilities

Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points identifies food-safety hazards, critical control points, critical limits, monitoring, corrective actions, verification and records. A temperature check may be a control, but controls must match the food process.

6 marks

Explain why a risk assessment and an accident report are both important in a busy kitchen.

Risk assessments identify hazards and controls before harm; accident reports record incident details, support investigation and corrective action. Distinguish preventative from reactive documentation.

Use these indicative points for self-assessment, not an automatic mark.

4 marks

Describe how HACCP helps a restaurant manage a chilled food delivery.

Identify delivery as control stage; check condition and temperature against the business’s safe limits; record results; reject or isolate an unsafe delivery and follow corrective procedure.

Use these indicative points for self-assessment, not an automatic mark.

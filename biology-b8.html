B8.1

Photosynthesis

Learn

The process

Photosynthesis takes place in chloroplasts. Chlorophyll absorbs light energy, which is used to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose. Oxygen is released as a by-product.

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Endothermic reaction

Photosynthesis is endothermic because energy is transferred from the environment into the reaction by light.

Leaf adaptations

Leaves are broad and thin, contain chloroplasts, have stomata and air spaces for gas exchange, and veins that supply water and transport sugars.

Light is absorbed by chlorophyll in chloroplasts. Carbon dioxide enters the leaf and oxygen leaves.

B8.2

The rate of photosynthesis

Learn

Limiting factors

Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature and chlorophyll amount can limit photosynthesis. The limiting factor is the one in shortest supply relative to what the plant needs.

Temperature

Photosynthesis is enzyme-controlled. Rate rises with temperature up to an optimum, then falls as enzymes denature.

Required practical

Investigate the effect of light intensity on pondweed. Change lamp distance, measure oxygen production per unit time, control temperature and carbon dioxide availability, repeat and calculate a mean.

Inverse-square law: when the distance from a point light source doubles, light intensity is approximately quartered.
B8.3

How plants use glucose

Learn

Respiration and storage

Plants use glucose in respiration and convert some to insoluble starch for storage.

Making new materials

Glucose is used to make cellulose for cell walls and lipids for energy storage. With nitrate ions from the soil, plants make amino acids and then proteins.

Starch test

A leaf is boiled in water, heated in ethanol in a water bath to remove chlorophyll, rinsed, then covered with iodine. Blue-black shows starch is present.

Plants use glucose in several different ways rather than storing all of it unchanged.

B8.4 · Higher tier

Making the most of photosynthesis

Learn

Greenhouse control

Growers can manipulate temperature, light intensity, carbon dioxide and mineral availability to reduce limiting factors and increase crop growth.

Hydroponics

Plants can be grown without soil in water containing the required mineral ions. This gives close control of water and nutrients.

Greenhouse economics

The biologically fastest photosynthesis rate is not always the most profitable. Extra heating, lighting and carbon dioxide cost money, so growers balance yield against operating cost.

Balance yield and cost

Increase conditions to boost yield, but watch the cost. The best profit is not necessarily at maximum inputs.

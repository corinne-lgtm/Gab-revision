B7.5 Alcohol and other carcinogens

How alcohol affects liver and brain function, unborn babies, and how ionising radiation acts as a carcinogen.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Alcohol is a carcinogen that damages the liver and brain. Ionising radiation — including UV, radon, and X-rays — causes DNA mutations that can lead to cancer.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • Alcohol affects liver and brain function
  • Alcohol can affect unborn babies
  • Alcohol is a carcinogen
  • Many other agents act as carcinogens

Alcohol — how it affects the body

Alcohol (ethanol) is a commonly used social drug. It is poisonous but the liver can usually remove it before permanent damage occurs. It is also very addictive.

When absorbed into the blood from the gut:

  • Passes easily into body tissues including the brain
  • Affects the nervous system — slows thought processes, reflexes, and reactions
  • Small amounts: relaxation, reduced inhibitions
  • Large amounts: lack of self-control and judgement
  • Very large doses: unconsciousness, coma, death

Long-term damage to liver and brain

🫁

Liver damage

  • Cirrhosis — active liver cells replaced by scar tissue that cannot carry out vital functions
  • Alcohol is a carcinogen → heavy drinkers at greatly increased risk of liver cancer (spreads rapidly, hard to treat)
  • Long-term damage usually develops over years — but very heavy short-term drinking can cause the same symptoms
🧠

Brain damage

  • Long-term heavy use causes damage to the brain
  • In some alcoholics, the brain becomes so soft and pulpy that normal brain structures are lost
  • The brain can no longer function properly — this can cause death

Alcohol and pregnancy

🤰

If a pregnant woman drinks alcohol, it passes across the placenta to the developing baby. The developing liver cannot cope with alcohol, so development of the brain and body can be badly affected.

Risks include:

  • Miscarriage, stillbirths, premature births, low birthweight
  • Facial deformities, problems with teeth, jaw, hearing
  • Kidney, liver, and heart problems
  • Learning and developmental problems

This is known as Fetal Alcohol Syndrome (FAS). Doctors are not sure how much alcohol is safe during pregnancy — the best advice is not to drink at all.

Ionising radiation — a well-known carcinogen

Ionising radiation penetrates cells and damages chromosomes — causing mutations in the DNA. The more you are exposed, the more likely mutations will occur and cancer will develop.

Particularly dangerous when taken directly into the body — e.g. breathing radioactive materials enables radiation to penetrate cells directly.

Sources of ionising radiation:

☀️

UV light (sun)

Increases risk of skin cancers such as melanoma. Protection: sunscreen and sensible clothing.

🌍

Radon gas

Radioactive material found naturally in soil, water, and air — especially in granite-rich areas (Cornwall, the Pennines). Can accumulate in homes, increasing lung cancer risk (especially in smokers).

🏥

Medical X-rays

Medical and dental X-rays are a source of ionising radiation — kept to necessary minimum doses.

☢️

Nuclear accidents

Accidents such as Chernobyl, Ukraine (1986) spread ionising radiation over wide areas.

⚠️ Study tip: Both ionising radiation AND alcohol AND tar in tobacco smoke act as carcinogens — they all cause mutations in DNA that can lead to cancer.

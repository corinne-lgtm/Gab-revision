B4.1 The blood

How substances are transported to and from cells, the components of blood, and the function of each.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Blood is a liquid tissue — plasma carries red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets around the body to supply cells and defend against infection.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How substances are transported to and from cells
  • That blood is made up of many different components
  • The function of each main component

Composition of blood

Plasma 55%
Red blood cells ~44%
<1%

Blood is a unique liquid tissue based on plasma. Average person has 4.7–5 litres of blood.

The four components

🟡

Plasma

A yellow liquid that carries everything else. It transports:

  • Red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets
  • CO₂ — from cells to the lungs
  • Urea — from liver to kidneys (for urine)
  • Digested food molecules — from small intestine to cells
  • Hormones, proteins, other dissolved substances
🔴

Red blood cells

Most numerous — ~5 million per mm³. Carry oxygen from lungs to cells. Three key adaptations:

  • Biconcave disc shape (concave on both sides) → increased surface area to volume ratio for faster diffusion of O₂
  • Packed with haemoglobin — red pigment that binds to oxygen (forming oxyhaemoglobin)
  • No nucleus → more space for haemoglobin
⚗️ Haemoglobin + O₂ ⇌ oxyhaemoglobin (reversible — releases O₂ at cells)
⚪

White blood cells

Much bigger than red blood cells. Much fewer. Have a nucleus. Part of the body's defence system against microorganisms:

  • Lymphocytes → produce antibodies against microorganisms
  • Some → produce antitoxins against poisons made by microorganisms
  • Phagocytes → engulf and digest invading bacteria and viruses
🟣

Platelets

Small fragments of cells — not whole cells. No nucleus. Role in blood clotting:

  • Triggered at the site of a wound
  • A series of enzyme-controlled reactions converts fibrinogen → fibrin
  • Fibrin forms a network of protein fibres — traps red blood cells and platelets to form a jelly-like clot
  • Clot dries → scab — protects new skin and stops bacteria entering

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • State three functions of the blood [3 marks]
  • Explain why it is not accurate to describe the blood as a red liquid [2 marks]
  • What actually makes the blood red? [1 mark]
  • Identify three important functions of blood plasma [3 marks]
  • Discuss the main ways blood helps you avoid infection — include description of the parts of the blood involved [6 marks]

Key answer: Blood is not simply red — only ~44% is red blood cells. Plasma (55%) is yellow. The red colour comes from haemoglobin in red blood cells.

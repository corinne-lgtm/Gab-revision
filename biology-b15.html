B15.1

The importance of communities

Learn

Community

A community is all the populations of different species living and interacting in the same habitat.

Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a community interacting with the non-living parts of its environment.

Interdependence

Species depend on each other for food, shelter, pollination, seed dispersal and other resources.

Community network

See how one change can affect several linked populations.

B15.2

Organisms in their environment

Learn

Abiotic factors

Light, temperature, moisture, soil pH and minerals, wind, oxygen and carbon dioxide can affect distribution.

Biotic factors

Food availability, pathogens, predators and competition also affect communities.

Distribution

An organism is most abundant where interacting environmental conditions allow it to survive and reproduce successfully.

B15.4

Competition in animals

Learn

Food

Predators compete for prey; herbivores may compete for the same plants.

Territory

Territories can secure food, nesting space and access to mates.

Mates

Displays, colour, weapons and fighting behaviour can increase mating success.

B15.5

Competition in plants

Learn

Resources

Plants compete for light, water, mineral ions and space.

Coping with competition

Early growth, climbing, different root depths and larger leaves can improve access to resources.

Seed dispersal

Spreading seeds away from the parent reduces competition between generations.

Crowding experiment

Increase crowding and watch average plant growth fall as resources become limiting.

B15.6

Adapt and survive

Learn

Structural

Physical features such as shape, colour, spines, fur or large ears.

Behavioural

Actions such as migration, nocturnal activity, basking or burrowing.

Functional

Internal processes such as concentrated urine or antifreeze production.

B15.7

Adaptation in animals

Learn

Cold climates

Compact bodies, small extremities, fur and blubber reduce heat loss.

Camouflage

Colouration can reduce detection by predators or prey.

Dry climates

Animals reduce overheating and conserve water through structural, behavioural and functional adaptations.

Surface area : volume

Move from a compact body to a long-thin body. The displayed surface-area-to-volume ratio changes.

B15.8

Adaptations in plants

Learn

Reducing water loss

Small or rolled leaves, thick waxy cuticles, hairs and fewer/sunken stomata reduce evaporation.

Collecting water

Wide shallow roots can absorb brief rain quickly; deep roots can reach groundwater.

Storing water

Succulent stems or leaves store water for dry periods.

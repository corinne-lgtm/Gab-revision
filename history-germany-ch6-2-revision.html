AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

6.2 The Night of the Long Knives

How Hitler used murder, intimidation and propaganda to strengthen his dictatorship in 1934.

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index Revision Questions ✏️ Quiz rapide
The Night of the Long Knives was when Hitler destroyed threats inside the Nazi movement and won the support of the army.
🎯

Objectives

  • Explain why Hitler wanted to remove Röhm and the SA.
  • Describe what happened during the Night of the Long Knives.
  • Assess why it helped Hitler establish a dictatorship.

1. Why was the SA a problem for Hitler?

⚠️

The SA had become too powerful

The SA, led by Ernst Röhm, had around 3 million members. They had protected Nazi meetings and intimidated opponents, but by 1934 they were becoming a threat to Hitler himself.

🪖

Röhm wanted to control the army

Röhm wanted the SA to merge with, or take over, the German army. This worried army leaders because the army had only about 100,000 men and did not want to be replaced by street fighters.

🤝

Hitler needed the army

Hitler wanted the army’s support because President Hindenburg was old and ill. If Hindenburg died, Hitler would need the army to accept him as Germany’s supreme leader.

💼

Conservatives feared disorder

Business leaders, landowners and conservative politicians disliked the SA’s violence and revolutionary talk. Hitler needed these powerful groups to feel safe under Nazi rule.

2. What happened?

30 Jun 1934
Attack begins

SS men arrested Röhm and other SA leaders. Many were taken from a hotel near Munich.

30 Jun–2 Jul
Executions

Röhm, SA leaders and other political enemies were shot. Around 400 people were killed.

Key victim
Ernst Röhm

Röhm was killed because Hitler saw him as a rival and the army saw him as dangerous.

Other victims
Political enemies

Some people killed were not SA members, including former Chancellor Kurt von Schleicher.

Afterwards
Justification

Hitler claimed he had saved Germany from a dangerous SA plot. The murders were presented as legal and necessary.

3. Why did this help Hitler?

✅

The army supported him

The army was pleased that Röhm and the SA had been weakened. This made it much easier for Hitler to gain the army’s loyalty after Hindenburg died.

🛡️

The SS became more important

The SS, led by Heinrich Himmler, gained power after helping carry out the purge. It became responsible for Hitler’s security and later helped run the Nazi police state.

☠️

Opponents were terrified

Hitler had shown he was willing to murder anyone who threatened him. This made opposition even more dangerous.

👑

Hitler looked in control

Many Germans accepted Hitler’s explanation that he had stopped a plot. Some saw him as a strong leader who had restored order.

4. Impact on Germany

  • Röhm and leading SA figures were removed.
  • The SA was no longer a major independent threat.
  • The SS and Gestapo became more powerful.
  • The army agreed to support Hitler.
  • Germany moved further towards a police state.
  • Hitler proved that violence and murder were part of Nazi government.

Exam focus

Describe two consequences means you need two clear results of the event, with detail.

Good consequences to use:

  • Army support: Hitler removed Röhm, so army leaders trusted him more.
  • SS power: the SS became the key security force and helped create a police state.
  • Fear: opponents knew Hitler could use murder without being stopped.

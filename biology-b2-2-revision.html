AQA GCSE Biology · B2 Cell division

B2.2 Growth and differentiation

How cell differentiation varies in animals and plants, and the production and use of plant clones.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
As cells divide and develop, they differentiate — switching genes on and off to become specialised for a specific function.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How cell differentiation varies in animals and plants
  • The production and use of plant clones

What is differentiation?

Differentiation is the process by which a cell becomes specialised for its job. As cells divide, grow and develop, they begin to differentiate — certain genes are switched on and others are switched off. As a result, different types of specialised cells have different sub-cellular structures to carry out specific functions.

💡 At conception, a human being starts as just one cell. By adulthood, the body contains around 37.2 trillion cells — almost all produced by mitosis.

Differentiation in animals vs plants

🐾

Animal cells

  • In early development, cells are unspecialised stem cells that can become any cell type
  • Most cells become specialised very early in life — by the time a baby is born, most cells are already specialised
  • Most specialised cells can only divide to make more of the same type
  • Some cells (e.g. red blood cells, skin cells) cannot divide at all — adult stem cells replace them
  • Nerve cells do not divide once differentiated — so are not usually replaced if damaged
  • In mature animals, cell division is mainly for repair and replacement
🌱

Plant cells

  • Most plant cells retain the ability to differentiate throughout their lives
  • Undifferentiated cells are found at meristems — active growing regions at tips of shoots and roots
  • Mitosis takes place almost continuously in meristems
  • Cells elongate and grow, then finally differentiate
  • Differentiation in plants is not permanent — a plant cell can be moved and redifferentiate into a completely different type
  • Plants keep growing at these 'growing points' throughout their lives
⚠️ Key difference: Animal cells lose the ability to differentiate early; plant cells retain it throughout life. This is why cloning plants is much easier than cloning animals.

Stem cells

🔬

Embryonic stem cells

In the early development of animal and plant embryos, cells are unspecialised. Each one (a stem cell) can become any type of cell that is needed.

🩺

Adult stem cells

Adult stem cells replace dead or damaged cells in animals. They are already partially differentiated — they can only form certain cell types. For example, stem cells in bone marrow produce blood cells.

Cloning plants

🌿

How plant cloning works

Producing identical offspring is called cloning. Huge numbers of identical plant clones can be produced from a tiny piece of leaf tissue:

  • In the right conditions, a plant cell becomes unspecialised and undergoes mitosis many times
  • Each undifferentiated cell then differentiates to form tissues such as xylem, phloem, photosynthetic cells, and root hair cells
  • A tiny new plant forms that is genetically identical to the original parent plant
✅

Why plant cloning is easy

Plant cells can redifferentiate — they can revert to an unspecialised state and then differentiate again into new cell types. This is what makes cloning possible.

❌

Why animal cloning is hard

Most animal cells differentiate permanently early in embryo development — the cells cannot change back. So artificial animal clones can only be made by cloning embryos, although adult cells can sometimes be used to make an embryo.

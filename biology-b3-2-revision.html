B3.2 The human digestive system

The position of the main organs of the human digestive system and their functions.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
The digestive system breaks down large insoluble food molecules into small soluble molecules that can be absorbed into the blood.
🎯

Learning objective

  • The position of the main organs of the human digestive system
  • The function of each organ
📏 The digestive system is 6–9 m long — 9 million times longer than an average human cell!

Main organs of the digestive system

Organ Function
🦷 Mouth
(teeth, tongue, salivary glands)		 Teeth physically break food into smaller pieces (mechanical digestion). Salivary glands produce saliva containing amylase to begin breaking down starch. Tongue shapes food into a ball (bolus) for swallowing.
🟣 Gullet (oesophagus) A muscular tube that squeezes food from the mouth to the stomach by peristalsis (wave-like muscle contractions).
🫃 Stomach Churns food and mixes it with gastric juice (containing protease enzymes and hydrochloric acid). Begins protein digestion. Acid kills bacteria.
🟡 Liver Produces bile — which emulsifies fats (breaks them into droplets), making digestion of lipids more efficient. Carries out many other functions in the body.
🟢 Gall bladder Stores bile produced by the liver, then releases it into the duodenum when needed.
🩷 Pancreas Produces digestive enzymes (amylase, protease, lipase) and releases them into the duodenum. Also makes insulin (a hormone).
🔵 Duodenum First part of the small intestine — where most chemical digestion takes place. Receives bile from gall bladder and enzymes from pancreas.
🩶 Small intestine Digestion is completed here. The large surface area of villi allows soluble food molecules to be absorbed into the blood. Good blood supply and short diffusion distances aid absorption.
🟤 Large intestine Absorbs water from undigested food back into the blood. The remaining material forms faeces.
Rectum & anus Faeces are stored in the rectum then pass out of the body through the anus.
⚠️ Study tip: Learn the difference between the liver (large, lobed, brown) and the pancreas (thinner, leaf-like, behind the stomach). The liver makes bile; the pancreas makes enzymes.

The small intestine — key adaptations

📐

Villi

Finger-like projections that give the small intestine a very large surface area — greatly increasing the rate of absorption of digested food molecules into the blood.

🩸

Good blood supply

A rich network of capillaries runs through each villus — maintaining a steep concentration gradient so absorption continues at maximum rate.

📏

Short diffusion distance

The wall of each villus is only one cell thick — providing a very short diffusion path from the gut to the blood.

⚡

Active transport

When glucose concentration in the gut is lower than in the blood, active transport is used to move glucose into the blood against the concentration gradient.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Match each organ to its correct function [4 marks]
  • Explain the difference between organs and organ systems, giving two examples [4 marks]
  • Using the human digestive system as an example, explain how the organs in an organ system rely on each other to function properly [6 marks]

Answers to match question: Liver → producing bile; Stomach → breaking down large molecules; Small intestine → breaking down molecules AND absorption; Large intestine → absorbing water.

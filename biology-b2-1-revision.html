B2.1 Cell division

The role of cells, the cell cycle, and how cells divide by mitosis.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
During the cell cycle, genetic material is doubled, then the nucleus divides by mitosis to produce two identical cells.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • The role of cells in organisms
  • The importance of the cell cycle
  • How cells divide by mitosis

Key definitions

🧬

Chromosome

A thread-like structure found in the nucleus, made of DNA. Human body cells have 46 chromosomes arranged in 23 pairs. One chromosome in each pair comes from the mother, one from the father.

🔬

Gene

A small section of DNA on a chromosome. A gene is a packet of information that controls a characteristic (or part of one). A chromosome may carry hundreds or thousands of genes.

🧪

DNA

Deoxyribonucleic acid — the unique molecule that makes up your chromosomes. It carries the genetic instructions for making and running every cell in your body.

💡 Remember: DNA → makes up chromosomes → chromosomes carry genes → genes control characteristics.

Why cells divide

📈

Growth

New cells are needed for an organism to grow. Cell division produces additional cells for growth and development in multicellular organisms.

🔄

Replacement

Cells wear out and are replaced. About 300 million body cells die every minute — mitosis constantly produces new cells to replace them.

🩹

Repair

New cells replace worn out or damaged tissue. The new cells must have the same genetic information as the originals so they can do the same job.

The cell cycle — 3 stages

1
Growth & DNA replication

The longest stage. Cells grow bigger, increase their mass, and carry out normal activities. Most importantly, they replicate their DNA to form two copies of each chromosome. They also increase the number of sub-cellular structures (mitochondria, ribosomes, chloroplasts).

2
Mitosis (nucleus divides)

One set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the dividing cell. The nucleus divides into two identical nuclei. Each nucleus contains the same number and type of chromosomes as the original.

3
Cytoplasm & membranes divide

The cytoplasm and cell membranes divide to form two identical daughter cells. Each has a nucleus with identical chromosomes to the original parent cell.

⚠️ Study tip: Cells produced when the nucleus divides by mitosis are genetically identical to each other and to the parent cell.

Mitosis — key facts

✅

Genetically identical

Mitosis produces two cells with exactly the same genetic information as each other and as the parent cell. No genetic variation.

📋

Same chromosome number

It is important that chromosome number stays the same. If daughter cells had different numbers of chromosomes, they could not carry out the same functions as the parent.

🌱

Asexual reproduction

In asexual reproduction, offspring cells are produced by mitosis from the parent. They contain exactly the same genes — little or no genetic variation.

🐟

Sex cells (gametes)

Sex cells (sperm and eggs) only have 23 chromosomes — one from each pair. This is because they are produced by a different type of division (meiosis — covered later).

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Define: chromosome, gene, DNA [1 mark each]
  • Describe what happens during the three stages of the cell cycle [6 marks]
  • Explain why cell division by mitosis is so important in the body [2 marks]
  • Suggest why it is important for the chromosome number to stay the same when cells divide [3 marks]

Key phrase: "Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells from one parent cell."

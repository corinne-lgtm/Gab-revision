3.3 Did Weimar recover after 1923?

How Gustav Stresemann helped Germany recover — and why the recovery was real but fragile.

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index Revision Questions
From 1924–1929, Weimar looked more stable and prosperous — but the recovery depended heavily on American loans.

Gustav Stresemann

👤 Who?

Chancellor in 1923, then Foreign Minister from 1924 until his death in 1929.

🎯 Aim

Restore Germany’s economy and improve Germany’s international reputation.

⚖️ Approach

Practical and moderate: stabilise money, negotiate, rebuild trust.

Four problems → Stresemann’s solutions

1 · Hyperinflation

Solution: introduced the Rentenmark in 1923.
Success: money became stable again.

2 · Ruhr crisis

Solution: ended passive resistance and agreed the Dawes Plan.
Success: French and Belgian troops left the Ruhr.

3 · International status

Solution: Locarno Pact + League of Nations.
Success: Germany was treated more like a major power again.

4 · Weak industry

Solution: US loans helped factories, jobs and public services.
Success: economic growth improved.

How far did Germany recover?

Evidence of recovery

  • Currency stable again.
  • Industry grew with US loans.
  • More jobs and public building.
  • Germany regained international respect.
  • Fewer attempts to overthrow the government.

Limits of recovery

  • Savers never got their money back.
  • Some hated paying reparations at all.
  • Extremist parties still existed.
  • Farmers and middle classes still struggled.
  • Recovery relied on American loans.

Exam summary

Weimar recovered significantly after 1923 because Stresemann stabilised the currency, improved foreign relations and encouraged economic growth. However, the recovery was fragile because it depended on American loans and many Germans still disliked Weimar.

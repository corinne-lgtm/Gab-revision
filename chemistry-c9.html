C9 Crude oil and fuels

From crude oil to useful fuels: learn the alkane pattern, separate fractions, explain combustion and crack large hydrocarbons into more useful molecules.

C9.1

Hydrocarbons

Crude oil is a finite resource formed over millions of years from ancient biomass, mainly plankton. It is a mixture of many compounds. Most are hydrocarbons: molecules containing carbon and hydrogen only.

Crude oil = mixture, not a pure substance

Hydrocarbon = carbon + hydrogen only

Most crude-oil hydrocarbons are alkanes

Alkanes: CnH2n+2

C9.2

Fractional distillation of oil

Crude oil is separated into fractions containing hydrocarbons with similar numbers of carbon atoms. Different molecule sizes have different boiling points, so they evaporate and condense at different levels in a fractionating column.

Small molecules: lower boiling point, lower viscosity

Small molecules: more volatile and more flammable

Large molecules: higher boiling point, more viscous, less flammable

C9.3

Burning hydrocarbon fuels

Complete combustion in plentiful oxygen produces carbon dioxide and water. With insufficient oxygen, incomplete combustion can produce carbon monoxide, which is toxic.

Complete: hydrocarbon + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

CO₂ turns limewater cloudy

Water turns blue cobalt chloride paper pink (or white anhydrous copper sulfate blue)

hydrocarbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Complete combustion: plentiful oxygen. Carbon and hydrogen are completely oxidised.

Incomplete combustion: insufficient oxygen; carbon monoxide can form.

C9.4

Cracking hydrocarbons

Cracking breaks large, less useful hydrocarbons into smaller, more useful molecules. Products include alkanes and alkenes. Alkenes are unsaturated and contain a C=C double bond.

Catalytic cracking: vaporise then pass over a hot catalyst

Steam cracking: mix vapour with steam and heat to a very high temperature

Alkene test: orange bromine water → colourless

large hydrocarbon → smaller alkane + alkene(s)
C—C=CC

Alkene: unsaturated because it contains C=C.

Catalytic cracking

Vaporise the hydrocarbon, then pass the vapour over a hot catalyst.

Steam cracking

Mix hydrocarbon vapour with steam and heat to a very high temperature.

Why crack?

High demand for small fuel molecules; alkenes are useful feedstock for polymers and other chemicals.

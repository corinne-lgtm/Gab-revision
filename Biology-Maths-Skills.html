MS1

Arithmetic and numerical computation

Decimal & standard form

Standard form writes very large or very small numbers as A × 10ⁿ, where A is at least 1 but less than 10.

5,000,000 = 5 × 10⁶

Positive powers make numbers larger than 1; negative powers make numbers smaller than 1.

Ratios, fractions & percentages

Simplify ratios by dividing both parts by the same common factor. A percentage is a fraction out of 100.

% change = change ÷ original × 100

For an estimate, round to simple values first, then check whether the calculator answer is sensible.

MS2

Handling data

Significant figures & averages

Count significant figures from the first non-zero digit. Mean = total ÷ number of values. Median = middle value after ordering. Mode = most frequent value.

Tables, charts & histograms

Bar charts are for categories/discrete groups and have gaps. Histograms show continuous grouped data; bar area represents frequency and the vertical axis is frequency density.

frequency density = frequency ÷ class width

Sampling & probability

Samples can estimate a larger population. Quadrat estimates use mean number per unit area × total area. Capture–recapture estimates animal populations.

estimated population = first sample × second sample ÷ marked recaptured

Correlation & order of magnitude

Positive, negative or no correlation describes a relationship; correlation alone does not prove causation. Order of magnitude is a rough comparison using powers of ten.

MS4

Data and graphs

Plotting data

Put the independent variable on the x-axis and dependent variable on the y-axis. Use even scales, labels and units, then draw an appropriate line of best fit.

Gradient

The gradient tells you how quickly y changes as x changes.

gradient = change in y ÷ change in x

For a straight line, y = mx + c, where m is gradient and c is the y-intercept.

MS5

Geometry and trigonometry

Area, surface area & volume

Rectangle area = length × width. Triangle area = ½ × base × height. Cuboid volume = length × width × height.

Cylinder surface area = 2πr² + 2πrL.

Surface area : volume

Surface area to volume ratio is important for exchange surfaces. As a cube gets larger, its SA:V ratio gets smaller.

SA:V = surface area ÷ volume

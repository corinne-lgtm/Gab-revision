B5.5 Bacterial diseases

Examples of bacterial diseases: Salmonella food poisoning, gonorrhoea, and Agrobacterium tumefaciens in plants.

Gonorrhoea and antibiotic resistance

⚠️

Gonorrhoea was originally easily cured using penicillin. However, bacteria evolve — many strains have now evolved resistance to penicillin and other antibiotics. It is increasingly difficult to treat.

This is a serious problem because there are ~106 million new cases of gonorrhoea per year globally. If strains become untreatable, the consequences include widespread infertility, pelvic pain, and ectopic pregnancies.

To prevent spread: all sexual partners of an infected person must be treated — even if they have no symptoms — to stop the disease spreading back into the community.

Preventing Salmonella food poisoning

🌡️

Cook thoroughly

Heat kills Salmonella — cook chicken and eggs until no pink remains and juices run clear.

🥩

Separate raw meat

Keep raw chicken away from food eaten uncooked. Never wash raw chicken — it sprays bacteria around the kitchen.

🧼

Wash hands

Wash hands and surfaces thoroughly after handling raw chicken or eggs.

💉

Vaccination (UK poultry)

Poultry in the UK are vaccinated against Salmonella to control the spread of the disease through the food chain.

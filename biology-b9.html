B9.1

Aerobic respiration

Learn

The reaction

Aerobic respiration is an enzyme-controlled process in which glucose reacts with oxygen. Carbon dioxide and water are produced and energy is transferred.

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Mitochondria

Most aerobic respiration reactions take place in mitochondria. Cells with high energy demand, such as muscle cells, often contain many mitochondria.

Why respiration matters

Energy transferred by respiration is used for movement, active transport, building larger molecules and maintaining body temperature.

Aerobic respiration uses glucose and oxygen and transfers energy for cellular processes.

B9.2

The response to exercise

Learn

Higher demand

During exercise, muscles contract more and need more energy. Their rate of respiration rises, so they need more glucose and oxygen and produce more carbon dioxide.

Heart and lungs respond

Heart rate, breathing rate and breathing depth increase. More oxygenated blood reaches muscles and carbon dioxide is removed faster.

Glycogen store

Muscles store glycogen, which can be converted rapidly to glucose during exercise to provide fuel for respiration.

B9.3 · Higher tier

Anaerobic respiration

Learn

In human muscles

When oxygen supply cannot meet demand, muscles respire anaerobically. Glucose is incompletely broken down to lactic acid, so less energy is transferred than in aerobic respiration.

glucose → lactic acid

Oxygen debt

Lactic acid must be removed after exercise. Extra oxygen is needed during recovery, so breathing and heart rate remain elevated for a time.

In yeast

Yeast respires anaerobically by fermentation, producing ethanol and carbon dioxide.

glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide

When oxygen supply is insufficient, human muscle cells use anaerobic respiration and produce lactic acid.

B9.4 · Higher tier

Metabolism and the liver

Learn

Metabolism

Metabolism is the sum of all the chemical reactions in a cell or organism. It includes respiration, photosynthesis and reactions that build or break down molecules.

Examples

Metabolic reactions include making glycogen, starch and cellulose from glucose, making lipids, making amino acids and proteins, and breaking down excess amino acids to form urea.

The liver and lactic acid

Blood carries lactic acid from muscles to the liver. It is converted back to glucose. The glucose can be used in respiration or converted to glycogen for storage.

The liver processes lactic acid carried in the blood after exercise.

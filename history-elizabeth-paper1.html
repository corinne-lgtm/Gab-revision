Interpretation

1.
Interpretation A
“Elizabeth’s refusal to marry was a serious political problem. It kept the succession uncertain and repeatedly encouraged Parliament and councillors to press the Queen for a decision.”
Original revision interpretation written for this site.
How convincing is Interpretation A about Elizabeth’s decision not to marry?
Explain your answer using contextual knowledge and what the interpretation says.

8 marks
How to write this answer well
  • Start with a judgement: convincing, partly convincing, or limited.
  • Test specific claims in the interpretation against knowledge: succession, Parliament in 1563/1566, Dudley, Philip II and Anjou.
  • Explain where the interpretation is strong and where it leaves something out. Finish with a supported overall judgement.
Revision marking points — not an official AQA mark scheme

Relevant revision points include the succession problem; pressure from Parliament; political advantages and dangers of marriage; Elizabeth’s wish to retain independence; the risks attached to particular suitors; and the fact that remaining unmarried also avoided a husband gaining influence.

Importance

2. Explain what was important about the growth of poverty in Elizabethan England.

8 marks
How to write this answer well
  • Choose two or three consequences, not a list of causes.
  • Use precise knowledge such as population growth, inflation, bad harvests, unemployment and pressure on local communities.
  • For each point, make the consequence explicit: “This was important because… which meant…”
Revision marking points — not an official AQA mark scheme

Relevant points include pressure on food and wages, migration and vagrancy concerns, changing attitudes to the poor, local experiments, Houses of Correction, and the development of government measures culminating in the 1601 Poor Law.

Write an account

3. Write an account of how the Catholic threat to Elizabeth developed between 1569 and 1587.

8 marks
How to write this answer well
  • Write a chronological chain rather than separate mini-descriptions.
  • Use turning points: Northern Rebellion, excommunication, Ridolfi, missionaries/Jesuits, Throckmorton, Babington and Mary’s execution.
  • Link each stage: explain how one development increased danger or changed the government response.
Revision marking points — not an official AQA mark scheme

Relevant points include the Northern Rebellion (1569), papal excommunication (1570), Ridolfi Plot (1571), missionary and Jesuit activity, harsher laws in the 1580s, Throckmorton (1583), Babington (1586), Walsingham’s intelligence network, Mary’s trial and execution (1587).

Historic Environment · 2027

4. “English naval tactics were the main reason the Spanish Armada failed.” How far does a study of the Spanish Armada support this statement?
Explain your answer. Refer to the Spanish Armada and your contextual knowledge.

16 marks
How to write this answer well
  • Build a sustained argument, not a story of the Armada.
  • Use the historic environment directly: Channel fighting, Calais, fire ships, Gravelines, the route north and the return around Scotland and Ireland.
  • Compare English tactics with Spanish planning/coordination, ship manoeuvrability and gunnery, logistics and weather. Reach a clear judgement about the main reason.
Revision marking points — not an official AQA mark scheme

Relevant points include English long-range gunnery and manoeuvrability; fire ships at Calais; Gravelines; difficulty linking with Parma’s army; Spanish formation and planning problems; shortages and damage; the forced route north; and storms/logistical losses on the return. A strong answer distinguishes failure of the invasion plan from later losses.

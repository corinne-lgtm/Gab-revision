B8.4 Making the most of photosynthesis

1
a) State the main differences between a garden greenhouse and a hydroponics growing system. [3] b) State the main benefits of artificially manipulating the environment in which food plants are grown. [3]

2
In each of these situations, identify the one factor that is most likely to be limiting photosynthesis. In each case explain why the rate of photosynthesis is limited. a) A wheat field first thing in the morning [3] b) The same field later in the day [3] c) Plants growing on a woodland floor in winter [3] d) Plants growing on a woodland floor in summer [3] e) Explain why it is impossible to be certain which factor is involved in each of these cases. [3]
[15 marks]
3
The cost of running a large greenhouse is particularly affected by the levels of light and temperature. If plants are provided with plenty of carbon dioxide the grower can get two crops a year at 20°C and three crops a year at 30°C. Explain why many growers use 20°C rather than 30°C in their greenhouses. In your explanation suggest why many growers are also investing in insulation systems that can be added and removed electronically.
[6 marks]

[2 marks]
5
Explain why, for a hydroponics-grown plant in a large commercial greenhouse, "limiting factors are a thing of the past".
[3 marks]
6
Describe the disadvantages and costs of growing crops in a large commercial greenhouse, and explain why growers must carefully balance the costs against the profits.
[4 marks]
7
The first recorded greenhouse was built in about AD 30 for the Roman Emperor Tiberius Caesar, who wanted to eat cucumbers out of season. Explain the principle behind why keeping plants in a glassed-in building helps them grow faster.
[3 marks]
8
Early in the morning, both light levels and temperature are limiting the rate of photosynthesis. As the day progresses, light intensity and temperature rise. Explain what happens to the limiting factor as these two variables increase.
[3 marks]
9

[2 marks]
10

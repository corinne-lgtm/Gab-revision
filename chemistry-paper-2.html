Chemistry Paper 2

A full mixed practice paper built from C8 onward, with rates, equilibrium, organic chemistry, analysis, atmosphere, resources and working scientifically.

01 Rates of reaction

10 marks

1.1 Explain using collision theory why increasing temperature increases reaction rate. [3 marks]

Marking points: Particles move faster; collide more often; larger fraction have energy ≥ activation energy.

1.2 A reaction produces 72 cm³ gas in 48 s. Calculate mean rate. [2 marks]

Marking points: 72/48 = 1.5 cm³/s.

1.3 Explain why powdered calcium carbonate reacts faster than the same mass of large chips. [3 marks]

Marking points: Greater surface area to volume ratio; more particles exposed; more frequent successful collisions.

1.4 What does a catalyst do to activation energy? [2 marks]

Marking points: Provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.

02 Reversible reactions & equilibrium

10 marks

2.1 Define dynamic equilibrium. [3 marks]

Marking points: In a closed system; forward and reverse reactions continue; at equal rates, so concentrations remain constant.

2.2 For an exothermic forward reaction, predict effect of increasing temperature on equilibrium yield of products. [2 marks]

Marking points: Yield of products decreases; equilibrium shifts in endothermic/reverse direction.

2.3 Predict effect of increasing pressure when product side has fewer gas molecules. [2 marks]

Marking points: Equilibrium shifts to product side.

2.4 Explain what happens after adding more reactant at equilibrium. [3 marks]

Marking points: System opposes change; equilibrium shifts to use added reactant; more products form until new equilibrium.

03 Organic chemistry

10 marks

3.1 Give the general formula of alkanes. [1 marks]

Marking points: CnH2n+2.

3.2 Describe the trends in boiling point, viscosity and flammability as hydrocarbon chain length increases. [3 marks]

Marking points: Boiling point ↑; viscosity ↑; flammability ↓.

3.3 Balance complete combustion of propane: C₃H₈ + __O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O. [1 marks]

Marking points: 5O₂.

3.4 Why is cracking useful? [2 marks]

Marking points: Converts less useful long-chain hydrocarbons into more useful short-chain alkanes and alkenes.

3.5 Describe the bromine-water test for an alkene. [3 marks]

Marking points: Add/shake with bromine water; orange/brown becomes colourless; shows C=C unsaturation.

04 Chemical analysis

12 marks

4.1 A pure substance melts sharply at 80°C. A sample melts from 72–77°C. What does this suggest? [2 marks]

Marking points: Sample is impure/a mixture; mixtures melt over a range and impurities lower melting point.

4.2 In paper chromatography, why must the start line be drawn in pencil and placed above the solvent? [3 marks]

Marking points: Pencil graphite does not dissolve/run; sample spots must not dissolve directly into solvent.

4.3 A spot moves 7.5 cm and solvent front 9.7 cm. Calculate Rf. [2 marks]

Marking points: Rf=7.5/9.7≈0.77.

4.4 Why must Rf values be compared using the same solvent and temperature? [2 marks]

Marking points: Rf depends on conditions; changing solvent/temperature changes relative attractions/movement.

4.5 Give test and positive result for hydrogen, oxygen and carbon dioxide. [3 marks]

Marking points: H₂: lighted splint, pop. O₂: glowing splint relights. CO₂: limewater turns milky/cloudy.

05 Atmosphere

10 marks

5.1 State the approximate percentages of nitrogen and oxygen in today's atmosphere. [2 marks]

Marking points: About 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen.

5.2 Give two processes that reduced carbon dioxide in the early atmosphere. [2 marks]

Marking points: Photosynthesis; carbon dioxide dissolving in oceans/forming carbonate rocks (or fossil fuel formation).

5.3 Explain one environmental problem caused by increasing greenhouse gases. [3 marks]

Marking points: Enhanced greenhouse effect/global warming leading to climate change, e.g. sea-level/weather/ecosystem impacts.

5.4 Why are carbon monoxide and sulfur dioxide pollutants? [3 marks]

Marking points: CO is toxic because it reduces blood oxygen transport; SO₂ can cause acid rain/respiratory problems.

06 Resources & sustainability

10 marks

6.1 Define a finite resource. [1 marks]

Marking points: A resource that is used faster than it can be replaced / will run out.

6.2 Give two stages that should be considered in a life-cycle assessment. [2 marks]

Marking points: Any two: raw materials, manufacture, use, transport, disposal/end-of-life.

6.3 Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of recycling metals. [3 marks]

Marking points: Advantage: conserves ores/energy, reduces waste. Disadvantage: collection/separation/processing uses energy/costs.

6.4 Explain the difference between potable water and pure water. [2 marks]

Marking points: Potable is safe to drink with acceptable dissolved substances; pure water contains only H₂O.

6.5 A water sample is filtered then distilled. What does each step remove? [2 marks]

Marking points: Filtration removes insoluble solids; distillation separates water from dissolved substances.

07 Working scientifically

8 marks

7.1 Define independent, dependent and control variables. [3 marks]

Marking points: Independent is changed; dependent is measured; controls are kept constant/monitored.

7.2 What is an anomalous result? [2 marks]

Marking points: A result clearly inconsistent with the pattern/other repeat readings.

7.3 Repeated values are 44,45,48,49 s. Estimate uncertainty in the mean as half the range. [2 marks]

Marking points: Range=5 s; uncertainty ≈ ±2.5 s.

7.4 Why does precision not necessarily mean accuracy? [1 marks]

Marking points: Closely grouped readings can all be offset from the true value.

AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

9.2 'Winning' people over

How the Nazis used propaganda and censorship to control what Germans heard, read and saw.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions ✅ Answers
The Nazis used propaganda to persuade — and censorship to silence. Together, they controlled what Germans thought.

Key definitions

📢 Propaganda

From the word 'propagate' — meaning to spread information and ideas. The Nazis used propaganda to persuade large numbers of Germans to think and believe what Hitler wanted. Key messages were repeated constantly using short, powerful images and slogans.

Key Nazi propaganda messages:

  • Blaming Jews for Germany's problems
  • Criticising the Treaty of Versailles
  • Making Germany strong and powerful again

✂️ Censorship

The government tightly controlled (or censored) what German people heard, read or saw. Anything viewed as harmful to the Nazis or Hitler was banned — including books, films, news articles and even jokes.

Joseph Goebbels (1897–1945)

Minister of Enlightenment and Propaganda

  • Couldn't fight in WWI because of a disability in his foot. After the war, he falsely claimed he had been wounded during fighting.
  • Joined the Nazi Party in 1924. Not fully supportive of Hitler at first, but grew to admire him totally.
  • In charge of all Nazi propaganda. Brilliant in the role and a gifted public speaker.
  • Understood that propaganda worked best if people were repeatedly given basic ideas with short messages and powerful images.

How Goebbels controlled what people heard, read and saw

📰

Newspapers

Only stories showing the Nazis doing good things were allowed to be printed. There were also many negative stories about Jews. Newspapers that printed stories Goebbels hadn't approved were closed down.

📻

Radio

The Nazis controlled all radio stations. Cheap radios were produced that could only tune in to Nazi-controlled stations. Loudspeakers were placed in streets, factories and cafés. More Germans owned radios in the 1930s than Americans.

🎬

Films

All film plots were shown to Goebbels before filming. All films had to show the Nazis in a good way and their 'enemies' in a bad way.

🎭

Books, theatre and music

Writers were forced to write books, plays and songs that praised Hitler and the Nazis. Works that didn't were banned.

🏟️

Mass rallies

Spectacular parades (called mass rallies) were held, often in celebration of Hitler. Special arenas could hold half a million people. Choirs, bands, speeches, fireworks and air shows were performed to show how impressive and well organised the Nazis were.

Why was it so effective?

📢 Propaganda worked because…

  • Messages were simple and repeated constantly
  • It used powerful images and emotional appeal
  • It was everywhere — radio, posters, films, schools
  • People had no access to alternative views

✂️ Censorship worked because…

  • Without other sources of information, people only heard the Nazi view
  • Fear of arrest stopped people sharing alternative opinions
  • Books, films, jokes — anything critical was banned
  • Even private conversation could be dangerous

Sources and Interpretation

SOURCE A — Goebbels speaking about propaganda at a Nazi rally in 1937:
"The essence of propaganda consists in winning people over to an idea so sincerely, so vitally, that in the end they succumb to it utterly and can never escape from it."

What this shows: Goebbels believed the goal was total psychological control — not just informing people, but making them unable to think any other way.

INTERPRETATION B — From The World at War by Richard Holmes (2007); Lieutenant von Kleist-Schmenzin, a surviving conspirator of the 1944 July Bomb Plot, interviewed:
"I think a person who has never lived in a dictatorship can't understand the power of propaganda. If you just hear always the same, if you read in every newspaper the same and you have very few possibilities for other information then you become very impressed by the things which you are told. And it's very difficult to have to make up your own mind, to be critical."

The writer's point: Propaganda is most powerful when people have no access to alternative information. Without other viewpoints, it becomes almost impossible to think critically.

Fear vs propaganda — two methods of control

😨 Fear (Gestapo / SS)

Arrest, imprisonment and violence kept many Germans silent — even if they disagreed privately. Many kept their heads down to stay safe.

📢 Propaganda (Goebbels)

Many Germans genuinely came to support Hitler. Propaganda convinced them he was making Germany great. Some were truly 'won over' — not just afraid.

Key question: Did most Germans support Hitler out of genuine belief (propaganda) or out of fear? Historians still debate this.

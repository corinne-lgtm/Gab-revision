B2.4 Stem cell dilemmas

Arguments for and against embryonic stem cells

✅

Arguments FOR

  • Huge potential to treat many serious diseases — paralysis, diabetes, blindness, heart damage
  • Many embryos come from fertility treatment and would otherwise be destroyed
  • Embryos used are tiny balls of cells with no brain or nervous system
  • Could allow growth of whole organs for transplant
  • Could benefit millions of people worldwide
❌

Arguments AGAINST

  • An embryo is a potential human life — using it as a source of cells destroys it
  • Embryo cannot give consent — seen as a violation of human rights
  • Religious beliefs: some cannot accept any interference with human reproduction
  • Research is slow, expensive and difficult to control — money could be better spent
  • Embryonic stem cells may cause cancer — shown in some mice and early human trials

Practical risks of stem cell treatments

⚠️

Cancer risk

Embryonic stem cells divide and grow rapidly — which is partly why they are so useful, but also why there is concern they might cause cancer if used to treat people. This has been a problem in mice and early human autoimmune trials.

🦠

Virus transfer (adult stem cells)

Adult stem cells may be infected with viruses and could transfer infections to patients. If stem cells from an adult are used to treat an unrelated person, they may also trigger an immune response — the patient may need immunosuppressant drugs.

🧪

Control and understanding

The signals that control cell differentiation are still not completely understood. It is proving difficult to persuade embryonic stem cells to differentiate into the specific type of cells needed for treatment.

Therapeutic cloning

🔬

What is therapeutic cloning?

Therapeutic cloning involves using cells from an adult patient to produce a cloned early embryo of themselves. This provides a source of perfectly matched embryonic stem cells.

These stem cells could then be used for medical treatments — the new organs/tissues would not be rejected by the body because they were made from the body's own cells and have the same genes.

The process: Adult cell → nucleus extracted → inserted into enucleated egg → embryo develops → stem cells harvested → differentiated into needed tissues → transplanted back into patient.

💡 Therapeutic cloning is different from reproductive cloning — it is used to produce cells/organs for medicine, NOT to create a whole human being.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Give three examples where stem cells could provide new treatments [3 marks]
  • Summarise the main arguments for and against the use of embryonic stem cells [4 marks]
  • Explain how scientists are hoping to overcome the ethical objections to using embryonic stem cells [5 marks]

For the 5-mark question — mention: therapeutic cloning (own cells = no rejection), umbilical cord blood, amniotic fluid, adult stem cells from bone marrow, and ongoing research to control differentiation better.

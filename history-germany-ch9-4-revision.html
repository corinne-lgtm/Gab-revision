9.4 Resistance and opposition to Hitler

Why some Germans resisted, what they did, and why resistance was difficult.

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index 📚 Germany index ✏️ Quiz rapide

Big idea

⚠️

Opposition was dangerous

The Nazis used propaganda, the Gestapo, the SS and concentration camps to frighten people. Openly opposing Hitler could lead to prison, torture or execution.

🗣️

Resistance took different forms

Some opposition was small and private, such as jokes or grumbling. Some was open, such as refusing the Hitler salute, writing leaflets, helping Jews or plotting to kill Hitler.

🧩

Resistance was not united

Different groups opposed Hitler for different reasons: religious beliefs, moral objections, youth rebellion, political beliefs, or anger at the war.

Why did many Germans not oppose Hitler?

Some supported the Nazis

  • Some young people enjoyed Hitler Youth activities.
  • Some workers liked jobs and Strength Through Joy schemes.
  • Some businesses liked rearmament and profits from government contracts.

Fear stopped opposition

  • People feared the Gestapo and informers.
  • Concentration camps were used against opponents.
  • Criticising Hitler could lead to arrest or punishment.

Opposition was hard to organise

  • Political parties and trade unions were banned.
  • The press, radio and culture were controlled.
  • Resistance groups were often isolated from one another.

Passive resistance

Passive resistance means showing opposition without directly attacking the regime. It was often small-scale but still risky.

Examples

  • Refusing to give the “Heil Hitler” salute.
  • Refusing to donate money to Nazi organisations.
  • Listening to banned radio broadcasts.
  • Making anti-Nazi jokes or complaining privately.

Why it mattered

Passive resistance showed that not everyone accepted Nazi rule. However, it was usually too small and private to seriously threaten the regime.

Open opposition and resistance groups

Swing Youth

Some young people rejected Nazi control by listening to jazz, wearing different clothes and refusing Nazi youth culture.

Edelweiss Pirates

They resisted Hitler Youth control. Some spread anti-Nazi graffiti, helped deserters and helped escaped prisoners or Allied airmen.

White Rose group

A student group led by Hans and Sophie Scholl. They wrote and distributed anti-Nazi leaflets. They were arrested in 1943 and executed.

Church opposition

Some religious leaders criticised Nazi policies. The Confessional Church opposed Nazi control of religion, and some Catholic leaders criticised Nazi actions such as the killing of disabled people.

Attempts to kill Hitler

Kreisau Circle

This group included army officers, aristocrats, intellectuals and religious figures. They discussed how Germany should be run after Hitler, but did not take direct action at first.

July Bomb Plot, 1944

Colonel Claus von Stauffenberg and others tried to kill Hitler with a bomb on 20 July 1944. Hitler survived because the bomb was moved and a table reduced the blast. The plotters were executed, and around 5,000 people were killed in revenge.

Why was opposition not very effective?

Nazi repression

The Gestapo, SS, courts and camps made opposition extremely dangerous.

Groups were divided

Opposition groups had different aims and did not work together effectively.

Many Germans still supported Hitler

Some people genuinely supported the Nazis or benefited from Nazi policies.

Plots failed

The most serious attempt, the July Bomb Plot, failed and was followed by brutal punishment.

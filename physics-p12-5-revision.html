🦴

Imaging

X-rays pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by bones, teeth and metal — creating a shadow image.

🎯

Therapy

High-energy X-rays are used to destroy cancerous tumours deep inside the body.

☢️

Ionising

X-rays are ionising radiation — they can damage living cells and cause gene mutation.

📊

Radiation Dose

Measured in sieverts (Sv). Depends on radiation type, exposure time, and energy absorbed per second.

📷 How X-ray Imaging Works

X-rays from the tube pass through the patient's body. A lightproof cassette with a photographic film (or a flat-panel detector) is placed on the other side of the patient.

Soft Tissue

X-rays pass through — appear dark on film. Organs, muscles, fat.

Bones & Teeth

X-rays are absorbed — appear lighter/bright on film. Also metal objects.

Contrast Medium

A substance that absorbs X-rays easily (e.g. barium) — used to image soft organs like the stomach.

Lead Plates

Block X-rays reaching other parts of the body. Lead is a good absorber of X-rays.

📱 Charge-Coupled Device (CCD)

A flat-panel detector contains a CCD — sensors that convert X-rays into light, then into electronic signals sent to a computer to display a digital X-ray image.

☢️ Radiation Dose

FactorExplanation
Type of radiationAlpha causes 10× more damage than X-rays for the same exposure time
Time of exposureLonger exposure = greater dose
Energy per second absorbedMore energy absorbed = greater dose
Units: Sieverts (Sv) or millisieverts (mSv). 1 Sv = 1000 mSv. You do not need to remember the unit of radiation dose.

High Doses

Kill living cells.

Low Doses

Can cause gene mutation and cancerous growth. No safe limit exists.

Background Radiation

Everyone is exposed: cosmic radiation from space, radon gas from underground.

Film Badge

Workers who use ionising radiation must wear a film badge to monitor their exposure.
📝 Exam Tip Medical X-rays account for about 13% of the average 2 mSv/year radiation dose from ionising radiation. Average medical X-ray dose = ~0.26 mSv per year.

🎯 X-ray Therapy

📷 Imaging X-rays

  • Lower energy
  • Absorbed by bones & teeth
  • Pass through soft tissue
  • Cannot destroy tumours
  • Longer wavelength

🎯 Therapy X-rays

  • Higher energy
  • Carry enough energy to destroy tumours
  • Shorter wavelength
  • Thick plates stop X-rays reaching healthy tissue
  • Gap between plates directs beam at tumour
⚠️ Common Mistake X-rays used for imaging cannot be used for therapy — they are low energy and do not carry enough energy to destroy cancerous tumours. Therapy X-rays are shorter in wavelength (higher frequency = higher energy).

✅ Key Points Summary

  • X-rays are used to make images of internal body parts
  • X-rays are also used to destroy tumours at or near the body surface
  • X-rays are ionising radiation — they damage living cells as they pass through
  • X-rays are absorbed more by bones and teeth than by soft tissues
  • Radiation dose depends on: type of radiation, time of exposure, energy absorbed per second
  • Therapy X-rays are shorter wavelength (higher energy) than imaging X-rays

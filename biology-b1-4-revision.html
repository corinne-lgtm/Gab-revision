B1.4 Specialisation in animal cells

How cells differentiate to become specialised, and how the structure of nerve, muscle and sperm cells relates to their function.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
As cells differentiate, they develop sub-cellular structures that allow them to carry out a specific function.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How cells differentiate to form specialised cells
  • Animal cells that are specialised to carry out a particular function
  • How structure relates to function in specialised cells

What is cell differentiation?

🔄

The process

As an organism develops, cells differentiate — they change to form different types of specialised cells. Each specialised cell acquires different sub-cellular structures to enable it to carry out a specific function.

🐣

When does it happen?

Most animal cells differentiate at an early stage of development. Many types of plant cells retain the ability to differentiate throughout life.

🏢

Organisation

Specialised cells can work individually (like sperm and eggs) or as part of a tissue, organ or whole organism (like nerve or muscle cells).

⚠️ When looking at a specialised cell, ask yourself: How is it different from a generalised cell? How does that difference help it carry out its function?

Nerve cells (neurons)

⚡
Nerve cell
Function: carry electrical impulses rapidly around the body
🌿 Many dendrites
Make connections to other nerve cells, allowing impulses to be received from many sources.
📏 Long axon
Carries impulses from one place to another. Can be up to 25 m long in a blue whale! Yours runs from spine to big toe.
🔗 Synapses
Nerve endings adapted to pass impulses to other cells using special transmitter chemicals.
⚡ Mitochondria
Lots of mitochondria at synapses to provide energy for making transmitter chemicals.
🛡️ Myelin sheath
Insulating layer around the axon that speeds up the transmission of electrical impulses.

Muscle cells

💪
Striated muscle cell
Function: contract and relax to move bones and organs
🔗 Special proteins
Contain proteins that slide over each other, making the fibres contract.
⚡ Many mitochondria
Transfer the energy needed for the chemical reactions as cells contract and relax.
🍬 Glycogen stores
Store glycogen which can be broken down and used in cellular respiration to provide energy for contraction.
Muscles contract and relax in pairs to move bones. Smooth muscle cells form layers in the digestive system and squeeze food through the gut.

Sperm cells

🏊
Sperm cell
Function: carry genetic information from male parent and fertilise an egg
🌀 Long tail
Whips from side to side to move the sperm through water or the female reproductive system towards the egg.
⚡ Middle section — mitochondria
Full of mitochondria to transfer the energy needed for the tail to work.
🧪 Acrosome
Stores digestive enzymes used to break down the outer layers of the egg so the sperm can enter.
🧬 Large nucleus
Contains the genetic information (DNA) to be passed on to the offspring.

Exam tip

How to answer a 6-mark question about an unknown specialised cell:
  • Look at what structures the cell has in large quantities (e.g. many mitochondria → needs lots of energy)
  • Look at the cell's shape (e.g. long and thin → carries things over distances)
  • Consider what sub-cellular structures are present or absent
  • Link each feature to a specific function
  • Use the phrase: "This adaptation allows the cell to... because..."

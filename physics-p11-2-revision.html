Key Concepts
〰️

Amplitude

Maximum displacement of a point from its rest position — the height of the crest (or depth of the trough) from the middle. Bigger amplitude = more energy carried.

↔️

Wavelength (λ)

Distance from a point on one wave to the equivalent point on the next wave — e.g. crest to crest. Measured in metres (m).

🔁

Frequency (f)

Number of complete waves passing a fixed point every second. Unit: hertz (Hz). 1 Hz = 1 wave per second.

⏱️

Period (T)

Time for one complete wave to pass a fixed point. Measured in seconds (s). Directly linked to frequency.

Formulae
Wave Speed Equation
v = f × λ
v (m/s)  ·  f (Hz)  ·  λ (m)
Period & Frequency
T = 1 / f
T (s)  ·  f (Hz)
💡 The bigger the amplitude, the more energy the wave carries — but amplitude does not affect wave speed, frequency, or wavelength.

Frequency vs Wavelength

Higher Frequency

  • More waves per second
  • Shorter wavelength
  • Higher period is shorter (T↓)
  • Same speed in same medium

Lower Frequency

  • Fewer waves per second
  • Longer wavelength
  • Period is longer (T↑)
  • Same speed in same medium

Exam Tips
⭐ Exam Tip — Rearranging v = f × λ If you're given speed and frequency, rearrange: λ = v ÷ f. If given speed and wavelength: f = v ÷ λ. Always show your rearrangement and include units.
⭐ Exam Tip — Period If a question gives frequency in kHz (kilohertz), convert first: 1 kHz = 1000 Hz. Then calculate T = 1/f. Show all steps.
⚠️ Common Mistake Don't confuse amplitude with wavelength. Amplitude is the height of the wave from the middle — not from crest to trough (that's twice the amplitude).
⚠️ Common Mistake Changing the amplitude does NOT change the wave speed, frequency, or wavelength — those depend on the source and medium only.

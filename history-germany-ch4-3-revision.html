AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

4.3 Who voted for the Nazis?

Different groups supported the Nazis for different reasons. The Nazis adapted their message so farmers, women, young people, middle-class voters and rich business owners could all feel that the party offered something to them.

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index 📚 Germany index ✏️ Quiz rapide
The Nazi vote was not from one single group. The Nazis became popular because they promised different things to different people.
🎯

Objectives

  • Outline the main types of people who supported the Nazi Party.
  • Explain why each group found Nazi promises appealing.
  • Understand that not everyone voted Nazi, but their message reached many different sections of society.

Who supported the Nazis?

🌾

Farmers and countryside voters

  • Farmers had been hit hard by the Depression.
  • Food prices were low, so many farmers struggled to make money.
  • The Weimar Government seemed unable to help them.
  • The Nazis promised farmers higher prices and a better quality of life.
  • They also promised to protect farmers from communism, which threatened private land ownership.
🏪

Middle-class voters

  • Small business owners, doctors, bank workers and managers often voted Nazi.
  • Many had lost wages, savings or jobs during the Depression.
  • They feared disorder, crime and communism.
  • The Nazis promised law, order and strong leadership.
  • They also promised to deal with problems decisively, unlike the arguing Weimar politicians.
👩‍👧

Women

  • The Nazis appealed to many women by talking about family life and moral values.
  • They promised stability after years of crisis.
  • Some women agreed that young people had been exposed to too much modern culture, such as films, theatre and magazines.
  • Nazi propaganda presented Hitler as someone who would restore discipline and protect families.
🧑‍✈️

Young people

  • Hitler presented himself as a former soldier who understood courage, discipline and national pride.
  • Many young people wanted excitement, jobs and a stronger Germany.
  • The Nazis promised to rebuild the armed forces, create work and restore Germany’s status.
  • Hitler’s speeches could make young people feel inspired and hopeful.
🏭

Rich business owners and upper classes

  • Some rich factory owners did not agree with every Nazi policy.
  • However, many supported Hitler because he opposed communism.
  • They feared communists would take over businesses and wealth.
  • Hitler promised to let factory owners run their businesses how they wanted.
  • He also planned to rebuild the army, which could bring large government contracts to factories.

Why did Nazi messages work?

Germany was in crisis after the Depression
➡
Different groups felt scared or angry
➡
The Nazis offered simple promises: jobs, order, family, pride and protection from communism

Important exam point: not everyone supported the Nazis

Why this matters

The Nazis gained support from many groups, but Germany was still divided. Many people still disliked Hitler or voted for other parties.

Example

One former German soldier later said Hitler seemed strange and unpleasant when he first heard him speak. This reminds us that Nazi appeal was powerful for some people, but not automatic for everyone.

Key points to remember

FarmersWanted better prices and protection from communism.
Middle classWanted order, strong leadership and protection of their savings/businesses.
WomenWere targeted with messages about family, morality and stability.
Young peopleWere attracted by jobs, military strength and national pride.
Rich business ownersFeared communism and thought the Nazis would protect wealth and industry.

Exam tip

For a strong answer, do not just write “people voted Nazi because of the Depression”. Explain which group, what problem they had, and which Nazi promise appealed to them.

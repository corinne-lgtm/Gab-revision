Core Concepts
🚗
Constant Velocity
Driving force = resistive forces (friction + air resistance). Net force = zero.
🧠
Thinking Distance
Distance travelled during reaction time. Proportional to speed.
🛑
Braking Distance
Distance to stop once brakes are applied. Proportional to speed².
Stopping distance = Thinking distance + Braking distance
At 30 mph (13 m/s): thinking 9 m + braking 14 m = 23 m total. At 70 mph (31 m/s): thinking 21 m + braking 75 m = 96 m total.
Key Formulas
F = ma
Braking force (N) = mass × deceleration
v² = u² + 2as
Higher: links speed, deceleration, distance
a = −u² / 2s
Higher: deceleration when final speed = 0

Factors Affecting Stopping Distance
📐 Thinking Distance Increases When…
  • Driver is tired or drowsy
  • Driver has drunk alcohol
  • Driver has taken certain drugs
  • Driver is distracted (e.g. mobile phone)
  • Vehicle is travelling faster
🛞 Braking Distance Increases When…
  • Road is wet or icy
  • Tyres are worn or under-inflated
  • Brakes are poorly maintained
  • Vehicle is travelling faster
  • Vehicle has greater mass
⚠️ Common Mistake
  • Students often confuse thinking distance with braking distance — remember: thinking = reaction time; braking = force applied.
🔢 Proportionality
  • Thinking distance ∝ speed (double speed → double thinking distance)
  • Braking distance ∝ speed² (double speed → × 4 braking distance)
⭐ Exam Tip
When explaining why doubling speed more than doubles braking distance, say: "The braking distance is proportional to speed squared. Doubling the speed quadruples the kinetic energy, so the brakes must do four times more work, meaning four times the braking distance."
⚡ Higher Tier Only
To find deceleration on a flat dry road (max ~6 m/s²), use a = −u² / 2s. Rearranging v² = u² + 2as with v = 0 gives s = −u² / 2a. This shows braking distance is proportional to u².
🚗 Constant Velocity
  • Net force = zero
  • Driving force = air resistance + friction
  • Accelerator varies engine's driving force
🏋️ Mass Matters
  • Car: ~1 000 kg → braking force ~6 kN
  • Heavy truck: ~38 000 kg → braking force ~250 kN
  • Same forces needed to accelerate both

Key Terms — hover to reveal
Stopping distanceThe shortest distance a vehicle can safely stop in. = Thinking distance + Braking distance. Thinking distanceDistance travelled at constant speed during the driver's reaction time. Braking distanceDistance the vehicle travels from when the brakes are applied until it stops. Reaction timeTime between a stimulus and a response. Typical range: 0.4–0.9 s. Resultant forceThe single force that has the same effect as all forces acting combined. Zero = constant velocity. DecelerationNegative acceleration — the rate at which a vehicle slows down (m/s²). FrictionA resistive force between surfaces in contact. Opposes motion. Air resistanceDrag force from the air that opposes a vehicle's motion; increases with speed.
📌 Key Points Summary
  • Friction and air resistance oppose the driving force of a vehicle.
  • Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance.
  • High speed, poor weather conditions, and poor vehicle maintenance all increase the braking distance.
  • Tiredness, alcohol, drugs, or high speed increase the thinking distance.
  • F = ma gives the braking force of a vehicle.

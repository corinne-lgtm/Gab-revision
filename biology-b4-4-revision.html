B4.4 Helping the heart

How the heart keeps its natural rhythm, how artificial pacemakers work, and what artificial hearts can do.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
When the heart has problems — leaky valves, irregular rhythm, or complete failure — medicine and technology can replace, regulate or support it.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How the heart keeps its natural rhythm
  • How artificial pacemakers work
  • What artificial hearts can do

Natural pacemaker

⚡

The resting heart rate of ~70 beats/min is controlled by a group of cells in the right atrium — the natural pacemaker. These send electrical signals through conducting fibres to make the heart beat in a coordinated rhythm.

If the natural pacemaker stops working properly:

  • Heart beats too slowly → person doesn't get enough oxygen
  • Heart beats too fast → cannot pump blood properly

Leaky heart valves

Heart valves must withstand enormous pressure. Over time they can leak or become stiff and not open fully — making the heart less efficient. People may become breathless and, without treatment, will eventually die.

Faulty valves can be replaced surgically with two types:

🔩 Mechanical valves

  • Made of titanium and polymers
  • Last a very long time
  • Patient must take blood-thinning medication for life to prevent clotting around the valve

🐷 Biological valves

  • Based on valves from animals (pigs, cattle) or human donors
  • Work extremely well — no medication needed
  • Only last approximately 12–15 years before needing replacement

Artificial pacemakers

🔋

An artificial pacemaker is an electrical device used to correct irregularities in the heart rate. It is implanted under the skin of the chest and attached to the heart by two wires.

  • Weighs only 20–50 g
  • Sends strong, regular electrical signals to the heart to stimulate it to beat properly
  • Modern pacemakers are sensitive to body needs — only work when the natural rhythm goes wrong
  • Some stimulate the heart to beat faster during exercise
  • Patient needs regular medical check-ups throughout life
💡 A pacemaker is a small price to pay for the improved quality and length of life it brings.

Artificial hearts

🤖

What they are

Mechanical devices that replace the heart when it fails completely. They need a lot of machinery to keep them working. By 2015 almost 1500 people worldwide had been fitted with a completely artificial heart.

⏳

Temporary use

Most commonly used to keep patients alive while waiting for a donor heart transplant, or to give a diseased heart a rest so it can recover. Most patients must stay in hospital until transplant.

🎒

Progress

In 2011, Matthew Green became the first UK patient to leave hospital with a fully artificial heart carried in a backpack — keeping him alive for two years until a donor heart became available.

⚠️ A key risk of artificial hearts: blood clotting can occur inside the device, which can be fatal.

Arguments for and against artificial hearts

✅

For

  • Keep patients alive while waiting for a donor
  • Give a diseased heart time to rest and recover
  • Allow a relatively normal life (portable versions)
  • Reduce pressure on donor waiting lists
  • Technology is rapidly improving
❌

Against

  • Risk of blood clotting inside device → can be fatal
  • Need a lot of machinery to operate
  • Most patients must remain in hospital
  • Very expensive to develop and use
  • Permanent replacement still a long way off

Exam tip

  • Describe how an artificial pacemaker works [3 marks]
  • Explain how a leaky heart valve can cause health issues [4 marks]
  • Give one advantage and one disadvantage of: a biological valve [2] and a mechanical valve [2]

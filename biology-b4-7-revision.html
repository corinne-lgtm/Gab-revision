B4.7 Transport systems in plants

The substances transported in plants, and how transport in xylem differs from transport in phloem.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Plants have xylem (transports water & minerals upwards) and phloem (transports dissolved sugars in both directions) — two separate systems, both vital for life.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • The substances transported in plants
  • How transport in xylem differs from transport in phloem

Why plants need a transport system

🍬

Glucose (sugars)

Made by photosynthesis in the leaves and green parts — but needed by every cell in the plant for respiration, growth, and storage. Must be transported everywhere.

💧

Water

Absorbed by roots from the soil — but needed by every cell. Required for photosynthesis, to keep cells turgid (upright), and for chemical reactions.

⚗️

Mineral ions

Absorbed by roots from the soil — needed throughout the plant to make proteins and other molecules for growth. Cannot be made by the plant.

Xylem vs Phloem — comparison

Feature💧 Xylem🍬 Phloem
What is transportedWater and dissolved mineral ionsDissolved sugars (food — mainly sucrose)
DirectionUpwards only — roots → stem → leavesBoth directions — from leaves to rest of plant (up or down)
Living or dead?Mature xylem cells are deadPhloem cells are living
Cell structureHollow tubes — no cell contents; thick, lignified walls for supportSieve tubes with sieve plates; companion cells provide energy
How it movesPulled up by transpiration (water evaporating from leaves)Active transport — requires energy (translocation)
Name for the processTranspiration streamTranslocation
Where found in stemVascular bundles (towards centre)Vascular bundles (towards outside, under bark in trees)
⚠️ Study tip: Phloem transports food (Photosynthate). Xylem transports water (think X-shaped cross-sections). Phloem is LIVING; Xylem is DEAD.

Phloem — moving food (translocation)

🍬

Phloem transports sugars made by photosynthesis from the leaves to:

  • Growing areas of stems and roots — where sugars are needed to make new plant cells
  • Storage organs — e.g. roots, tubers, fruits — where sugars are stored as starch for the winter

The movement of dissolved sugars from leaves to the rest of the plant is called translocation. Phloem is a living tissue — the process requires energy from the companion cells.

🐛 Aphids push their mouthparts directly into the phloem and feed on the sugary fluid. A major infestation can kill a plant by taking all of its food.

Xylem — moving water and minerals

💧

Xylem carries water and dissolved mineral ions from the soil (via roots) up through the stem to the leaves. Mature xylem cells are dead — hollow tubes with no cell contents to block flow. Thick lignified walls provide strength and support.

In woody plants like trees, xylem makes up the bulk of the wood. Phloem is found in a ring just under the bark. If a complete ring of bark is removed or eaten (by deer etc.), the phloem is destroyed and the tree dies — this is why tree trunks need protection.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • State why a plant needs a transport system [1 mark]
  • Describe the main differences between xylem and phloem [3 marks]
  • A woodland trust puts protective covers on young tree trunks — explain exactly why this is necessary and the impact if trees were not protected [6 marks]

Key for 6-mark question: Phloem is under the bark (ring) → deer eat bark → phloem destroyed → translocation stops → no sugars reach roots/growing areas → cells cannot respire/grow → tree dies.

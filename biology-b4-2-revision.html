B4.2 The blood vessels

How the blood flows round the body, the different types of blood vessels, the importance of valves, and the double circulatory system.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Blood travels in three types of vessel: arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins return it, and capillaries link them — exchanging substances with every cell.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How blood flows round the body
  • The different types of blood vessels
  • Why valves are important
  • The importance of a double circulatory system

Comparing the three blood vessels

Feature 🔴 Artery 🔵 Vein 🟢 Capillary
Direction Away from heart → organs Organs → back to heart Links arteries to veins
Blood type Usually oxygenated (bright red) Usually deoxygenated (dark purple-red) Transitions oxygenated → deoxygenated
Wall thickness Thick — muscle + elastic fibres Thinner walls One cell thick (very thin)
Lumen size Small lumen (relative to wall) Large lumen Tiny, narrow lumen
Pressure High — blood under pressure from heart Low — blood returning to heart Very low
Valves? No Yes — prevent backflow No
Pulse? Yes — you can feel it at wrist, neck No pulse No
Key function Transport blood at high pressure to organs. Elastic walls stretch + recoil with each heartbeat. Return blood to heart. Valves + skeletal muscle contractions squeeze blood towards heart. Exchange of O₂, CO₂, glucose, urea with body cells by diffusion through walls.
⚠️ Study tip: Arteries carry blood Away from the heart. This applies to BOTH the pulmonary and aortic arteries — even the pulmonary artery (which carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs) carries blood AWAY from the heart.

Why valves are important

🚪

Valves in veins

Blood in veins is at low pressure — there is a risk it could flow backwards. Valves open when blood flows towards the heart, and close when blood starts to flow backwards — preventing backflow.

Blood in veins is also squeezed forwards by the contraction of skeletal muscles surrounding the veins.

💧

Capillary exchange

Capillaries have walls only one cell thick — substances can easily diffuse in and out:

  • O₂ and glucose → out to body cells
  • CO₂ and urea → in from body cells into blood

Every cell in your body is within 0.05 mm of a capillary.

Double circulatory system

Humans have a double circulatory system — two separate loops that both pass through the heart:

🫁
Pulmonary circulation

Heart → lungs → heart
Allows O₂ and CO₂ to be exchanged with the air in the lungs.

🫀
Systemic circulation

Heart → all other organs → heart
Delivers fully oxygenated blood to all body organs at high pressure.

💡 Why is double circulation better? Fully oxygenated blood returns to the heart from the lungs, is pumped again at full pressure, and can be sent quickly to all parts of the body. A single circulation (like fish) means blood loses pressure as it passes through the gills — it reaches body organs at low pressure, making active animals less efficient.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • State the function of arteries / veins / capillaries + relate structure to function [3+3+2 marks]
  • Describe how heart, arteries, veins, capillaries are linked [2 marks]
  • Describe what happens in capillaries in a cell [2 marks]
  • Fish have a single circulation — describe the disadvantages for an active land mammal [4 marks]

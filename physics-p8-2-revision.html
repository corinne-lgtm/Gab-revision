💡 The Big Ideas
💪
Force
A push or pull acting on an object due to interaction with another object
🔗
Contact Force
Requires objects to touch each other — friction, tension, normal
🔄
Newton's 3rd Law
Two objects exert equal and opposite forces on each other
🚗
Driving Force
Engine force that moves the car; balanced by friction from the road
⚡ Newton's Third Law: When two objects interact, they always exert equal and opposite forces on each other — every single time, no exceptions.
⚖️ Contact vs Non-Contact Forces

✋ Contact Forces

  • Objects must touch to interact
  • Friction — opposes motion
  • Air resistance — drag through air
  • Tension — stretching forces in ropes
  • Normal contact force — support

🌐 Non-Contact Forces

  • Act at a distance — no touching needed
  • Magnetic force — magnets attracting/repelling
  • Electrostatic force — charged objects
  • Gravity — force of attraction between masses
🎮 Interactive Demo: Equal & Opposite Forces

Two roller skaters on a rope

🧑
👩
⬅️ Pull (100 N) Pull (100 N) ➡️
Each skater pulls the other with exactly 100 N — same size, opposite direction
✅ The forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction — this is Newton's Third Law in action.
🚘 Friction in Action

Driving force vs Friction at the tyre

🚗
➡️ Friction of road on tyre (forward) Friction of tyre on road (backward) ⬅️
These two friction forces are equal and opposite — a Newton's Third Law pair
⚠️ The driving force is the engine/motive force pushing the car forward.
✅ Friction acts at the contact point between tyre and road — it's what actually propels the car.

🧩 Key Concepts at a Glance

Force definition

A push or pull due to interaction with another object

Unit of force

The newton (N) — named after Isaac Newton

Newton's 3rd Law

Equal and opposite forces always act in pairs between objects

Friction

Contact force opposing relative motion at surfaces

📝
Exam Tip: When describing a Newton's Third Law pair, you must name both objects and both forces — e.g. "the force of the tyre on the road" AND "the force of the road on the tyre." Don't just say "equal and opposite forces."
🚨

Common mistake: Newton's Third Law pairs act on different objects. A book resting on a table — the weight of the book downward and the normal force of the table upward are NOT a Newton's Third Law pair (they act on the same object!)

🌧️ In the Mud — Unequal Effects

To pull a car from mud, the tractor's engine force must be greater than the mud's resistance force on the car.

❌ The mud's force on the tractor ≠ mud's force on the car (the forces on each object are different sizes).
⚠️ The rope pull on the car = the car's pull on the rope — that is the Newton's Third Law pair.
Remember:

Newton's 3rd Law is always true — equal & opposite forces between two objects — but the effect depends on mass. The larger the mass, the smaller the acceleration (Newton's 2nd Law — coming in P10.1!).

⭐ Key Points Summary
Forces can change the shape of an object, or change its motion or its state of rest.
The unit of force is the newton (N).
A contact force is a force that acts on objects only when the objects touch each other.
When two objects interact, they always exert equal and opposite forces on each other.
The driving force on a car is the engine/motive force — friction between tyre and road actually propels it forward.
The effects of equal forces on each object depend on their masses — larger mass = smaller effect.

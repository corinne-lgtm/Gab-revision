B3.3 The chemistry of food

The basic structures of carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
The three main food groups — carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins — are large molecules made up of smaller building blocks joined together.
🎯

Learning objective

  • The basic structures of carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids
  • How to test foods for each type

Carbohydrates

🍞

Carbohydrates provide fuel for cellular respiration — they are the body's main energy source. They contain the elements carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.

All carbohydrates are made up of units of sugars.

🍬 Simple sugars (monosaccharides)

  • Contain only one or two sugar units
  • Best known single sugar: glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)
  • Two sugar units joined: e.g. sucrose (table sugar)
  • Turn Benedict's solution brick red on heating

🌾 Complex carbohydrates

  • Made of long chains of simple sugar units bonded together
  • Examples: starch (energy store in plants), cellulose (structural in plants)
  • Most carbohydrates are broken down to glucose for respiration
  • Starch turns iodine solution yellow-red → blue-black

Lipids

🧈

Lipids are fats (solids) and oils (liquids). They are the most efficient energy store in the body and an important source of energy in the diet. They also form cell membranes, hormones, and components of the nervous system.

Like carbohydrates, lipids are made of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. All lipids are insoluble in water.

A lipid molecule = 3 fatty acid molecules joined to a molecule of glycerol. The glycerol is always the same — the different fatty acid combinations determine whether the lipid is a solid fat or a liquid oil.

💡 Glycerol + 3 fatty acids = 1 lipid molecule. Always remember: THREE fatty acids joined to ONE glycerol.

Proteins

💪

Proteins are used for building cells and tissues and as the basis of all your enzymes. About 15–16% of body mass is protein. Proteins contain carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen.

A protein molecule is made up of long chains of amino acids. There are around 20 different amino acids, joined by special bonds. Different arrangements produce different proteins.

Chains of amino acids are folded, coiled, and twisted into specific 3D shapes — which allow other molecules to fit into the protein (e.g. enzymes and substrates).

Functions of proteins

  • Structural — muscles, tendons, hair, skin
  • Hormones — e.g. insulin
  • Antibodies — destroy pathogens (immune system)
  • Enzymes — catalyse reactions in cells

Denaturation

The bonds holding the 3D shape of a protein are very sensitive to temperature and pH. If conditions change too much, the shape is lost — the protein is denatured and can no longer function.

Food tests

Food type Test Positive result Notes
Starch (complex carbohydrate) Add iodine solution Yellow-red → blue-black Simple sugars give no colour change
Sugars (simple carbohydrates) Add Benedict's solution + heat Blue → brick red Must be heated. Tests for reducing sugars (e.g. glucose)
Proteins Biuret test — add Biuret reagent (NaOH + CuSO₄) Blue → purple Biuret solution is corrosive — wear eye protection
Lipids Ethanol test — add ethanol, then pour into water Cloudy white layer forms Ethanol is highly flammable and harmful — handle with care

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • State what a protein is [1 mark] + how proteins are used in the body [4 marks]
  • Describe the main similarities and differences between carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids [6 marks]
  • Describe how you would test a food sample for starch / lipids [2 marks each]
  • Explain why lipids can be either fats or oils [3 marks]
  • Explain how simple sugars are related to complex carbohydrates [3 marks]

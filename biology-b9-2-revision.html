B9.2 The response to exercise

How your body responds to the increased demands for energy during exercise.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
During exercise, muscles need more glucose + O₂ and produce more CO₂ — so the heart rate, breathing rate, and breath volume all increase to keep up.
🎯

Learning objective

  • How your body responds to the increased demands for energy during exercise

Why muscles need a lot of energy

Muscles are made of protein fibres that contract when energy is transferred from respiration. They contain many mitochondria to carry out aerobic respiration.

Muscles also store glucose as the carbohydrate glycogen. During exercise, glycogen is rapidly converted back to glucose for aerobic respiration — providing a fast local fuel supply.

⚠️ Study tip: Rate of respiration = how fast glucose + oxygen are used up. Breathing rate = how fast you breathe. Rate of respiration ≠ breathing rate — these are different things.

What happens during exercise — three responses

❤️

1. Heart rate increases

Heart beats faster AND arteries supplying muscles dilate (widen) → more oxygenated blood delivered to muscles per minute → more O₂ and glucose supplied → faster respiration → more energy for contraction. Also removes CO₂ faster.

💨

2. Breathing rate + depth increase

You breathe more often AND more deeply (greater volume of air each breath) → more O₂ enters blood → more CO₂ removed. Both rate and depth matter — "rate and depth" not just "rate".

🍬

3. Glycogen → glucose

Glycogen stored in muscles is converted to glucose — providing cells directly with the fuel they need for increased aerobic respiration. Not all tissues store glycogen — this is why muscles have it but most other tissues don't.

💡 All three responses act together to increase the rate of supply of glucose and oxygen to muscles, and to increase the rate of removal of carbon dioxide from muscles.

Fit vs unfit — heart and lung data (Table 1)

MeasurementUnfit personFit person
Blood pumped per beat at rest (cm³)6480
Volume of heart at rest (cm³)120140
Resting breathing rate (breaths/min)1412
Resting pulse rate (beats/min)7263
💡 A fit person's heart is larger and stronger — it pumps more blood per beat. So at rest it doesn't need to beat as fast (lower resting pulse). Similarly, a fit person's lungs are more efficient — lower resting breathing rate. During exercise, a fit person can deliver more O₂ per beat, so their heart and breathing rate don't need to rise as much.

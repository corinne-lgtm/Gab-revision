B7.4 Diet, exercise, and disease

The effect of diet and exercise on the development of obesity, cardiovascular disease, and type 2 diabetes.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Poor diet and lack of exercise → obesity → increased risk of cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes. Exercise directly lowers these risks too.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • Effect of diet and exercise on obesity
  • How diet and exercise affect risk of cardiovascular disease
  • Obesity as a risk factor for type 2 diabetes

Diet, exercise, and obesity

🍔

How obesity develops

If you eat more food than you need, the excess is stored as fat. Some body fat is normal and useful. However, regularly eating too much → overweight → obese.

Obesity leads to serious health problems: type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease.

🏃

Exercise and body weight

Exercise increases the amount of respiration in muscles → more energy used from food. Regular exercise makes bigger muscles (up to 40% of body mass) — muscle needs much more energy than fat. This raises your metabolic rate.

60–75% of daily food is needed for basic body reactions; ~10% to digest food; only 15–30% is affected by physical activity level.

How exercise directly protects health

❤️

Fitter heart and lungs

Regular exercise gives you a fitter heart (pumps blood more efficiently) and bigger lungs — both reduce cardiovascular disease risk.

📉

Lower cholesterol

Regular exercise lowers blood cholesterol levels and helps maintain a better balance of cholesterol types. Reduces risk of fatty deposits building up in coronary arteries → lower heart disease risk.

🩸

Lower blood pressure

Exercise lowers blood pressure — reducing strain on the heart and arteries. Reduces risk of heart attacks and strokes.

⚖️

Healthy weight

Exercise helps maintain a healthy body weight — reducing risk of obesity, type 2 diabetes, arthritis, and other health problems.

💡 People who exercise regularly are much less likely to develop cardiovascular disease and many other health problems. The least active people have approximately 5x the relative risk of cardiovascular death compared to the most active.

Obesity and type 2 diabetes

In type 2 diabetes, either:

  • Your body doesn't make enough insulin to control blood sugar levels, OR
  • Your cells stop responding to insulin (insulin resistance)

This leads to problems with: circulation, kidney function, and eyesight — and may eventually lead to death.

Type 2 diabetes is more common with age. Some people have a genetic tendency. But being overweight/obese and doing little exercise are strong risk factors at any age.

By 2025, an estimated 4 million people in the UK will have diabetes — 90% type 2. Many cases can be managed or reversed by: eating a balanced diet, controlling carbohydrate intake, losing weight, and regular exercise.

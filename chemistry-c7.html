C7.1

Exothermic and endothermic reactions

Energy is transferred, not created or destroyed.

Required Practical 10

Exothermic

Energy transfers from the reacting substances to the surroundings, so the surroundings warm up. Combustion and neutralisation are examples.

Endothermic

Energy transfers from the surroundings to the reacting substances, so the surroundings cool. Thermal decomposition is an example.

Conservation

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. Chemical reactions transfer energy while bonds are broken and made.

C7.2

Using energy transfers

Warm things with exothermic changes; cool things with endothermic changes.

Applications

Warmers / self-heating cans

Disposable iron oxidation and reusable sodium ethanoate crystallisation hand warmers are exothermic. Self-heating cans can use:

calcium oxide + water → calcium hydroxide

Cold packs

Ammonium nitrate dissolving in water is endothermic. Energy is absorbed from the surroundings, so the pack cools.

AQA GCSE Biology · B5 Communicable diseases

B5.1 Health and disease

What health is, the different causes of ill health, and how different types of disease interact.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Health is physical and mental well-being — not just the absence of disease. Diet, stress, life situations, and pathogens all interact to affect it.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • What health is
  • The different causes of ill health
  • How different types of disease interact

What is health?

Health is a state of physical and mental well-being — not just the absence of disease. It is at least partly based on individual perceptions. A cold might mean staying in bed on a school day but not ruin a holiday.

Communicable vs Non-communicable diseases

🦠

Communicable (infectious)

Caused by pathogens (bacteria, viruses, fungi, protists) that can be passed from person to person.

Examples: tuberculosis, flu, malaria, HIV/AIDS, diarrhoeal diseases.

🫀

Non-communicable

Cannot be transmitted from person to person. Often linked to lifestyle, genetics, or environment.

Examples: heart disease, arthritis, cancer, type 2 diabetes.

💡 Both communicable and non-communicable diseases are major causes of ill health — but other factors also play a big role.

Other causes of ill health

🥗

Diet

  • Too little food → starvation, anaemia, rickets
  • Too much / wrong food → obesity, type 2 diabetes, some cancers
  • Malnutrition → weakened immune system
😰

Stress

Some stress is necessary — but too much is increasingly linked to heart disease, some cancers, and mental health problems.

🌍

Life situations

  • Where in the world you live
  • Gender and ethnic group
  • Financial status
  • Access to free healthcare
  • Local sewage and rubbish disposal

People often have little control over life situations — especially children. These factors drive many causes of ill health worldwide.

How health problems interact

🔗

Virus → Cancer

Some viruses trigger cell changes that lead to cancer. The human papilloma virus (HPV) can cause cervical cancer.

🛡️

Immune deficiency → More infections

Defects in the immune system — due to genetics, poor nutrition, or HIV/AIDS — mean the body cannot fight pathogens effectively → more communicable diseases.

🤧

Pathogen → Allergy

Immune reactions initially caused by a pathogen (e.g. common cold) can trigger allergies to environmental factors → skin rashes, hives, or asthma.

🧠

Physical → Mental health

Severe physical ill health can lead to depression and other mental illness. Physical and mental health are often closely linked.

🍔

Malnutrition → Multiple diseases

Malnutrition is linked to deficiency diseases, weakened immunity, obesity, cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and cancer.

⚠️ Key principle: The interaction between different factors — lifestyle, environment, and pathogens — is an important principle when looking at any type of disease.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Define what is meant by good health [1 mark]
  • State three different factors that can cause ill health + give one example each [6 marks]
  • Explain how interactions between different types of disease can affect prevalence of disease around the world [6 marks]
  • Data question on TB rates vs HIV status / drug use [graph reading, 1–2 marks each]

Key definition: "Good health is a state of complete physical and mental well-being, not merely the absence of disease."

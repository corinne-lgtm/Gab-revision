Lesson C12.1

Finite and renewable resources

01 · Understand

Finite resources are used faster than they can be replaced: metal ores, fossil fuels and limestone. Renewable resources can be replenished at a similar rate to their use, if managed sustainably.

02 · Connect

Synthetic alternatives can replace natural products: cotton → polyester; wool → acrylic; wood → composites. Bioethanol can be made from crops rather than finite crude oil. Sustainability means meeting today’s needs without harming future generations.

03 · Exam detail

A resource may be renewable but still be used unsustainably. Reserve-life estimates depend on consumption, discoveries, recycling and assumptions.

Visual · Finite vs renewable
Crude oil / metal ores→Finite reservesManaged crops / trees→Can be regrown

Estimates of years remaining are predictions, not countdown clocks. Demand, discoveries and recycling can all change them.

Lesson C12.2

Water safe to drink

01 · Understand

Potable water is safe to drink, but it is not necessarily chemically pure. Fresh water from rivers, lakes and aquifers can contain dissolved salts and microorganisms.

02 · Connect

Fresh-water treatment: filter to remove insoluble solids → sterilise with chlorine, ozone or UV to kill microorganisms. Salty water can be desalinated by distillation or reverse osmosis.

03 · Exam detail

Distillation evaporates then condenses water, leaving dissolved salts. Reverse osmosis forces water through a membrane at high pressure. Both require energy. Required Practical 13: distil a salt solution, test the distillate for dissolved solids and measure its boiling point.

Visual · From reservoir to tap
Fresh water→Filter beds→Sterilisation→Potable water
Desalination
Salt water→Distillation OR reverse osmosis→Fresh water

Lesson C12.3

Treating waste water

01 · Understand

Sewage contains suspended solids, organic matter and harmful microorganisms. It must be treated before release to the environment.

02 · Connect

Screening removes large objects and grit. Sedimentation produces sludge and effluent. Aerobic biological treatment uses bacteria with oxygen to break down remaining organic matter. The treated effluent is settled and may be disinfected.

03 · Exam detail

Sludge is digested anaerobically, producing biogas (including methane), and may be dried for fertiliser or fuel. Sewage treatment and obtaining potable water are different processes.

Visual · Sewage treatment route
Screening→Primary sedimentation→Aerobic treatment→Final settling / disinfection
Separated sludge→Anaerobic digestion→Biogas + treated solids

Lesson C12.4

Extracting metals from ores · Higher

01 · Understand

Copper-rich ores are usually extracted by mining and smelting. Copper can also be obtained from copper salt solutions by displacement with scrap iron or by electrolysis.

02 · Connect

Displacement: Fe(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Fe²⁺(aq) + Cu(s). At the negative electrode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. At the positive copper electrode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻.

03 · Exam detail

Low-grade ores may be processed using bioleaching (bacteria) or phytomining (plants absorb copper compounds, then are harvested and burned). These methods can reduce environmental damage but are often slower.

Visual · Copper extraction options
High-grade ore→Smelting→Impure copper→Electrolytic purification
Low-grade ore→Bioleaching / phytomining→Copper-containing solution→Displacement / electrolysis

Half-equations: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (reduction); Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ (oxidation).

Lesson C12.5

Life Cycle Assessments (LCAs)

01 · Understand

An LCA considers environmental impacts throughout a product’s life: raw-material extraction → manufacture and transport → use/reuse → disposal or recycling.

02 · Connect

Inputs include raw materials, energy and water. Outputs include emissions, waste and other environmental effects. A fair comparison must use comparable assumptions and the full life cycle.

03 · Exam detail

Impact scores can be subjective because different people give different weights to global warming, pollution and resource use. Missing data, estimates and commercial bias also reduce certainty.

Visual · Product life cycle
Extract→Manufacture + transport→Use / reuse→Recycle / dispose

At every stage, compare energy, materials, water, emissions and waste. A light plastic bag can have a different transport impact from a heavier paper bag; reuse changes the comparison.

Lesson C12.6

Reduce, reuse and recycle

01 · Understand

Reducing consumption, reusing products and recycling materials save finite resources, energy and waste. Recycling metals usually uses less energy than extracting them from ores.

02 · Connect

Aluminium recycling saves around 95% of the energy needed to produce it from ore (textbook figure). Steel is often separated magnetically; copper scrap can need purification because it is mixed with other metals.

03 · Exam detail

Recycling still needs collection, sorting, transport and energy. Evaluate costs, pollution, resource savings and practical difficulties rather than claiming recycling has no impact.

Visual · Why recycle metals?
Ore extraction→Processing + high energy
Collected scrap→Sorting + remelting→New product

Worked example: If making aluminium from ore uses 100 units of energy, a 95% saving means recycling uses approximately 5 units under that comparison.

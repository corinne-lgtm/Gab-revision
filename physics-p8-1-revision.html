📍 Distance vs Displacement

Distance and Displacement

When you travel to school, the distance you travel may be much greater than the direct distance from your home to school. A bus takes a winding route, picking up students — so the distance travelled is much longer.

💡 Distance without change of direction is called displacement. In other words, displacement is distance in a certain direction.

For example, if the displacement from home to school is 5 km due East, the magnitude of displacement is 5 km and the direction is East.

✅ Displacement always needs a direction: "5 km East" not just "5 km"
⚠️ Distance can be much greater than displacement — think of a winding bus route vs. a straight line home!
⚖️ Vectors vs Scalars

What makes a quantity a vector or scalar?

🔵 Vector Quantities

  • Have size AND direction
  • Displacement
  • Velocity
  • Acceleration
  • Force
  • Momentum
  • Weight
  • Gravitational field strength

🟠 Scalar Quantities

  • Have size (magnitude) ONLY
  • Distance
  • Speed
  • Time
  • Mass
  • Energy
  • Power
  • Temperature
The magnitude of a quantity is its size — a vector has magnitude and direction, a scalar has magnitude only.
⭐ Exam Tip When asked to state the difference between a vector and scalar: always say vector has "magnitude AND direction" while scalar has "magnitude only". Don't just say "direction" — include both words!
➡️ Representing Vector Quantities

How to draw vectors as arrows

Any vector quantity can be represented by an arrow:

📌 The direction of the arrow shows the direction of the vector quantity.
📏 The length of the arrow represents the magnitude of the vector quantity.

For example, a force on a nail from a hammer can be shown by a red arrow where the length represents the force magnitude.

⚠️ If a diagram is a scale diagram, the scale must always be shown — e.g. 10 mm ≡ 1.0 N (≡ means "represents").

📐 Scale Diagrams

Drawing forces to scale

When more than one force acts on an object, forces need to be shown on a scale diagram. For example, two forces of 3.0 N and 4.0 N act at right angles — using a scale of 10 mm = 1.0 N, the arrows would be 30 mm and 40 mm long.

💬 Force diagrams don't always need to be scale diagrams — but if it is a scale diagram, the scale MUST be shown.
📚 Key Words

Glossary — hover to reveal definitions

Displacement Distance in a given direction — a vector quantity with both magnitude and direction. Vector A physical quantity that has both magnitude (size) and direction. Scalar A physical quantity that has magnitude (size) only — no direction. Magnitude The size or amount of a quantity — e.g. how many newtons, or how many kilometres. Scale diagram A diagram where arrow lengths represent force magnitudes according to a stated scale. Force diagram A diagram showing forces acting on an object as labelled arrows. Velocity Speed in a given direction — a vector quantity. Acceleration Change in velocity per unit time — a vector quantity.
⭐ Key Points

Remember these!

📌 Displacement is distance in a given direction.
📌 A vector quantity is a physical quantity which has magnitude AND direction.
📌 A scalar quantity has magnitude but NO direction.
📌 A vector quantity can be represented by an arrow — the direction of the arrow shows the direction, and the length represents the magnitude.

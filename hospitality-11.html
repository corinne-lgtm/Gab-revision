Hospitality and catering provisions

Learn → Quiz → Exam Practice. Your quiz score is saved on this device.

Lesson 1 of 5

Commercial vs non-commercial

Commercial providers aim to make a profit: restaurants, hotels, cafés and takeaways. Non-commercial providers prioritise a service or welfare, such as hospital or school catering; they still manage costs.

Lesson 2 of 5

Structure and job roles

Front of house includes reception, waiting staff and restaurant managers; back of house includes chefs, kitchen porters and stores staff. The kitchen brigade is a hierarchy led by the head chef, with sous chef and chef de partie roles.

Lesson 3 of 5

Responsibilities and contracts

The head chef manages food production and kitchen standards. Waiting staff serve customers and handle requests. Contracts may be full-time, part-time, fixed-term, temporary or zero-hours; consider hours, pay, holiday and job security.

Lesson 4 of 5

Dress codes and hygiene

Clean chef whites or uniforms, suitable non-slip footwear, restrained hair and appropriate protective clothing support hygiene and safety. Staff should follow the organisation’s policy.

Lesson 5 of 5

Sustainability and success

Reducing food waste, energy and water use, sourcing seasonal produce and recycling can lower costs and environmental impact. Success also depends on location, prices, customer reviews, competition and economic conditions.

Exam-style written questions

4 marks

Explain two ways a catering business could become more sustainable.

Award credit for two explained measures such as reducing food waste through stock control (less waste/disposal cost) and energy-efficient equipment (lower energy use and bills).

Use these indicative points for self-assessment, not an automatic mark.

4 marks

Explain two duties of a head chef.

Could include supervising food production and staff so dishes meet standards, and planning safe preparation and hygiene to protect customers.

Use these indicative points for self-assessment, not an automatic mark.

B7.3 Smoking and the risk of disease

How smoking affects cardiovascular disease, lung disease, lung cancer, and unborn babies.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Tobacco smoke contains ~4000 chemicals — at least 150 are linked to disease. Nicotine, carbon monoxide, and tar each cause specific, serious damage.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How smoking affects risk of cardiovascular disease
  • How smoking affects risk of lung disease and lung cancer
  • The effect of smoking on unborn babies

Three key chemicals in tobacco smoke

🧪

Nicotine

The addictive drug in tobacco — relatively harmless itself. Produces a sensation of calm and well-being (why people like smoking). But also:

  • Narrows blood vessels — ages the skin
  • Makes the heart rate increase
  • Increases risk of clot formation
💨

Carbon monoxide (CO)

A poisonous gas in tobacco smoke.

  • Takes up some of the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood
  • After smoking, up to 10% of blood carries CO instead of O₂
  • Leads to shortage of oxygen → smokers get breathless more easily
  • Particularly dangerous in pregnancy — restricts oxygen to fetus
  • Damages the lining of the arteries → coronary heart disease risk
🏭

Tar

A sticky black chemical that accumulates in the lungs, turning them from pink to grey.

  • Causes bronchitis — inflammation and infection of the bronchi
  • Build-up in lung tissue breaks down alveoli structure → COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) → severe breathlessness
  • Carcinogen — greatly increases risk of lung cancer
  • Also causes cancers of the throat, larynx, and trachea

Cilia damage and infections

Chemicals in tobacco smoke anaesthetise the cilia in the trachea and bronchi — the tiny hair-like projections that normally waft mucus (with trapped pathogens and dirt) up and out of the lungs. When cilia stop working:

  • Dirt and pathogens move down into the lungs
  • Risk of lung infections greatly increases
  • Mucus builds up over time → chronic cough

Smoking and cardiovascular disease

The chemicals in tobacco smoke affect the heart and blood vessels. Smoking has a proven causal mechanism for cardiovascular disease — not just a correlation.

  • Nicotine narrows blood vessels → increased blood pressure
  • Nicotine increases heart rate
  • Other chemicals damage the lining of arteries → makes coronary heart disease more likely
  • Increases risk of blood clot formation
  • This combination of effects increases risk of heart attacks and strokes

Smoking during pregnancy

Carbon monoxide reduces the oxygen available to the developing fetus. A pregnant woman carries oxygen for herself AND her fetus. If her blood carries CO instead of O₂:

  • Premature births
  • Low birthweight babies
  • Stillbirths (baby born dead)

~3500 stillbirths in the UK each year. Scientists estimate ~20% result from mothers smoking — around 700 babies per year born dead due to smoking.

Data from Figure 1: non-smokers = 3.6% stillbirth rate; smokers 1–9/day = 5.8%; 10+/day = 8.0%; all smokers = 7.1% — clear dose-response relationship.

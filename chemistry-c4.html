C4.1

Relative masses and moles

Relative atomic mass, Ar

Ar is the weighted mean mass of an atom of an element compared with 1/12 of the mass of carbon-12.

Ar = Σ(isotope mass × abundance) ÷ total abundance

Example: Cu = (69×63 + 31×65) ÷ 100 = 63.62.

Relative formula mass, Mr

Add all the Ar values shown by the formula — including atoms inside brackets.

Mr(Al₂(SO₄)₃) = 2×27 + 3×32 + 12×16 = 342
Trap: the 3 outside the bracket multiplies both S and O₄.

The mole

One mole contains the same number of stated particles, whatever the substance.

NA = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹

The mass of 1 mole in grams is numerically equal to Ar or Mr.

Mass ⇄ moles

moles = mass (g) ÷ Mr
mass (g) = moles × Mr
Higher: use this conversion repeatedly in reacting-mass and limiting-reactant problems.

Calculation route

Find Ar/Mr
Use the formula carefully.
Choose direction
mass → moles: divide. moles → mass: multiply.
Keep units and significant figures
mol for amount; g for mass.

Percentage by mass

% element = mass contribution of element ÷ Mr × 100

CO₂: carbon = 12/44×100 = 27.3%; oxygen = 72.7%.

AQA extra: apparent mass changes in open systems

Metal + oxygen: oxygen from the air enters the measured solid → mass increases.

Metal carbonate → metal oxide + CO₂: gas can escape → measured solid mass decreases.

Conservation of mass still holds. The apparent change happens because the whole system is not enclosed/measured.

AQA extra: measurement uncertainty

Every measurement has uncertainty. AQA expects you to represent a distribution of results and use the range around the mean as a measure/estimate of uncertainty.

range = highest result − lowest result

C4.2 · Higher tier

Equations and calculations

Balanced equations are mole maps

H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl

means 1 mol : 1 mol : 2 mol. The large numbers in front are coefficients/multipliers.

Never change a subscript to balance an equation: that would change the substance.

Mass-to-mass route

Convert the known mass → moles.
Use the balanced equation ratio.
Convert required moles → mass.

From masses to balanced equations

Reverse the process

If experimental masses are given, turn each into moles and find the simplest whole-number ratio.

mass ÷ Mr → moles → simplest ratio → equation

Example: sodium nitrate

Limiting reactants

The limiting reactant is used up first, so it fixes the maximum amount of product. Another reactant may be in excess.

Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂

0.20 mol Mg needs 0.40 mol HCl.

If you only have 0.20 mol HCl, HCl runs out first.

HCl = limiting
Mg = excess

Common trap: the limiting reactant is not simply the one with the smallest mass or fewest moles. Compare the amounts using the equation ratio.

C4.4

Expressing concentrations

What concentration means

How much solute is present in a given volume of solution.

concentration (g/dm³) = mass of solute (g) ÷ volume of solution (dm³)

Volume conversion

1 dm³ = 1000 cm³

cm³ → dm³: divide by 1000.
dm³ → cm³: multiply by 1000.

Do this conversion before using a g/dm³ formula.

Rearrange

mass = concentration × volume

200 g/dm³ × 0.700 dm³ = 140 g.

Higher reasoning

At the same volume, more solute → higher concentration.

For the same mass of solute, smaller solution volume → higher concentration.

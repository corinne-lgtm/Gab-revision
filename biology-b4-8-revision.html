B4.8 Evaporation and transpiration

What transpiration is, and the role of stomata and guard cells in controlling gas exchange and water loss.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from leaves through stomata — this drives a constant transpiration stream pulling water up from the roots.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • What transpiration is
  • The role of stomata and guard cells in controlling gas exchange and water loss

Stomata and guard cells

🔵

Stomata (singular: stoma)

Small pores (openings) found all over the leaf surface — mostly on the lower epidermis. They allow:

  • CO₂ in (for photosynthesis)
  • O₂ out (product of photosynthesis)
  • Water vapour out (transpiration)
🟢

Guard cells

Specialised cells that control the opening and closing of each stoma:

  • Open when the plant needs CO₂ for photosynthesis (daytime) or has plenty of water
  • Close to limit water loss when the plant is short of water or in hot/dry conditions

Guard cells change shape by taking in or losing water — when full of water (turgid) they bow outward, opening the stoma.

Transpiration

Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from the surface of plant leaves. When stomata are open:

  • Water vapour evaporates from cells lining the air spaces inside the leaf
  • Water vapour passes out through the stomata by diffusion (down a concentration gradient — moist inside, drier outside)

Most water vapour is lost from leaves through stomata when they are open. This is one reason plants close stomata — to limit water loss.

The transpiration stream

☁️ Water vapour leaves through open stomata by diffusion
⬆️
🍃 Water evaporates from cells lining air spaces in leaf
⬆️
🌿 Water moves up through xylem in stem
⬆️
🌱 Water moves from roots into xylem
⬆️
🌍 Water absorbed from soil into roots by osmosis
💡 The transpiration stream is driven by evaporation from the leaves. As water is lost from the top, more is pulled up behind it — like water moving up a drinking straw. Anything that affects the rate of evaporation affects transpiration rate.

Maths: calculating mean stomata count

Finding the mean: Add all values and divide by the number of samples.
Example: counts were 10, 12, 15, 8, 10 Mean = (10 + 12 + 15 + 8 + 10) ÷ 5 = 55 ÷ 5 = 11 stomata per 0.1 mm²

Estimating total stomata on a leaf:

If mean = 11 per 0.1 mm² → per 1 mm² = 11 × 10 = 110 If leaf area = 5 cm² = 500 mm² → total = 110 × 500 = 55,000 stomata

Give answers to 3 significant figures when asked.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • a) What are stomata? [1] b) Describe their role in the plant [2]
  • Describe the process of transpiration [3 marks]
  • Explain how water moves up a plant in the transpiration stream [3 marks]
  • Calculate the mean number of stomata (to 3 significant figures) [3 marks]

Key for transpiration stream [3 marks]: Water evaporates from leaf surface → passes out of stomata by diffusion → more water drawn up through xylem → water absorbed by roots from soil by osmosis.

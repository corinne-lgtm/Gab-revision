AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

6.1 How did Hitler begin to eliminate his opposition?

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index 📚 Germany index ✏️ Quiz rapide
In 1933–34, Hitler used elections, fear, emergency laws and violence to turn Germany from a democracy into a Nazi dictatorship.
🎯

Objectives

  • Summarise the key events that helped Hitler take complete control.
  • Explain why these events were able to happen.
  • Evaluate the impact on ordinary German people.

The key sequence: February 1933 to August 1934

Feb 1933
New election

Hitler called a March election because he wanted a Nazi majority in the Reichstag. As Chancellor, he had more influence over newspapers and radio. The SA intimidated opponents.

27 Feb 1933
Reichstag Fire

The Reichstag building burned down. A young communist, Marinus van der Lubbe, was blamed. Hitler claimed it was evidence of a communist plot.

28 Feb 1933
Emergency decree

Hindenburg agreed to the Decree for the Protection of the People and State. This removed important civil rights and allowed the Nazis to arrest opponents.

5 Mar 1933
Election

The Nazis won more seats, but still not a full majority. Communist candidates had been banned or arrested, so the election was not fully fair.

23 Mar 1933
Enabling Act

The Enabling Act gave Hitler power to make laws without the Reichstag. This was the most important legal step towards dictatorship.

7 Apr 1933
Control of states

Nazis took control of local government, councils and police. The Gestapo was formed and the first concentration camp opened at Dachau.

2 May 1933
Trade unions banned

Trade unions were closed, their money was taken and leaders were imprisoned. Workers lost an important way to complain about pay and conditions.

14 July 1933
One-party state

All political parties were banned except the Nazi Party. Germany became a one-party state.

2 Aug 1934
Der Führer

After Hindenburg died, Hitler combined the jobs of President and Chancellor. The army swore loyalty to him personally and he took the title Der Führer.

Why the Reichstag Fire helped Hitler

🔥

Fear of communism

Many Germans already feared a communist revolution. Hitler used the fire to make this fear much stronger.

🚨

Emergency powers

The emergency decree allowed the Nazis to ban meetings, shut down newspapers and imprison political opponents without normal legal protections.

🗳️

Election advantage

Communists were banned from taking part in the March election, which made it easier for the Nazis to increase their power.

Why the Enabling Act mattered so much

Before the Act

  • Laws usually needed Reichstag agreement.
  • Hitler still depended on Hindenburg and other politicians.
  • Germany still had some democratic rules.

After the Act

  • Hitler could make laws without asking the Reichstag.
  • He could ignore Hindenburg and Parliament in practice.
  • It became much easier to destroy democracy from within.
Exam point: The Enabling Act is often seen as the key turning point because it made Hitler’s dictatorship look “legal”.

How ordinary people were affected

Political opponents

Communists, Social Democrats and critics of the Nazis were arrested, intimidated or silenced.

Workers

Trade unions were banned, so workers could no longer organise freely to protect pay and conditions.

Everyone else

People lost freedoms such as free speech, free press and the right to support different political parties.

Supporters

Some Germans supported Hitler because they believed he had restored order, ended political chaos and fought communism.

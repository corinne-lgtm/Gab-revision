B3.1 Tissues and organs

How specialised cells become organised into tissues, and how tissues work together to form organs and organ systems.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Cells → Tissues → Organs → Organ systems → Organism. Each level of organisation carries out more complex functions.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How specialised cells become organised into tissues
  • How several different tissues work together to form an organ

The hierarchy of organisation

🔬 Cells — the basic building blocks of all living organisms
⬇️
🧱 Tissues — groups of cells with similar structure and function
⬇️
🫁 Organs — collections of tissues performing a specific function
⬇️
🔄 Organ systems — groups of organs working together
⬇️
🧬 Organism — the whole multicellular living thing

Tissues

A tissue is a group of cells with similar structure and function working together. Examples in animals:

💪

Muscular tissue

Can contract to bring about movement. Found in the stomach wall — churns food and digestive juices together.

🧪

Glandular tissue

Contains secretory cells that produce and release substances such as enzymes and hormones. Found in the stomach — produces digestive juices.

🛡️

Epithelial tissue

Covers the outside of the body and internal organs. Forms a lining/covering layer. Found on the inside and outside of the stomach.

Organs

Organs are collections of tissues, all working together to perform a specific function. Each organ contains several different tissues.

🫃

The stomach

An organ involved in the digestion of food. It contains:

  • Muscular tissue — churns food and digestive juices
  • Glandular tissue — produces digestive juices
  • Epithelial tissue — covers the inside and outside
🫀

The pancreas

An organ with two important functions:

  • Makes hormones (e.g. insulin) to control blood sugar
  • Makes enzymes to digest food

Contains two very different types of tissue producing these different secretions.

🫘

The kidney

An organ that filters the blood and produces urine. Contains several types of tissue including specialised tubule cells, blood vessel tissue, and connective tissue.

Organ systems

Organ systems are groups of organs that all work together to perform specific functions. A whole multicellular organism is made up of a number of organ systems working together.

🍽️

Digestive system

Breaks down food and absorbs nutrients into the blood.

❤️

Circulatory system

Transports substances around the body in the blood.

💨

Gas exchange system

Takes in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide via the lungs.

💡 Organ systems have adaptations to increase effectiveness as exchange surfaces — e.g. large surface area, rich blood supply, short diffusion distances, and mechanisms to increase concentration gradients.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Define the word tissue [1 mark]
  • Define the word organ [1 mark]
  • For sperm / kidney / stomach — state whether each is a specialised cell, tissue, or organ, and explain [2 marks each]
  • Describe how the stomach is adapted for its role in the digestion of food [5 marks]

Key tip: Sperm = specialised cell. Kidney = organ (contains several different tissues). Stomach = organ (muscular + glandular + epithelial tissue).

B17 Biodiversity and ecosystems

Human population growth, pollution, deforestation, peat, global warming and ways of maintaining biodiversity.

B17.1

The human population explosion

Learn

Biodiversity

The variety of species within an ecosystem or across Earth. High biodiversity can improve ecosystem resilience.

Population growth

Improved food supply, healthcare and living standards have reduced death rates and increased human population size.

Land, resources and waste

More people need more food, housing, energy and materials, increasing habitat loss, resource use and waste.

Population pressure

Watch how population growth can increase land use, resource demand and waste if consumption per person stays high.

B17.2

Land and water pollution

Learn

Land pollution

Landfill, toxic chemicals, pesticides and industrial waste can contaminate soils and habitats.

Water pollution

Sewage and fertiliser run-off add nutrients and organic material to waterways.

Bioaccumulation

Persistent toxins can build up in organisms and become more concentrated at higher trophic levels.

Eutrophication
Toxin through a food chain

Higher trophic levels can contain greater toxin concentrations because predators consume many contaminated prey.

B17.3

Air pollution

Learn

Acid rain

Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides dissolve in atmospheric water and form acids.

Smoke pollution

Particulates can damage lungs and reduce light reaching Earth’s surface.

Smog

Smoke particles and acidic gases can combine to create harmful urban air pollution.

From fuel to acid rain
B17.4

Deforestation and peat destruction

Learn

Deforestation

Forests may be cleared for farming, cattle, timber, biofuels, roads and settlements.

Carbon cycle effect

Burning and decay release CO₂, while fewer trees remain to remove CO₂ by photosynthesis.

Peat bogs

Peat stores carbon for long periods because decay is slow in waterlogged acidic conditions.

Clear the forest

As forest cover falls, habitat area and carbon uptake fall while stored carbon can be released.

B17.5

Global warming

Learn

Greenhouse effect

Greenhouse gases absorb outgoing infrared radiation and re-radiate energy, warming Earth’s surface.

Enhanced warming

Rising CO₂ and methane concentrations increase the greenhouse effect.

Biological consequences

Habitats, migration patterns, species distributions and biodiversity can change as climate changes.

Greenhouse gas level

This is a conceptual model: more greenhouse gas means a larger fraction of outgoing infrared energy is retained in the Earth system.

CO₂ trend + seasonal cycle

The long-term baseline rises while annual biological cycles create small oscillations.

B17.6

Maintaining biodiversity

Learn

Species protection

Breeding programmes, reintroduction and avoiding inbreeding can support endangered species.

Habitat protection

Protecting and restoring rare habitats conserves whole communities and ecological interactions.

Reducing human impact

Recycling, reducing deforestation, lowering emissions and restoring field margins can reduce pressure on biodiversity.

AQA GCSE Biology · B1 Cell structure and transport

B1.2 Animal and plant cells

The main parts of animal and plant cells, their functions, and what makes plant cells unique.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
All cells share common features — but plant cells have extra structures for photosynthesis and support.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • Know the main parts of animal cells
  • Know the similarities and differences between plant and animal cells
  • Understand the function of each organelle

Organelles — structure and function

🧬 Nucleus
Animal & Plant

Controls all cell activities. Contains chromosomes made of DNA — the instructions for making proteins. Average diameter ~10 μm.

💧 Cytoplasm
Animal & Plant

Liquid gel where organelles are suspended. Most chemical reactions for life take place here.

🚪 Cell membrane
Animal & Plant

Controls what enters and leaves the cell — glucose, mineral ions, urea, hormones. Selectively permeable.

⚡ Mitochondria
Animal & Plant

Where aerobic respiration takes place, releasing energy. Very small: 1–2 μm long, 0.2–0.7 μm diameter.

🔩 Ribosomes
Animal & Plant

Where protein synthesis takes place. Found in the cytoplasm. Extremely small — only visible with electron microscopes.

🌿 Chloroplasts
Plant only

Found in green parts of plants. Contain chlorophyll which absorbs light for photosynthesis. Size: 3–5 μm. Not in root cells (underground, no light).

🧱 Cell wall
Plant only

Made of cellulose. Strengthens and supports the cell. Also present in algal cells.

💦 Permanent vacuole
Plant only

Filled with cell sap. Keeps cells rigid and helps support the plant. Important for maintaining turgor pressure.

⚠️ Study tip: Not all plant cells have chloroplasts — root cells are underground and don't photosynthesise. Don't confuse chloroplasts (the organelle) with chlorophyll (the green pigment inside them).

Size facts to remember

📏

Animal cells

Average size: 10–30 μm long

Nucleus diameter: ~10 μm

100,000–300,000 could fit along 1 metre ruler

🌱

Plant cells

Larger: 10–100 μm long

Chloroplast: 3–5 μm

Mitochondria: 1–2 μm long

🔬

Algal cells

Simple aquatic organisms. Have a cell wall, nucleus and chloroplasts — very similar to plant cells.

Now classified as protists, not plants.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • List the structures found in animal cells [5 marks]
  • State the THREE extra features found in plant cells but not animal cells [3 marks]
  • Describe the function of each extra structure [3 marks]
  • Suggest why root cells have no chloroplasts [2 marks]
  • Suggest types of plant cells unlikely to have chloroplasts [4 marks]

Top tip: When asked to "suggest" why a cell lacks a structure, think about what that structure does and whether that cell needs it.

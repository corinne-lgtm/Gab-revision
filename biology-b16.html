B16 Organising an ecosystem

Feeding relationships, predator–prey cycles, decomposition, materials cycling, the water cycle and the carbon cycle.

B16.1

Feeding relationships

Learn

Producers

Green plants and algae make biomass by photosynthesis and form the start of food chains.

Consumers

Primary consumers eat producers. Secondary and tertiary consumers eat other animals.

Predators and prey

Predator and prey numbers are linked and often rise and fall in cycles.

Build the feeding level

Follow how biomass and energy move through a food chain.

Predator–prey cycle

Move through time. Prey peaks first; predator numbers follow after a time lag.

B16.2

Materials cycling

Learn

Decay

Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi break down dead organisms and waste material.

Recycling

Decay returns mineral ions to the soil and carbon dioxide to the atmosphere so materials can be reused.

Water cycle

Water continually moves through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, run-off, transpiration and respiration.

The carbon cycle

Learn

Photosynthesis

Removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and stores carbon in plant biomass.

Respiration & decay

Return carbon dioxide to the atmosphere.

Combustion

Burning wood and fossil fuels releases stored carbon as carbon dioxide.

Each step shows the process that moves carbon between stores.

AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

8.1 How did the Nazis change the lives of young people?

How education and youth organisations were used to control, train and indoctrinate young Germans.

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index 📚 Germany index ✏️ Quiz rapide
The Nazis wanted young people to become loyal Nazis. Boys were trained to be future soldiers; girls were trained to be future wives and mothers.

Exam focus

Remember: Nazi policies affected young people both inside school and outside school. A strong answer should mention education, Hitler Youth, League of German Girls, propaganda and resistance.

1. Nazi control of schools

🏫

Schools became political

  • Hitler used schools to teach loyalty to him and the Nazi Party.
  • Students were encouraged to admire Hitler and prepare to serve Germany.
  • Teachers had to join the German Teachers’ League and teach Nazi ideas.
📚

Subjects changed

  • History focused on German military victories and how badly Germany was treated after the First World War.
  • Geography focused on the lands Germany had lost and areas it might conquer.
  • Science and Maths were linked to war, weapons and Nazi ideas.
🧬

Race Studies and eugenics

  • Race Studies taught racist Nazi ideas about “superior” and “inferior” people.
  • Students were taught antisemitic ideas about Jewish people and other groups.
  • Eugenics meant the idea that the Nazis could “improve” the population by controlling who could have children. This was racist and unscientific.

2. Universities and older students

Universities lost freedom

  • Lecturers were forced to accept Nazi ideas.
  • Some lecturers were sacked for racial or political reasons.
  • Courses were changed to fit Nazi beliefs; for example, Einstein’s theory was removed from Physics courses because Einstein was Jewish.

Education standards fell

  • Many clever academics left Germany, including Albert Einstein.
  • By 1939, about 3,000 university teachers had been dismissed.
  • University became less about independent thinking and more about obedience to Nazism.

3. Controlling young people outside school

🚩

Hitler Youth

  • For boys, youth groups prepared them for future roles as soldiers.
  • Activities included marching, camping, sports, military drill, map-reading, shooting practice and learning to obey orders.
  • Membership became compulsory in 1939.
👧

League of German Girls

  • Girls were trained for the Nazi ideal of becoming wives and mothers.
  • They learned domestic skills such as cooking, sewing and caring for babies.
  • They also did sport and camping to make them physically healthy.
📈

Membership grew quickly

  • In 1933, Hitler Youth membership was about 2.3 million.
  • By 1936, it was over 5.4 million.
  • By 1939, it reached about 7.3 million as membership became compulsory.

4. Did all young people support Nazi youth groups?

Some young people liked them

  • Many enjoyed the outdoor activities, camping, sports, uniforms and sense of belonging.
  • Some liked feeling important and serving Germany.
  • Young people were surrounded by Nazi propaganda, so many accepted the ideas they were taught.

Some young people resisted

  • Some found the Hitler Youth boring, strict and too militaristic.
  • Some listened to banned music such as American jazz.
  • Opposition groups included the Swing Youth and Edelweiss Pirates, who rejected Nazi control and sometimes mocked or attacked Hitler Youth members.

5. Key points to remember

SchoolsNazi ideas were taught through lessons and textbooks.
BoysTrained for discipline, obedience and future military service.
GirlsTrained for motherhood, domestic work and racial duty.
ResistanceNot all young people accepted Nazi control.

Best exam sentence: Nazi control of young people was important because it aimed to create a future generation that would obey Hitler, accept Nazi racist beliefs and support the dictatorship.

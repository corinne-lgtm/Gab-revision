💡 The Big Ideas

📏Gradient of a d–t graph = Speed
📐Gradient of a v–t graph = Acceleration
⬛Area under a v–t graph = Distance travelled
🖊️Tangent to curve = Instantaneous speed

📏 Distance–Time Graphs

Key Rule
For an object moving at constant speed, the d–t graph is a straight line sloping upwards. The gradient = speed.
Speed from gradient
speed = height of triangle ÷ base of triangle
= change in distance (m) ÷ change in time (s)
✅ Constant speed
Straight line → gradient is the same everywhere → speed is constant.
⚠️ Changing speed
Curved line → gradient changes → draw a tangent to find the speed at a specific instant.

The Tangent Method (changing speed)

A tangent is a straight line that touches the curve at one point without crossing it. Draw it at the time you care about, then calculate its gradient — that gradient is the instantaneous speed at that moment.

📌 Example (Figure 2)
Tangent drawn at t = 12 s gives height = 130 m, base = 11 s → speed ≈ 11.8 m/s

📐 Velocity–Time Graphs

Key Rule
The gradient of a v–t graph gives the acceleration. The area under a v–t graph gives the distance travelled.
Acceleration from gradient
acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time taken
Units: m/s²  |  a = (v − u) / t
✅ Constant acceleration
Straight line on v–t graph → gradient is constant → acceleration is constant.

Finding Distance from a v–t Graph

Calculate the area under the line (between the line and the time axis). Split complex shapes into triangles and rectangles and add them up.

📌 Example (Figure 3 — Higher)
Shape = triangle (top) + rectangle (bottom). Each represents 40 m → total distance = 80 m.
⚠️ Study Tip
Always check: is it a distance–time or velocity–time graph? The same gradient rule gives completely different quantities on each!

Velocity–Time Graph: Constant Acceleration

🧮 Worked Example: Finding Acceleration

From Figure 3: a velocity–time graph with u = 4 m/s and v = 12 m/s over t = 10 s.

Step-by-step
Height of triangle = 12 − 4 = 8 m/s (change in velocity)
Base of triangle = 10 s (time taken)
Acceleration = 8 ÷ 10 = 0.8 m/s²
✅ Check
The line is straight → constant acceleration confirmed. Units are m/s² ✓

⚠️ Higher: The v² = u² + 2as Equation

💡 Where it comes from
Derived by combining a = (v − u)/t and s = ½(u + v) × t. Rearranging gives v² − u² = 2as.
The Equation
v² = u² + 2as
v = final velocity (m/s)  ·  u = initial velocity (m/s)
a = acceleration (m/s²)  ·  s = distance (m)
⚠️ When to use it
Use when you are not given time t but you know u, v, a or s. You do NOT need to prove this derivation in an exam.
📌 Example
A motorcycle accelerates from rest (u = 0) at a = 2 m/s² for s = 1000 m.
v² = 0² + 2 × 2 × 1000 = 4000 → v = √4000 ≈ 63.2 m/s

⚖️ d–t vs v–t: What Does Each Tell You?

📏 Distance–Time Graph

  • Gradient = speed
  • Straight line = constant speed
  • Curved line = changing speed
  • Tangent gradient = instantaneous speed
  • Flat line = object stationary

📐 Velocity–Time Graph

  • Gradient = acceleration
  • Straight line = constant acceleration
  • Flat line = constant velocity (a = 0)
  • Area under line = distance
  • Negative gradient = deceleration

⭐ Key Points to Remember

Exam checklist
The speed of an object at constant speed = gradient of the d–t graph.

The acceleration = gradient of the v–t graph.

(Higher) Distance = area under the v–t graph.

(Higher) Instantaneous speed at any point on a curved d–t graph = gradient of the tangent at that point.

📚 Keywords & Glossary

GradientRise divided by run on a graph — gives speed or acceleration depending on graph type.
TangentA straight line that touches a curve at exactly one point without crossing it.
AccelerationThe rate of change of velocity; gradient of a v–t graph. Units: m/s².
DecelerationNegative acceleration — velocity is decreasing. Negative gradient on a v–t graph.
Instantaneous speedThe speed at a single moment in time, found from the tangent to a d–t curve.
Area under v–t graph(Higher) The total distance travelled by the object.
Initial velocity (u)Velocity at the start of the time period being considered.
Final velocity (v)Velocity at the end of the time period being considered.

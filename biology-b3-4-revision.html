Catalysts and enzymes

⚗️

Catalyst

A catalyst speeds up a chemical reaction without being used up or chemically changed. It can be used over and over again.

🧬

Enzyme

Enzymes are biological catalysts — special protein molecules that speed up reactions in living organisms. Each enzyme interacts with a specific substrate (reactant).

Enzymes are large protein molecules. Long chains of amino acids are folded to form an active site — a uniquely shaped region that can bind to a specific substrate.

🔑

Active site

The active site of an enzyme has a unique shape that matches the shape of its specific substrate molecule — like a lock and key. It is the shape of the active site that allows the enzyme to function.

⚠️ Study tip: It is the shape of the active site that is vital. If the shape changes (e.g. due to heat or pH), the enzyme is denatured and stops working.

The lock and key theory

1

Substrate approaches the enzyme and fits into the active site — like a key into a lock.

2

Enzyme and substrate bind together (enzyme-substrate complex). The reaction takes place rapidly.

3

Products are released from the active site. The enzyme is unchanged and ready to use again.

💡 Enzymes can join small molecules together as well as break large ones apart.

Metabolism

The metabolism of an organism is the sum of all the chemical reactions taking place in its cells at any one time. Enzymes control all these reactions — different enzymes catalyse specific types of reaction.

🏗️

Building large molecules

  • Building starch, glycogen, cellulose from glucose
  • Building lipids from fatty acids and glycerol
  • Building proteins from amino acids
  • Plants: combining CO₂ + water → glucose + nitrate → amino acids
✂️

Changing molecules

  • Converting one simple sugar into another (e.g. glucose → fructose)
  • Converting one amino acid into another
💥

Breaking down large molecules

  • Breaking down carbohydrates, lipids, proteins during digestion
  • Breaking down glucose in cellular respiration for energy
  • Breaking down excess amino acids → urea
  • Breaking down hydrogen peroxide (toxic waste) → O₂ + H₂O

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Define: catalyst / enzyme / active site [1 mark each]
  • State what enzymes are made of [1 mark]
  • Explain in detail how enzymes speed up reactions in the body [5 marks]
  • Give three examples of reactions catalysed by enzymes [3 marks]
  • Explain how enzymes are important in the metabolism of a cell or organism [6 marks]

Key phrase: "Enzymes are biological catalysts that catalyse specific reactions in living organisms due to the shape of their active site — the lock and key theory."

Big Ideas

🧲
Poles & Forces
Every magnet has a north (N) and south (S) pole. Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.
📐
Magnetic Field
The region around a magnet where magnetic materials feel a force. Shown by field lines from N to S.
⚡
Induced Magnetism
Placing an unmagnetised iron object in a magnetic field temporarily makes it a magnet.
🔩
Steel vs Iron
Steel keeps its magnetism (permanent magnets). Iron loses it easily — used for induced/temporary magnets.
Like poles repel. Unlike poles attract.
The force is always attractive between a bar magnet and an unmagnetised magnetic material.

Magnetic Materials

Iron & Steel Iron, steel, cobalt and nickel are all magnetic materials — they can be magnetised.
Permanent Magnets Made of steel because steel does not lose its magnetism easily once magnetised.
Temporary Magnets Iron is used because it loses its magnetism easily — good for electromagnets and induced magnetism.
Non-magnetic Most materials (e.g. plastic, wood, copper, aluminium) cannot be magnetised at all.

Iron vs Steel

Iron

  • Loses magnetism easily
  • Used for induced / temporary magnetism
  • Good for electromagnets

Steel

  • Keeps magnetism once magnetised
  • Used to make permanent magnets
  • Harder to demagnetise

Magnetic Field Lines

Direction Field lines always go from the N-pole to the S-pole outside the magnet.
Concentration Lines closest together = strongest field. The field is strongest at the poles.
Never Cross Magnetic field lines never cross each other.
Plotting Compass Aligns along the field line, pointing away from N-pole and towards S-pole of the magnet.

Induced Magnetism

What it is An unmagnetised magnetic material placed in a magnetic field becomes a magnet itself.
Polarity rule The nearest pole of the induced magnet is always opposite to the pole of the bar magnet — so it is always attracted.
Always attractive The force between a bar magnet and an unmagnetised material is always attraction, whichever end of the bar magnet is closest.
Example An iron rod placed in line with a bar magnet becomes a magnet with opposite polarity at its nearest end.
⭐ Exam Tip
If you see two magnets and they attract, they could be unlike poles or one could be unmagnetised. If they repel, they must both be magnets with like poles — an unmagnetised material never repels.
⚠ Common Mistake
Students often say "the field is strongest in the middle of the magnet" — wrong! The field is strongest at the poles, where the field lines are most concentrated.

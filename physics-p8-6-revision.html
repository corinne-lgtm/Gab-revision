📖 What Is Resolution of Forces?

A single force vector can be broken down (resolved) into two perpendicular components. This is the reverse of combining forces — instead of adding vectors, we split one into two.

⭐ Key Principle

To resolve a force F at angle θ from the horizontal: the horizontal component = F cos θ and the vertical component = F sin θ. The two components together have exactly the same effect as the original force.

The Rectangle Method (OABC)

Draw a rectangle around the force vector OB. The force vector lies along the diagonal. The two sides of the rectangle — OA (parallel to slope) and OC (perpendicular to slope) — are the two resolved components.

✅ The parallel component (down the slope) = the force causing the object to slide. Friction must equal this to keep the object still.
🔶 The perpendicular component (into the slope) = the contact/normal force pressing the object into the surface.

🏔️ Forces on an Inclined Plane

When an object sits on a slope, the weight acts straight down. We resolve it into two components relative to the slope:

Weight parallel to slope = W sin θ  |  Weight perpendicular to slope = W cos θ Normal reaction N = W cos θ

Worked Example — Car on a Hill

A car of weight 12 kN is parked on a road inclined at 10° to the horizontal.

Weight = 12 kN downward
→
Ratio: small side to diagonal = 1 : 6.0
→
Parallel = 12/6 = 2.0 kN
✅ The frictional force from the road acts up the slope and equals the parallel component: 2.0 kN up the slope.
📝 Exam Tip

Always draw a clear diagram first. Label W downward, then draw the rectangle. The diagonal IS the weight vector. The sides ARE the components. Use this ratio method or trigonometry (sin/cos) — both are valid. Show your working clearly for full marks.

⚖️ Equilibrium

Conditions for Equilibrium

An object at rest (or moving at constant velocity) is in equilibrium. Two conditions must be met:

⭐ Key Conditions

1. The resultant force on the object is zero.
2. The forces acting on the object have no overall turning effect (moments balance).

How to Check for Equilibrium

✅ Parallel forces

  • Sum all forces in one direction
  • Sum all forces in the opposite direction
  • If equal → resultant = 0 → equilibrium ✓

🔀 Non-parallel forces

  • Resolve ALL forces along two perpendicular axes
  • Components along each axis must sum to zero
  • Otherwise not in equilibrium ✗
🚨 Common mistake: thinking "the forces look balanced" from the diagram. Always resolve — non-parallel forces can still produce a resultant if components don't cancel!

Example — Aircraft in Level Flight

An aircraft travels east at constant speed. A wind force of 800 N acts at 30° to the line of flight.

Resolve 800 N at 30°
→
Along flight = 800 cos 30° ≈ 693 N
→
Perpendicular = 800 sin 30° = 400 N

The component along the direction of motion = 800 cos 30° ≈ 693 N.

🔑 Key Points Summary
📌 Resolution

Resolving a force means finding perpendicular components whose resultant equals the original force.

📌 Two Components

To resolve in two perpendicular directions, draw a rectangle with adjacent sides along those directions — the diagonal represents the force vector.

📌 Equilibrium Rule

For equilibrium: resultant force = zero. An object at rest is in equilibrium because the resultant force on it is zero.

⚠️ Watch Out!
⚠️

On a slope, the angle θ in the formula (W sin θ, W cos θ) is the angle between the slope and the horizontal — not the angle from vertical. Sketch the right-angle triangle carefully to avoid confusion.

⚠️

The normal reaction is always perpendicular to the surface (not vertical), so on a slope N = W cos θ, not W.

🧮 Quick Practice

Try These Before the Quiz

1. A trolley of weight 510 N is pushed up a slope at 15°.

  • a) Component of weight down the slope = 510 × sin 15° = 132 N
  • b) The student's push is greater because they also have to overcome friction.

2. A box of weight 50 N sits on a slope at 30°.

  • a) Parallel = 50 sin 30° = 25 N down slope; Perpendicular = 50 cos 30° ≈ 43.3 N into slope
  • b) Friction = 25 N up the slope (if stationary).
💡 Remember

A ladder against a wall slides if friction is insufficient to provide a force equal to the parallel component of weight. Too slippery = friction force < parallel component of weight.

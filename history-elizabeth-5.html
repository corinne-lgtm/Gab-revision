5.1
1577–1580

The Americas and Drake's circumnavigation

Drake's voyage combined exploration, privateering, trade and conflict with Spain. It made him — and Elizabeth — extremely wealthy and gave England valuable knowledge of global sea routes.

Before the voyage

Drake left Plymouth in 1577 with five ships. Publicly the expedition could be presented as exploration and trade, but Drake also intended to attack Spanish shipping and ports. Wealthy backers, including the Queen, invested secretly because open support risked worsening relations with Spain.

Why it mattered

Drake returned in 1580 after sailing around the globe. He brought treasure and spices, improved English knowledge of navigation and showed that English sailors could challenge Spanish power overseas.

5.2A
Voyages and trade

Did voyages abroad make England rich and powerful?

Elizabethan voyages opened new trading possibilities. They also involved piracy, violence and the beginnings of England's participation in the transatlantic slave trade.

Francis Drake

Privateer, explorer and naval commander. His circumnavigation brought treasure and prestige and helped develop English maritime knowledge.

John Hawkins

A leading sailor and trader who helped develop English long-distance trade and naval organisation. He also participated directly in the trafficking and sale of enslaved Africans.

Trade with Asia

English merchants sought direct routes to Asian markets for valuable goods such as spices and silk. The Muscovy Company traded through Russia, while later ventures looked towards the East Indies.

Human cost: Hawkins' voyages were part of the early English transatlantic slave trade. Africans were captured or purchased through coercive trading networks, transported across the Atlantic and sold into slavery. This must be understood as exploitation, not simply as a source of English profit.

5.2B
Raleigh and the New World

Exploration, colonisation and power

English voyages increasingly aimed not only to trade or raid, but also to establish colonies and extend English influence overseas.

Walter Raleigh

Raleigh was a courtier and favourite of Elizabeth. He supported voyages to North America and was granted permission to explore and colonise land claimed for England. The Roanoke colony was established in 1585, but it did not survive.

Why colonies?

Supporters hoped colonies would create bases for trade, provide raw materials, challenge Spain and extend English influence. Early settlements were difficult to supply and depended heavily on relations with Indigenous peoples.

Consequences for Indigenous peoples

European expansion brought competition for land and resources, violence, disruption and disease. English accounts often described America from a coloniser's viewpoint, so historians must question whose experiences are being represented.

Build the argument: rich and powerful?

AQA focus: the current specification explicitly requires Hawkins and Drake; circumnavigation 1577–1580, voyages and trade; and the role of Raleigh. Learn the links between individuals, money, trade, sea power and colonisation — not isolated biographies.

1 · Explain two consequences of voyages of discovery for Elizabethan England.

8 marks

How to build a strong consequence answer

  • Choose two clearly different consequences.
  • For each, start with the consequence and then explain how the voyage produced it.
  • Use precise evidence: Drake's treasure, Hawkins and the slave trade, new trading opportunities or increased sea power.
  • Do not just narrate a voyage.
Useful structure: “One consequence was… This happened because… As a result…”
Revision marking points

Possible consequences include increased royal and private wealth from captured treasure and trade; greater English maritime experience and confidence; worsening relations with Spain; expansion of the English slave trade; and growing interest in colonies and overseas markets.

2 · Explain what was important about Drake's circumnavigation of 1577–1580.

8 marks

Importance means: what difference did it make?

  • Use at least two areas of importance, such as wealth and maritime power.
  • Support each with a specific detail from the voyage.
  • Keep linking back to England, not just Drake personally.
Revision marking points — original revision question

Useful knowledge includes captured Spanish treasure, profits for investors including Elizabeth, improved knowledge of global navigation and trade routes, Drake's prestige and knighthood, and the demonstration that English ships could operate globally and challenge Spain.

3 · How convincing is the view that wealth was the main reason Elizabethans supported overseas voyages?

8 marks

Test the claim rather than agreeing immediately

  • First explain strong evidence for profit: treasure, spices, trade and investment.
  • Then test other motives: rivalry with Spain, Protestant/strategic interests, exploration and colonisation.
  • Finish with a supported judgement about which motive mattered most.
Revision marking points — original revision question

Profit was a powerful motive, but voyages could also weaken Spain, extend English influence, open new routes and markets and support attempts at colonisation.

4 · Explain the importance of Walter Raleigh in Elizabethan overseas expansion.

8 marks

Keep Raleigh connected to wider developments

  • Identify what Raleigh actually did: patronage and support for North American colonisation.
  • Explain why attempted colonies mattered even when they failed.
  • Connect Raleigh to English ambitions for trade, territory and rivalry with Spain.
Revision marking points — original revision question

Raleigh promoted and financed attempts to establish an English presence in North America. Roanoke failed, but the ventures reflected and encouraged English ambitions for colonies, trade and strategic competition overseas.

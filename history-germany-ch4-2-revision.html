AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

4.2 The growth of the Nazi Party

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index 📚 Germany index ✏️ Quiz rapide
Between 1928 and 1932, the Nazi Party grew from a small party into Germany’s largest party. This happened because several problems came together at the same time.
🎯

Objectives

  • Explain how the Nazi Party grew in popularity from 1928 to 1932.
  • Summarise the key factors behind Nazi support.
  • Understand the role of the Depression, Weimar weakness, Hitler’s appeal, fear of communism and propaganda.

1. The Depression helped the Nazis

📉

Economic crisis

The Depression caused unemployment, poverty and business collapse. By 1932, around 6 million Germans were unemployed.

🍞

Desperate voters

Many Germans could not afford food, rent or heating. People wanted quick solutions and began to listen to parties with extreme promises.

🗳️

Nazi votes increased

The Nazis promised jobs, bread, national pride and strong leadership. Their vote rose sharply, especially after 1930.

Key idea: The Nazis did not create the Depression, but they used it brilliantly. They blamed the Treaty of Versailles, Weimar politicians, communists and Jews, then promised to make Germany strong again.

2. Many Germans were unhappy with Weimar government

🏛️

Weak politicians

Weimar politicians argued and struggled to agree on how to deal with unemployment and poverty.

📜

Emergency rule

Governments increasingly used Article 48, which allowed the President to pass emergency laws. This made democracy look weak.

😡

Loss of trust

Many people felt the democratic system was not working. Some moved towards more extreme parties on the left and right.

3. Hitler’s appeal

🎤

A powerful speaker

Hitler gave emotional speeches that made him seem confident, strong and full of hope. He promised work, national pride and a better future.

💪

Strong leadership

Many Germans were tired of weak coalitions and wanted a leader who seemed decisive. Hitler presented himself as the man who could restore order.

Exam point: Hitler’s appeal mattered because he gave people simple answers during a frightening crisis. He did not just talk about politics; he made people feel hopeful and angry at the same time.

4. Fear of communism

🚩

Communist threat

Many middle-class and upper-class Germans feared the Communists because of the Russian Revolution. They worried communists would take property, businesses, farms and churches.

🛡️

Nazis as protection

The Nazis promised to stop communism. This attracted people such as business owners, farmers, churchgoers and some conservative voters.

👥

SA intimidation

The SA protected Nazi meetings, disrupted opponents and made the Nazis look organised and powerful.

5. Nazi organisation, propaganda and tactics

🏢

Party offices

The Nazis set up offices across Germany and recruited more followers. Membership rose from about 100,000 in 1928 to around 400,000 by 1932.

📢

Propaganda

Goebbels used posters, newspapers, leaflets, radio, cinema newsreels and rallies to spread simple messages to millions of people.

✈️

Modern campaigning

In 1932 Hitler used aeroplanes to visit many cities in a few days. This made the Nazis look modern, energetic and connected to ordinary people.

6. Election growth: what happened?

1928
Small party

Nazis won only 12 Reichstag seats.

1930
Big rise

Nazis won 107 seats as the Depression deepened.

July 1932
Largest party

Nazis won 230 seats and became the biggest party.

Nov 1932
Still strong

Nazi seats fell to 196, but they remained the largest party.

Remember: No single factor explains Nazi growth. The best answer links several reasons together.

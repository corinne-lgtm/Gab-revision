AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

8.4 Nazi ideas and racial policy

Revision notes designed to make sense without needing the textbook.

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index 📚 Germany index ✏️ Quiz rapide
Nazi racial policy was based on the false belief that some races were superior and others were inferior. This led to discrimination, persecution and murder.
🎯

Objectives

  • Explain what Nazis meant by “Aryan” ideas.
  • Describe how minority and racial groups were persecuted.
  • Outline why the Nazis persecuted different groups.

1. Nazi racism and hatred

The “master race” idea

The Nazis claimed that Germans belonged to a superior “Aryan” race and that they were the rightful rulers of Europe.

“Inferior” groups

Nazis described Jewish people, Roma and Sinti people, Slavs and black people as “inferior”. These ideas were racist and completely false.

“Pure” Germany

Hitler wanted German society to be “pure” and not mix with people he labelled “weak” or “inferior”. This became a reason for persecution.

2. The persecution of Jews

Hatred from the beginning

Hitler had an obsessive hatred of Jewish people. As soon as the Nazis came to power, they began to discriminate against Jews.

Forced out of normal life

Jewish people were removed from jobs, schools, public places and businesses. Their rights were gradually taken away.

Ghettos, camps and murder

When war began in 1939, persecution became worse. Jewish people were forced into ghettos, sent to camps, worked to death or murdered by mobile killing squads and poison gas.

3. Timeline of anti-Jewish laws and persecution

Mar 1933
Jobs

All Jewish lawyers and judges were sacked.

Apr 1933
Schools & sport

Jews were banned from sports clubs. Jewish teachers were sacked.

Sep 1933
Education

“Race studies” were introduced in German schools.

Sep 1935
Nuremberg Laws

Jews lost German citizenship, could not vote, and marriage between Jews and non-Jews was banned.

Nov 1938
Kristallnacht

Jewish homes, synagogues and businesses were attacked. Around 100 Jews were killed and 20,000 were sent to concentration camps.

4. More restrictions before war

1934

Jewish shops were marked with a yellow Star of David as a warning to others not to shop there.

1936

Jews were not allowed to own electrical equipment such as cameras, bicycles, typewriters or music records.

1938–1939

Jewish doctors were sacked. Jewish children were banned from German schools and playing with non-Jewish children. Jews could be evicted for no reason and later had curfew restrictions.

5. Persecution of “undesirables”

Who did the Nazis target?

The Nazis also persecuted groups they claimed did not fit their vision of a perfect Aryan Germany: Roma and Sinti people, gay people, criminals, prostitutes, Jehovah’s Witnesses, political opponents, pacifists and homeless people.

Why did they target them?

The Nazis believed these groups were “inferior” or a threat to Germany. This was linked to Nazi racism, eugenics and the desire to control society.

Disability and euthanasia

People with physical or learning disabilities were targeted because Nazis thought they damaged the “purity” of the German race. Around 400,000 people with hereditary illnesses were sterilised, and over 200,000 people with disabilities were killed.

6. What happened in concentration camps?

Conditions

A 1939 British report described thousands of men in German camps, including Jews, Bible Students, criminals and political prisoners. Deaths took place daily.

Purpose

Camps were used to imprison, terrorise and remove people the Nazis saw as enemies or “undesirable”. Later, some camps became places of mass murder.

B4.6 Tissues and organs in plants

How the roots, stem, and leaves form a plant organ system for the transport of substances around the plant.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Plants have specialised tissues arranged into organs — leaves, stems, and roots — that form an organ system for transporting substances throughout the whole plant.
🎯

Learning objective

Plant tissues and their functions

TissueWhere foundFunctionAdaptations
🛡️ Epidermal tissue Upper and lower surface of leaf, outer stem and root Covers and protects the surface of the plant Often secretes a waxy substance (cuticle) that waterproofs the leaf surface and reduces water loss
🟢 Palisade mesophyll Upper part of leaf (under upper epidermis) Main site of photosynthesis Cells packed with chloroplasts; positioned at the top of the leaf to receive maximum light
🫧 Spongy mesophyll Lower part of leaf (above lower epidermis) Photosynthesis + gas exchange Some chloroplasts; large air spaces and large surface area to make diffusion of gases easier. Connected to stomata.
💧 Xylem Vascular bundles in leaf, stem, root Transports water and dissolved mineral ions from roots upwards to leaves Dead hollow tubes — no cell contents to block flow; thick lignified walls for support; continuous column from root to leaf
🍬 Phloem Vascular bundles in leaf, stem, root Transports dissolved food (sugars) from leaves to rest of plant (up and down) Living cells with sieve plates; can transport in both directions; companion cells provide energy for active transport
🌱 Meristem tissue Tips of roots and shoots (growing points) Rapid cell division — produces new cells for growth Cells divide and then differentiate into all other plant cell types needed

Plant organs

🍃

Leaf

Main organ of photosynthesis. Contains palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, epidermal tissue, xylem, phloem, guard cells, and stomata. Large flat surface for maximum light absorption.

🌿

Stem

Supports leaves and flowers. Contains vascular bundles (xylem + phloem) arranged in a ring. Transports water up (xylem) and sugars up and down (phloem).

🌱

Root

Anchors the plant and absorbs water and minerals from the soil. Vascular bundles in the centre. Root hair cells increase surface area for absorption.

💡 Together, roots + stem + leaves form a complete plant organ system for transporting substances. This is one of the largest organ systems in the living world — a giant redwood tree trunk can be over 40 m tall!

How the leaf is adapted as an efficient organ for photosynthesis

☀️

Large flat shape

Provides a large surface area to absorb maximum light for photosynthesis.

🟢

Palisade mesophyll at top

Packed with chloroplasts and positioned at the top of the leaf — closest to the light source.

💨

Air spaces in spongy layer

Large air spaces allow CO₂ and O₂ to diffuse easily to and from cells. Large internal surface area for gas exchange.

🔵

Stomata + guard cells

Stomata (pores) in the lower epidermis allow gases and water vapour to move in and out. Guard cells open and close stomata to control this.

💧

Xylem and phloem

Xylem delivers water (needed for photosynthesis). Phloem carries away the glucose produced by photosynthesis.

🛡️

Thin transparent epidermis + waxy cuticle

The upper epidermis is thin and transparent so light passes through to the palisade cells. The waxy cuticle reduces water loss.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Explain how epidermal tissue protects the surface of the leaf [1 mark]
  • Explain how palisade mesophyll tissue is adapted for photosynthesis [1 mark]
  • Explain how spongy mesophyll tissue is adapted for photosynthesis [1 mark]
  • Explain how the tissues in a leaf are arranged to form an effective organ for photosynthesis [6 marks]

Key for 6-mark question: Work through the leaf top to bottom — transparent epidermis → palisade (chloroplasts, light) → spongy mesophyll (air spaces, CO₂ diffusion) → stomata/guard cells → xylem (water in) → phloem (glucose out).

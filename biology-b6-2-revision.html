B6.2 Antibiotics and painkillers

What medicines are, how some of them work, and the ways in which antibiotics can and cannot be used.

Three types of drugs to know

💊

Painkillers

e.g. aspirin, paracetamol

  • Relieve symptoms (headache, pain, fever)
  • Have no effect on pathogens — do not kill bacteria or viruses
  • You must still wait for your immune system to overcome the pathogen
🧪

Antiseptics & disinfectants

e.g. bleach, alcohol hand gel

  • Kill microorganisms outside the body (on surfaces, skin)
  • Far too poisonous to use inside the body — would kill you and your pathogens at the same time
💉

Antibiotics

e.g. penicillin, amoxicillin

  • Work inside your body to kill bacterial pathogens
  • Damage bacterial cells without harming your own cells
  • Do NOT kill viruses
  • The right antibiotic must be chosen — some are broad-spectrum, others are specific
⚠️ Don't confuse: Antiseptics kill microorganisms in the environment. Antibiotics kill bacteria in the body. Antibodies are made by white blood cells to destroy pathogens (both bacteria and viruses).

Why antibiotics cannot kill viruses

🔴

Antibiotics work by damaging bacterial cell structures — e.g. cell walls, which human cells don't have. Viruses are completely different — they don't have cell walls or the structures that antibiotics target. Viruses also reproduce inside your own cells, so any drug targeting them risks damaging your own cells too. This is why it is extremely difficult to develop antiviral drugs.

Antibiotic resistance — a major problem

⚠️

Strains of bacteria are evolving resistance to antibiotics. The antibiotics used to kill a particular bacterium no longer have an effect. Some bacteria are now resistant to ALL known antibiotics.

Unless scientists discover new antibiotics soon, we may no longer be able to cure bacterial diseases — many millions of people could die from diseases we can currently cure.

It is important to use the specific antibiotic for each bacterium — using the wrong one is ineffective and contributes to resistance developing.

Impact of antibiotics — historical data

Antibiotics first became widely available in the 1940s. The number of women dying from infections after childbirth (maternal septicaemia) dropped dramatically. This shows the enormous impact antibiotics had on communicable disease deaths.

If antibiotics had been available earlier, millions of lives could have been saved.

AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

8.5 The journey to the ‘Final Solution’

How and why Nazi persecution of Jewish people worsened between 1933 and 1945.

🏠 Main hub📚 History indexRevisionQuestions
The Nazis moved from persecution and exclusion to mass murder. This process became known as the Holocaust.

Objectives

  • Investigate how persecution of Jews worsened between 1933 and 1945.
  • Explain why Hitler persecuted Jewish people.
  • Describe the reasons for, and methods of, the ‘Final Solution’.
  • Understand that Jewish people resisted Nazi persecution in different ways.

1. Persecution before the war

🚫

Long-term antisemitism

Antisemitism means hatred or prejudice against Jewish people. It existed in Europe for centuries. Jews were often treated as outsiders and falsely blamed for problems.

📰

Nazi propaganda

After Hitler came to power in 1933, Nazi propaganda blamed Jewish people for Germany’s problems and presented them as enemies of the German nation.

⚖️

Laws and exclusion

Jewish people were pushed out of jobs, schools and public life. The Nuremberg Laws of 1935 removed citizenship rights and banned marriage between Jews and non-Jews.

🔥

Kristallnacht, 1938

On Kristallnacht, Jewish shops, homes and synagogues were attacked. Around 100 Jews were killed, thousands were sent to concentration camps, and Jewish businesses were destroyed.

2. War made persecution worse

Conquest and ghettos

When Germany invaded Poland and other countries, millions more Jewish people came under Nazi control. Many were forced into ghettos: crowded, sealed areas of cities with terrible conditions, hunger and disease.

Einsatzgruppen

Special killing squads called Einsatzgruppen followed the German army into eastern Europe. They murdered Jewish people and other groups in mass shootings.

Key idea: the war gave the Nazis control over many more Jewish people and made violence easier to hide from the wider German public.

3. The ‘Final Solution’

🚂

Wannsee Conference, 1942

Nazi leaders met at Wannsee in January 1942. They discussed how to organise the mass murder of Europe’s Jewish population. This plan became known as the ‘Final Solution’.

⛓️

Deportation

Jewish people were transported by train from ghettos and occupied areas to camps. Many were tricked or forced onto trains in horrific conditions.

🏚️

Concentration camps

Some camps imprisoned people and forced them to work in brutal conditions. Prisoners included Jews, Roma and Sinti people, political opponents, disabled people and others persecuted by the Nazis.

☠️

Extermination camps

Extermination camps such as Auschwitz-Birkenau, Treblinka, Belzec, Chelmno, Sobibor and Majdanek were designed for mass murder, mainly using gas chambers.

4. Who was responsible?

Around six million Jewish people were murdered by the Nazis and their collaborators. The SS organised much of the killing, but many ordinary people also helped: railway workers transported victims, police guarded them, companies used forced labour, and some local people collaborated or benefited from stolen property.

Exam point: avoid saying only “Hitler did it”. A strong answer explains the Nazi state, the SS, collaborators, transport workers, companies and local officials all played roles.

5. Jewish resistance

✊

Ghetto resistance

Jewish people resisted by secretly teaching, preserving culture, sharing information, hiding people and smuggling food. Some resisted with weapons, including the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising in 1943.

🕯️

Spiritual and cultural resistance

Resistance did not always mean fighting with weapons. Keeping religion, education, family life, records and hope alive was also a way of resisting Nazi attempts to destroy Jewish life.

C8 Rates and equilibrium

Measure rates, explain them with collision theory, then control reversible reactions and equilibrium. Every major rule is followed by an interactive check.

C8.1

Rate of reaction

Rate = quantity of reactant used or product formed ÷ time. Use mass loss, gas volume, cloudiness, or a graph. At a specific time draw a tangent and calculate its gradient.

Mean rate = quantity ÷ time

Steeper graph = faster reaction

Tangent gradient = rate at a specific time

C8.2

Collision theory and surface area

Particles must collide with enough energy to react. Smaller pieces have a larger surface area : volume ratio, exposing more particles and increasing collision frequency.

Collision is required

Energy must be ≥ activation energy

Larger SA:V → more frequent collisions

C8.3

The effect of temperature

Heating makes particles move faster. They collide more frequently and with more energy. A greater proportion of collisions have energy above the activation energy.

Particles move faster

Collisions become more frequent

More particles have E ≥ activation energy

Why does higher temperature increase rate?

C8.4

The effect of concentration and pressure

Higher concentration puts more particles in the same solution volume. Higher gas pressure pushes gas particles closer together. Both increase collision frequency.

Higher concentration → faster rate

Higher gas pressure → faster rate

Explain both using collision frequency

REQUIRED PRACTICAL 11

Concentration and rate

Gas method: calcium carbonate + hydrochloric acid; vary acid concentration and measure CO₂ volume over time (or time to a fixed volume).

Cloudiness method: sodium thiosulfate + hydrochloric acid; vary sodium thiosulfate concentration and time until the cross disappears.

C8.5

The effect of catalysts

A catalyst increases rate without being chemically changed overall. It provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy and does not change the products or overall energy change.

Alternative pathway

Lower activation energy

Catalyst not used up overall

C8.6

Reversible reactions

In a reversible reaction the products can react to re-form the original reactants. Use the ⇌ symbol.

Forward and reverse reactions are possible

Products can re-form reactants

Use ⇌

A + B ⇌ C + D

C8.7

Energy and reversible reactions

If one direction is exothermic, the reverse direction is endothermic. The amount of energy transferred is equal in size and opposite in direction.

Forward exothermic ↔ reverse endothermic

Same magnitude of energy transfer

Energy direction reverses

C8.8

Dynamic equilibrium

In a closed system, equilibrium is reached when forward and reverse reactions occur at the same rate. They continue: equilibrium is dynamic. Amounts stay constant but need not be equal.

Closed system

Forward rate = reverse rate

Reactions continue at equilibrium

C8.9

Altering conditions · Higher

Le Chatelier’s principle: an equilibrium shifts to oppose a change. Increasing pressure favours fewer gas molecules; increasing temperature favours the endothermic direction.

Concentration: shift to oppose change

Pressure: favour fewer gas molecules

Temperature: favour endothermic direction

Section A · The Kaiser & WWI
1
Describe two features of…
Describe two features of Germany's government under Kaiser Wilhelm II.
4 marks
✏️ How to answer — 4-mark "Describe two features" question
  • Two separate points, each followed by a specific supporting detail.
  • Do not write an introduction. Go straight in: "One feature was… For example…"
  • Each correct feature + detail = 2 marks. Max 4 marks total.
  • Good features: the Kaiser's personal power, the role of the Reichstag, the army's influence, the lack of full democracy.
Aim for 2 clear paragraphs — one per feature. Approx. 50–80 words total.
📋 Mark Scheme — 4 marks
Feature 1 (any): The Kaiser had enormous personal power — he could appoint and dismiss the Chancellor, control foreign policy and command the army without needing Reichstag approval.
Feature 1 (any): Germany had a Reichstag (parliament) elected by men over 25, but it had limited power as the Kaiser could ignore it on key matters such as war and diplomacy.
Feature 2 (any): The army was highly influential in German society — military leaders were respected and Germany spent heavily on rearmament, contributing to the naval race with Britain.
Feature 2 (any): Germany was a federal state made up of 25 different states — Prussia was the largest and most powerful, and its conservative traditions shaped national politics.
2
Describe two features of…
Describe two features of life for Jewish people in Nazi Germany before 1939.
4 marks
✏️ How to answer — 4-mark "Describe two features" question
  • Feature + specific detail twice. Keep the two features clearly separate.
  • Strong answers include specific dates, law names, or events as the "detail."
  • Good features: Nuremberg Laws, loss of citizenship, boycotts, Kristallnacht, exclusion from professions.
Approx. 50–80 words total.
📋 Mark Scheme — 4 marks
Feature 1 (any): The Nuremberg Laws (1935) stripped Jews of German citizenship — they could no longer vote, hold public office or marry non-Jewish Germans.
Feature 1 (any): Jews were excluded from many professions — they were banned from being lawyers, doctors, teachers or civil servants, making it very difficult to earn a living.
Feature 2 (any): Kristallnacht (Night of Broken Glass), November 1938 — Jewish homes, businesses and synagogues were attacked across Germany; around 30,000 Jewish men were arrested and sent to concentration camps.
Feature 2 (any): Jews were required to wear a yellow Star of David from 1941 — before this, anti-Jewish propaganda portrayed them as subhuman enemies of Germany in schools, newspapers and films.
Section B · Source analysis
3
How useful is Source A… (8 marks)
How useful is Source A to a historian studying opposition to Hitler?
Explain your answer, using Source A and your own knowledge of the historical context.
8 marks
Source A — A letter from Sophie Scholl to a friend, written in early 1943, shortly before her arrest "How can we expect righteousness to prevail when there is hardly anyone willing to give themselves up individually to a righteous cause? It is such a splendid sunny day, and I have to go. But how many have to die on the battlefield in these days, how many young promising lives. What does my death matter if by our acts thousands are warned and alerted?"
✏️ How to answer — 8-mark source usefulness question
  • Nature: What type of source is it? (personal letter) — what does this tell us about its reliability and limitations?
  • Origin: Who wrote it, when, and in what circumstances? Sophie Scholl, early 1943, shortly before arrest by the Gestapo.
  • Purpose: Why was it written? A private letter — less likely to be propaganda, but written from a very particular perspective.
  • Content: What does it tell us about opposition? Her motivation, moral/religious basis, awareness of danger, link to war context.
  • Contextual knowledge: White Rose group, leaflets, 1943 arrests, executions. What does the source confirm or not show?
  • Judgement: How useful is it — and for what specifically?
Aim for 3–4 developed paragraphs. Approx. 200–280 words.
📋 Mark Scheme — 8 marks (Levels 1–4)
Level 4 — 7–8 marks
Analyses NOP and content with precise contextual knowledge, and makes a clear overall judgement about what the source is and isn't useful for.
Level 3 — 5–6 marks
Uses at least two of NOP/content/context. Some judgement present.
Level 2 — 3–4 marks
Some valid comment on usefulness but mainly descriptive or relies on content only.
Level 1 — 1–2 marks
Simple statements, little or no analysis or context.
Key points: Origin — private letter by a resister moments before arrest → highly authentic expression of motivation; not written for public consumption so less likely to be performative. Content — reveals moral/religious basis of resistance; awareness that death may follow; links to wartime deaths (WWII context, 1943 turning point, Stalingrad). Context — White Rose group, Hans and Sophie Scholl, leaflets distributed at Munich University, arrested Feb 1943, executed by guillotine. Limitation — shows only one individual's perspective; does not represent the range of opposition groups; cannot show extent or impact of resistance.
Section C · Explanation
4
Explain why… (12 marks)
Explain why the Weimar Republic faced so many problems in the years 1919–1923.
12 marks
✏️ How to answer — 12-mark "Explain why" question
  • Aim for three distinct reasons, each in a separate paragraph.
  • Structure each paragraph: Point → Explanation → Evidence → Link back ("This weakened Weimar because…")
  • The factors should be clearly different from each other — don't write the same point twice with different words.
  • A brief conclusion that connects the factors scores higher than listing them separately.

Think about: the "stab in the back" myth and loss of trust; terms of the Treaty of Versailles; left-wing and right-wing uprisings (Spartacus, Kapp); hyperinflation and the Ruhr crisis; weaknesses built into the Weimar constitution itself.

Aim for 3–4 developed paragraphs + conclusion. Approx. 300–400 words.
📋 Mark Scheme — 12 marks (Levels 1–4)
Level 4 — 10–12 marks
Multiple well-developed reasons with specific evidence and clear causal links. Analytical conclusion that weighs or connects factors.
Level 3 — 7–9 marks
At least two developed reasons with evidence. Some causal language. May lack conclusion or full analysis.
Level 2 — 4–6 marks
Some explanation but reasons may be undeveloped or mainly narrative.
Level 1 — 1–3 marks
Simple general statements with little specific knowledge.
Reasons to reward: Treaty of Versailles — reparations (£6.6bn), war guilt clause, loss of land and colonies → made Weimar associated with humiliation; "November Criminals." Political violence — Spartacus Revolt Jan 1919 (left), Kapp Putsch 1920 (right), Munich Putsch 1923 → showed Weimar could not command loyalty of all. Hyperinflation 1923 — French invasion of Ruhr, Germany printed money → savings wiped out → middle class suffered → lost faith in democracy. Constitutional weaknesses — proportional representation → fragile coalitions; Article 48 emergency powers → could bypass democracy. Legacy of war — soldiers returning to poverty, "stab in the back" myth spread by nationalists undermined legitimacy from the start.
Section D · Extended essay
5
"How far do you agree…" — 16 marks + 4 SPaG
"The persecution of Jewish people in Nazi Germany was caused mainly by Hitler's personal beliefs." How far do you agree with this statement? Explain your answer.
20 marks
✏️ How to answer — 16+4 mark "How far do you agree?" essay
  • Introduction: Give your overall argument straight away. Avoid "In this essay I will…"
  • Agree paragraphs: Develop Hitler's personal antisemitism — Mein Kampf, his speeches, his role in passing the Nuremberg Laws, his approval of Kristallnacht and later the Final Solution.
  • Disagree / other factors paragraphs: Long tradition of antisemitism in Germany and Europe; the role of Goebbels and propaganda; the pressure of war and radicalisation; willing participation of ordinary Germans (Browning / Goldhagen debate); the role of the SS and bureaucracy.
  • Conclusion: Weigh up: was Hitler the unique cause, or did he channel existing attitudes? Use precise vocabulary: antisemitism, ideology, scapegoating, Final Solution, Wannsee Conference.
  • SPaG: Write formally. Avoid contractions. Vary your sentence structure. Spell key terms correctly.

High-level tip: The best answers consider how Hitler's beliefs interacted with other factors — e.g. propaganda made persecution socially acceptable; the war provided cover and opportunity for escalation.

Aim for intro + 4–5 paragraphs + conclusion. Approx. 500–700 words. Check spelling and use subject vocabulary.
📋 Mark Scheme — 16 marks + 4 SPaG
Level 4 — 13–16 marks
Sustained analysis of multiple factors, both agreeing and disagreeing. Hitler's personal role fully developed AND other factors analysed with evidence. Clear substantiated conclusion that weighs factors and reaches a supported judgement.
Level 3 — 9–12 marks
At least two factors developed with evidence. Some analysis and a judgement, but may not fully weigh Hitler's role against other factors.
Level 2 — 5–8 marks
Some developed points but mainly descriptive. Limited range of factors. Weak or absent conclusion.
Level 1 — 1–4 marks
Simple general points. Little specific knowledge. No real judgement.
FOR (Hitler's beliefs): Mein Kampf (1925) set out extreme racial antisemitism; Hitler's speeches portrayed Jews as Germany's enemy; he personally approved Nuremberg Laws 1935; Kristallnacht 1938 ordered from above; Hitler gave verbal authorisation for the Final Solution; without Hitler the Holocaust could not have happened in this form.
AGAINST / other factors: Long tradition of European antisemitism — pogroms, "scientific" racism pre-dated Hitler; widespread German antisemitism meant persecution had popular support. Goebbels' propaganda machine turned hatred into policy with public acceptance. War provided cover — escalation from persecution to genocide 1941–42. "Ordinary men" argument (Browning) — bureaucrats, soldiers, policemen participated without direct orders. Wannsee Conference 1942 — organised the Final Solution through state machinery, not just Hitler's will. Nazi ideology was a party-wide belief system, not just Hitler's alone.
SPaG: 4 = accurate throughout, varied vocabulary, clear paragraphing. 3 = mostly accurate. 2 = some errors, meaning clear. 1 = frequent errors impede meaning.

The Big Idea

When an object falls through a fluid, the frictional force increases with speed. The resultant force gradually decreases until it reaches zero — at that point the object has reached its terminal velocity and no longer accelerates.

Key Formula
W = m × g
W = weight (N)
m = mass (kg)
g = gravitational field strength (N/kg)

Mass

  • The amount of matter in an object
  • Measured in kilograms (kg)
  • Does not change with location
  • Same on Earth and on the Moon

Weight

  • The force due to gravity on an object
  • Measured in Newtons (N)
  • Changes depending on value of g
  • Less on the Moon (smaller g)

Free Fall

  • Only gravity acts → free fall
  • Constant acceleration ≈ 10 m/s²
  • Resultant force = weight
  • Velocity increases steadily

Falling in a Fluid

  • Friction (drag) increases with speed
  • Resultant force = weight − friction
  • Acceleration decreases over time
  • At terminal velocity: resultant = 0

Terminal Velocity

  • Weight = frictional force
  • Resultant force = zero
  • Acceleration = zero
  • Object moves at constant speed

Air vs Liquid

  • Air resistance is much smaller
  • → terminal velocity reached much later
  • In liquid: terminal velocity reached quickly
  • Graph: shallower slope in liquid
💡 Remember: A 1 kg object weighs 9.8 N on Earth. A 5 kg object weighs 49 N. Use W = m × g anywhere in the universe — just change the value of g.
💜 Exam Tip

When asked to explain why an object reaches terminal velocity, always mention two forces and compare them: "The frictional force increases until it equals the weight. The resultant force is therefore zero, so the acceleration is zero and the velocity remains constant."

⚠️ Common Mistake

At terminal velocity, the object does not stop — it continues to move at constant speed! The resultant force is zero but the velocity is not zero. Don't confuse zero acceleration with zero velocity.

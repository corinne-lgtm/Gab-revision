AQA GCSE Biology · B5 Communicable diseases

B5.3 Preventing infections

How the spread of disease can be reduced or prevented.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Disease spread can be prevented by hygiene, isolating the infected, destroying vectors, and vaccination — many of these ideas came from 19th century pioneers.
🎯

Learning objective

  • How the spread of disease can be reduced or prevented

19th century pioneers

1850s
Ignaz Semmelweis — noticed women delivered by medical students (who went straight from dissecting corpses) died far more often than those delivered by midwives. He insisted doctors wash hands before delivering babies → fewer mothers died from childbed fever. Other doctors were very resistant — germ theory was not yet established.
1860s
Louis Pasteur — proved that microorganisms cause disease (germ theory). Developed vaccines against anthrax and rabies.
1860s
Joseph Lister — started using antiseptic chemicals to destroy pathogens before they caused infection in operating theatres — greatly reducing post-surgery deaths.
Later
Improved microscopes allowed pathogens to be seen — convincing people they were really there. Understanding how diseases spread helped us prevent transmission.
⚠️ Why were other doctors resistant to Semmelweis? At the time there was no germ theory — no scientific framework to explain WHY handwashing would help. Doctors considered themselves clean and professional — the idea of being the cause of their patients' deaths was offensive. There was no way to see microorganisms.

Preventing the spread of communicable diseases

🧼

Hygiene

  • Washing hands — after toilet, before cooking, after contact with infected person
  • Disinfectants on kitchen surfaces, toilets
  • Keeping raw meat away from ready-to-eat food
  • Coughing/sneezing into a tissue, then washing hands
  • Hygiene of agricultural machinery (plant diseases)
🏥

Isolating infected individuals

People (or plants) with serious infectious diseases are kept away from healthy people. The fewer contacts, the less likely pathogens are passed on.

For plants: smaller infected plants can be moved and destroyed easily.

🦟

Destroying or controlling vectors

Some diseases are spread by vectors. For example:

  • Mosquitoes carry malaria and dengue fever
  • Houseflies carry 100+ human diseases
  • Rats act as vectors
  • Aphids transmit 150+ plant diseases

Destroying or controlling vectors reduces the spread of the diseases they carry.

💉

Vaccination

A small amount of a harmless form of a pathogen is introduced into the body. The immune system responds and becomes prepared — if the real pathogen is encountered later, the person will not become ill.

Very effective for humans and animals. Cannot protect plants — they have no immune system.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Give three examples of things people can do to reduce spread of pathogens [3 marks]
  • For each example, explain how it helps prevent spread [6 marks]
  • Suggest why other doctors were so resistant to Semmelweis's ideas [6 marks]

Key for Semmelweis resistance [6 marks]: No germ theory yet / could not see microorganisms / no scientific explanation WHY it would work / doctors offended at idea they caused deaths / Semmelweis had no professional authority to enforce change / no controlled trials / ideas challenged the medical establishment.

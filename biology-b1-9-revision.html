AQA GCSE Biology · B1 Cell structure and transport

B1.9 Active transport

How active transport works, why it needs energy, and why it is so important in cells.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Active transport moves substances from a low concentration to a high concentration — against the concentration gradient. It requires energy from respiration.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How active transport works
  • The importance of active transport in cells
  • How it differs from diffusion and osmosis

What is active transport?

⬆️

Against the gradient

Active transport allows cells to move substances from an area of low concentration to an area of high concentration — the opposite direction to diffusion.

This movement is against the concentration gradient.

⚡

Needs energy

Energy is needed to carry a molecule across the membrane and return to its original position. This energy comes from cell respiration.

The rate of active transport is closely linked to the rate of respiration.

🧬

Uses carrier proteins

Special carrier proteins in the cell membrane pick up molecules and use energy to move them across — against the concentration gradient.

Cells with a lot of active transport usually have many mitochondria to supply the energy needed.

⚠️ Study tip: Always say movement against a concentration gradient (from lower to higher) for active transport. Never say "along" — say "down" for diffusion/osmosis and "against" for active transport.

Comparing the three transport methods

Feature Diffusion Osmosis Active transport
What moves? Any soluble particles Water only Ions, sugars, specific molecules
Direction High → Low (down gradient) High water → Low water (down gradient) Low → High (against gradient)
Energy needed? No No Yes — from respiration
Membrane needed? Not always Yes (partially permeable) Yes (with carrier proteins)

Why active transport is important

🌱

Plant root hair cells

Mineral ions (e.g. nitrate) in the soil are usually at very low concentration — lower than in the root hair cells. Plants use active transport to absorb them against the concentration gradient.

This allows healthy growth even from very dilute soil solutions.

🍬

Glucose absorption in the gut

Glucose is needed for respiration. Sometimes the concentration of glucose in the gut is lower than in the blood. Active transport moves glucose from the gut into the blood against the concentration gradient.

🐊

Salt glands in crocodiles

Some marine birds and crocodiles have special salt glands that remove excess salt from the body against the concentration gradient — using active transport.

This allows them to live in salty estuaries and seas.

🫁

Cystic fibrosis link

People with cystic fibrosis have thick, sticky mucus in their lungs, gut and reproductive systems — because an active transport system in mucus-producing cells doesn't work properly.

Mitochondria and active transport

Cells involved in a lot of active transport usually have many mitochondria. This is because:

  • Active transport requires energy from respiration
  • Respiration takes place in the mitochondria
  • More mitochondria = more energy available = faster active transport

Examples: root hair cells in plants, gut lining cells absorbing glucose, kidney tubule cells reabsorbing glucose.

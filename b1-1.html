01 · A tiny history

From light to electrons

Mid-17th century

Light microscopes

Visible light and lenses made cells accessible. Modern school light microscopes are relatively cheap and can be used to view living specimens.

→
1930s onwards

Electron microscopes

Electron microscopes brought much higher magnification and resolving power, revealing finer sub-cellular detail.

02 · Interactive idea

Magnification ≠ resolution

Make the image bigger with one slider. Make nearby details easier to distinguish with the other.

Magnification tells you how many times larger the image is than the real object.

Resolution is the ability to distinguish two close points as separate.

Exam distinction: making a blurry image bigger does not create extra detail. Better resolution does.
03 · Compare

Light vs electron microscope

🔆 Light microscope

Magnification: lower

Resolution: lower

Useful because: relatively cheap and living specimens can be viewed.

⚡ Electron microscope

Magnification: much higher

Resolution: much higher

Result: finer detail and more sub-cellular structures can be seen.

Textbook reference values can be useful for scale, but AQA’s core requirement is the difference in magnification and resolving power rather than memorising every numerical microscope limit.

04 · Zoom tunnel

Get the units under control

1 mmetre
→ ×100
1 cmcentimetre
→ ×10
1 mmmillimetre
→ ×1000
1 μmmicrometre
→ ×1000
1 nmnanometre
Going to a smaller unit makes the number bigger. Example: 0.025 mm = 25 μm.
05 · The maths

One equation, three jobs

MAGNIFICATION
M = I ÷ A

image size ÷ actual size

Worked example

An image is 1 mm long at ×40.

Actual size = image size ÷ magnification

1 mm ÷ 40 = 0.025 mm = 25 μm

Before calculating: make sure image size and actual size use compatible units.
06 · Light microscope practical

What you must be able to do

1. Prepare

Place the specimen on a slide, add a coverslip where appropriate and use a suitable stain if needed.

2. Observe

Start with a low-power objective, focus carefully, then increase magnification if useful.

3. Record

Produce a clear biological drawing, label visible structures and include magnification or a scale.

How to build a GCSE answer: read the command word, identify the science you need, make each sentence earn a mark, and link ideas clearly. The hints below help with structure only — they do not give away the scientific answer.

Q1 · Explain · 3 marks

3 marks

Explain why an electron microscope can reveal more detail in a cell than a light microscope.

Writing guidance — no answer given
  1. Start by identifying the two microscope properties that affect what can be seen.
  2. Use a cause-and-effect sentence: because ___ is greater, the microscope can ___.
  3. Finish by linking the science back to more detail in a cell.

Command word: Explain = give scientific reasons, not just a description.

Revision marking guidance

Look for these scientific ideas in the submitted answer: much higher resolving power; much higher magnification; finer/smaller sub-cellular detail can therefore be distinguished.

This is revision guidance based on the specification. It is not presented as an official AQA mark scheme.

Q2 · Calculate · 2 marks

2 marks

A capillary appears 5 mm wide in an image magnified ×1000. Calculate its actual width in micrometres.

Writing guidance — no answer given
  1. Write the equation you need before inserting numbers.
  2. Substitute the values and show the calculation.
  3. Convert to the requested unit and include the unit in the final answer.

Calculation routine: equation → substitute → calculate → convert → unit.

Revision marking guidance

Expected method: actual size = image size ÷ magnification. Correct result: 5 μm, with working and unit.

Q3 · Compare · 4 marks

4 marks

Compare the use of light microscopes and electron microscopes for studying cells.

Writing guidance — no answer given
  1. Organise the answer as comparisons, not two unrelated lists.
  2. Include at least one point about what each microscope can reveal.
  3. Include at least one practical/use point.
  4. Use linking words such as whereas, in contrast or both.

Command word: Compare = refer to both items and make the relationship explicit.

Revision marking guidance

Useful comparison points include: electron microscopes have much greater magnification and resolving power and reveal finer detail; light microscopes are cheaper and can be used to view living specimens; electron microscopes are expensive and require specialised conditions/preparation.

This is revision marking guidance for this revision question, not an official AQA mark scheme.

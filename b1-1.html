01 · A tiny history

From light to electrons

Mid-17th century

Light microscopes

Visible light and lenses made cells accessible. Modern school light microscopes are relatively cheap and can be used to view living specimens.

→
1930s onwards

Electron microscopes

Electron microscopes brought much higher magnification and resolving power, revealing finer sub-cellular detail.

02 · Interactive idea

Magnification ≠ resolution

Make the image bigger with one slider. Make nearby details easier to distinguish with the other.

Magnification tells you how many times larger the image is than the real object.

Resolution is the ability to distinguish two close points as separate.

Exam distinction: making a blurry image bigger does not create extra detail. Better resolution does.
03 · Compare

Light vs electron microscope

🔆 Light microscope

Magnification: lower

Resolution: lower

Useful because: relatively cheap and living specimens can be viewed.

⚡ Electron microscope

Magnification: much higher

Resolution: much higher

Result: finer detail and more sub-cellular structures can be seen.

Textbook reference values can be useful for scale, but AQA’s core requirement is the difference in magnification and resolving power rather than memorising every numerical microscope limit.

04 · Zoom tunnel

Get the units under control

1 mmetre
→ ×100
1 cmcentimetre
→ ×10
1 mmmillimetre
→ ×1000
1 μmmicrometre
→ ×1000
1 nmnanometre
Going to a smaller unit makes the number bigger. Example: 0.025 mm = 25 μm.
05 · The maths

One equation, three jobs

MAGNIFICATION
M = I ÷ A

image size ÷ actual size

Worked example

An image is 1 mm long at ×40.

Actual size = image size ÷ magnification

1 mm ÷ 40 = 0.025 mm = 25 μm

Before calculating: make sure image size and actual size use compatible units.
06 · Light microscope practical

What you must be able to do

1. Prepare

Place the specimen on a slide, add a coverslip where appropriate and use a suitable stain if needed.

2. Observe

Start with a low-power objective, focus carefully, then increase magnification if useful.

3. Record

Produce a clear biological drawing, label visible structures and include magnification or a scale.

Q1 · Explain · 3 marks

3 marks

Explain why an electron microscope can reveal more detail in a cell than a light microscope.

A strong answer should include:

Electron microscopes have a much higher resolving power and much higher magnification. This allows smaller structures or two very close points to be distinguished, so finer sub-cellular detail can be seen.

This is revision guidance based on the specification, not a claim that this wording is an official AQA mark scheme.

Q2 · Calculate · 2 marks

2 marks

A capillary appears 5 mm wide in an image magnified ×1000. Calculate its actual width in micrometres.

Actual size = 5 mm ÷ 1000 = 0.005 mm = 5 μm.

Q3 · Compare · 4 marks

4 marks

Compare the use of light microscopes and electron microscopes for studying cells.

Useful comparison points include: electron microscopes have much greater magnification and resolving power, so they reveal finer detail; light microscopes are cheaper and can be used to view living specimens. Electron microscopes are large, expensive and require specialised conditions/preparation.

Use comparative language such as “whereas”, “in contrast” and “both”.

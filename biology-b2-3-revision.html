B2.3 Stem cells

How stem cells differ from other body cells, their functions in embryos, adults and plants, and how they may be used to treat disease.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Stem cells are unspecialised cells that can divide and differentiate into many different cell types — making them vital for growth, repair, and potential medical treatments.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How stem cells differ from other body cells
  • Functions of stem cells in embryos, adults and plants
  • How stem cells may treat medical conditions

What makes stem cells special?

🔬

Unspecialised

Stem cells are undifferentiated — they have not yet become a specific cell type. They can divide by mitosis and then differentiate into specialised cells.

♾️

Self-renewing

Stem cells can divide to produce more stem cells, staying in tissues for years — only dividing when cells are injured or diseased and need replacing.

🔄

Pluripotent / multipotent

Embryonic stem cells can form almost any cell type. Adult stem cells are more limited — they form certain types within one tissue (e.g. blood cells from bone marrow).

Three sources of stem cells

🐣

1. Embryonic stem cells

An egg and sperm fuse to form a zygote. This divides to form a hollow ball — the embryo. The inner cells are embryonic stem cells that can differentiate into all the specialised cells of the body.

These are the most flexible but also the most controversial to use.

🦴

2. Adult stem cells

Found in specific tissues of adults — bone marrow is a key source. Adult stem cells are undifferentiated cells that can form certain specific cell types.

Scientists now think there may be tiny numbers of stem cells in most tissues including blood, brain, muscle, and liver.

🌱

3. Plant meristem stem cells

Stem cells found at the meristems (tips of shoots and roots) of plants. They divide almost continuously and can differentiate into all types of plant cells.

Used to clone large numbers of identical plants quickly and economically.

Using stem cells to treat disease

🦿

Paralysis (spinal injuries)

Spinal injuries cause paralysis because spinal nerves cannot repair themselves. Scientists have used nerve cells grown from embryonic stem cells to restore some movement to the legs of paralysed rats. Trials in humans began in 2010.

👁️

Macular degeneration (blindness)

In 2014, doctors transplanted embryonic stem cells into the eyes of people going blind from macular degeneration. All patients could see better. Larger trials are now taking place.

🩸

Diabetes

Scientists are using stem cells to try to grow cells sensitive to blood sugar that produce insulin — potentially treating people with type 1 diabetes who must inject insulin daily.

🏥

Future possibilities

Whole new organs could potentially be grown from embryonic stem cells for use in transplant surgery. Conditions from infertility to dementia may eventually be treated using stem cells.

Stem cells in plants — uses

🌸

Horticulture

Plant stem cells from meristems are used to clone large numbers of identical plants quickly and cheaply. For example, every orchid sold in a shop is produced by cloning from plant stem cells.

🌾

Agriculture

Used to produce large numbers of identical crop plants with special features such as disease resistance. Every banana you eat is a cloned plant.

🦺

Conservation

Plant cloning from stem cells can save rare plants from extinction — large populations of identical plants can be produced for conservation and research.

🧪

Research

Cloning produces genetically identical individuals — scientists can change one variable and observe effects without genetic variation confusing results.

Exam tip

Common questions:

  • Give three sources of stem cells [3 marks]
  • Identify the advantages of using stem cells to treat diseases [4 marks]
  • Explain why the ability to clone large numbers of individual plants is such an advantage in plant research [3 marks]

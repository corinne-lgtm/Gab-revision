B13 Variation and evolution

Genetic and environmental variation, natural selection, selective breeding, genetic engineering and the ethics of new genetic technologies.

B13.1

Variation

Learn

Genetic variation

Inherited differences in genes cause genetic variation. Traits such as blood group are strongly genetic.

Environmental variation

Conditions and experiences can change phenotype. Scars, diet effects and sun exposure are environmental examples.

Combined causes

Many characteristics, such as height and mass, are influenced by both genes and environment.

What causes the difference?

The visual changes to show how phenotype can arise.

B13.2

Evolution by natural selection

Learn

Mutation and variation

Mutations create new alleles. Most have little effect, some are harmful, and a few can provide an advantage in a particular environment.

Natural selection

Individuals with advantageous inherited traits are more likely to survive and reproduce successfully.

Evolution

Over generations, advantageous alleles become more common. Populations change, and sufficiently diverged populations may eventually form new species.

Follow natural selection
B13.3

Selective breeding

Learn

Artificial selection

Humans choose parents with desired characteristics and breed them. The best offspring are selected and bred again over many generations.

Desired traits

Examples include high milk yield, disease resistance, gentle temperament and large flowers.

Limitations

Selective breeding can reduce genetic variation and increase inbreeding, making harmful inherited conditions more common.

Build a selectively bred population

As selection continues, the desired trait becomes more common while variation falls.

B13.4 · Higher tier

Genetic engineering

Learn

Changing genetic material

A desired gene is cut from one organism and inserted into the DNA of another organism.

Vectors

Bacterial plasmids or viruses can act as vectors. Enzymes are used to cut DNA and insert the chosen gene.

Applications

Engineered bacteria can make human insulin. GM crops can be designed for higher yield, pest resistance or improved nutrition.

Transfer a gene
B13.5

Ethics of genetic technologies

Learn

Possible benefits

Genetic engineering may improve medicine, food security, crop nutrition and resistance to disease or harsh conditions.

Possible concerns

Concerns include gene flow to wild populations, uncertain long-term effects and unequal access to the technology.

Ethics

Human genetic modification and embryo selection raise difficult questions about acceptable uses of genetic technologies.

Section A · Weimar Republic
1
Describe two features of…
Describe two features of the Weimar Republic's constitution.
4 marks
✏️ How to answer — 4-mark "Describe two features" question
  • Give Feature 1 in one sentence, then add one supporting detail (a specific fact, name, date or example).
  • Do the same for Feature 2 — totally separate point.
  • Never mix the two features together. Each feature + detail = 2 marks.
  • Do not write an introduction or conclusion — go straight to the points.

Structure: "One feature of [topic] was [point]. For example / This meant that [detail]."

Aim for 2 clear paragraphs — one per feature. Approx. 50–80 words total.
📋 Mark Scheme — 4 marks (2 per feature)
Feature 1 (any): Proportional representation — seats in the Reichstag were awarded according to share of votes, meaning many parties were elected and coalition governments were necessary.
Feature 1 (any): The President had Article 48 emergency powers — he could pass laws without the Reichstag in a crisis, which was later used by Hindenburg to bypass democracy.
Feature 2 (any): A Bill of Rights guaranteed rights such as freedom of speech and the right to vote — all Germans over 20 could vote, including women for the first time.
Feature 2 (any): The Chancellor needed the support of the Reichstag majority to govern — this made it difficult to pass laws if no party had a majority.
2
Describe two features of…
Describe two features of the Nazi police state.
4 marks
✏️ How to answer — 4-mark "Describe two features" question
  • Same structure as Q1: Feature + specific detail, twice.
  • Good features here: the Gestapo, the SS, concentration camps, the courts, informers.
  • The detail should explain how the feature worked or give a specific fact about it.
Aim for 2 clear paragraphs — one per feature. Approx. 50–80 words total.
📋 Mark Scheme — 4 marks (2 per feature)
Feature 1 (any): The Gestapo (secret police) monitored and arrested opponents of the regime — they relied heavily on ordinary Germans reporting their neighbours and colleagues.
Feature 1 (any): The SS (Schutzstaffel) was Hitler's personal bodyguard that grew into a powerful force controlling the concentration camps and enforcing racial policy.
Feature 2 (any): Concentration camps were used to imprison political opponents, Jews, and others — Dachau was the first, opened in 1933.
Feature 2 (any): The Nazi courts were not independent — judges were expected to rule in favour of the Nazi state, so opponents could not rely on fair trials.
Section B · Source analysis
3
How useful is Source A… (8 marks)
How useful is Source A to a historian studying Nazi propaganda?
Explain your answer, using Source A and your own knowledge of the historical context.
8 marks
Source A — A poster produced by the Nazi Ministry of Propaganda, 1936 The poster shows a strong, blond German family standing in a field of wheat. A caption reads: "Blood and Soil — the strength of the German nation." The poster was displayed widely in schools, government buildings and public spaces across Germany.
✏️ How to answer — 8-mark "How useful is the source?" question
  • Nature / Origin / Purpose (NOP): Who made it? When? Why? Does this make it more or less useful?
  • Content: What does it show or say? How does this help a historian?
  • Contextual knowledge: What do you know about this topic that supports or limits the source's usefulness?
  • Limitations: What does it NOT tell us? Is it biased? Is it typical or exceptional?
  • Always reach a judgement: "Overall, this source is useful because… but limited because…"

Avoid: saying a source is "not useful because it is biased" — bias itself can be useful evidence of attitudes.

Aim for 3–4 developed paragraphs. Approx. 200–280 words.
📋 Mark Scheme — 8 marks (Levels 1–4)
Level 4 — 7–8 marks
Developed analysis of usefulness using NOP AND content AND contextual knowledge, with a clear overall judgement about the degree of usefulness.
Level 3 — 5–6 marks
Analyses usefulness using at least two of: NOP, content, context. Some judgement made but may not be fully developed.
Level 2 — 3–4 marks
Some analysis but mostly descriptive. May comment on content or identify bias without explaining why this affects usefulness.
Level 1 — 1–2 marks
Simple statement about the source with little or no explanation. Very limited reference to context.
Key points to reward: NOP — Nazi Ministry of Propaganda, 1936, made to persuade → shows what the regime wanted people to believe (useful for studying propaganda techniques). Content — "Blood and Soil" ideology, racial purity, links to land. Context — Goebbels, Reich Chamber of Culture, media control, Hitler Youth. Limitation — only shows intended message, not public reaction; may not reflect what people actually believed.
Section C · Explanation
4
Explain why… (12 marks)
Explain why the Nazis were able to rise to power between 1929 and 1933.
12 marks
✏️ How to answer — 12-mark "Explain why" question
  • Give at least three separate reasons, each in its own paragraph.
  • Each paragraph: State the reason → Explain how it contributed → Give specific evidence.
  • Use connective language to show causation: "This meant that…", "As a result…", "This led to…"
  • The hints given in the question (if any) are a starting point, not the full answer.
  • A brief conclusion linking the reasons together gains the top marks.

Think about: the Great Depression and unemployment; weaknesses of the Weimar Republic; Nazi propaganda and appeal; role of Hindenburg and von Papen; fear of Communism.

Aim for 3–4 developed paragraphs + a brief conclusion. Approx. 300–400 words.
📋 Mark Scheme — 12 marks (Levels 1–4)
Level 4 — 10–12 marks
Multiple well-developed reasons with specific evidence. Clear causal links between reasons. Analytical conclusion that weighs factors.
Level 3 — 7–9 marks
At least two developed reasons with evidence. Some causal language used. May lack a conclusion or full analysis.
Level 2 — 4–6 marks
Some explanation attempted but reasons may be undeveloped or descriptive. Limited evidence used.
Level 1 — 1–3 marks
Simple general statements. Little or no specific knowledge or explanation of causation.
Reasons to reward: 1929 Wall Street Crash → Depression → 6 million unemployed by 1932 → people lost faith in Weimar. Nazi propaganda (Goebbels) targeted different groups. Hitler's powerful speeches offered simple solutions. Fear of Communist takeover pushed middle classes and businesses to support Nazis. Hindenburg appointed Hitler Chancellor Jan 1933 due to von Papen's political scheming. Weimar system's proportional representation created unstable coalition governments.
Section D · Extended essay
5
"How far do you agree…" — 16 marks + 4 SPaG
"The main reason why ordinary Germans supported Hitler was propaganda." How far do you agree with this statement? Explain your answer.
20 marks
✏️ How to answer — 16+4 mark "How far do you agree?" essay
  • Introduction: State your overall argument in 2–3 sentences. Don't just repeat the question.
  • Paragraphs FOR the statement: Develop the given factor (propaganda) with at least 2 specific examples and explain why it mattered.
  • Paragraphs AGAINST / other factors: Give at least 2 other reasons why Germans supported Hitler (economic recovery, fear, genuine belief in Nazi ideology, tradition). Develop each with evidence.
  • Conclusion: Reach a clear judgement — do you agree, partially agree, or disagree? Explain which factor you think was most important and why.
  • SPaG (4 marks): Write in full sentences. Check spelling, punctuation, and paragraphing. Use subject-specific vocabulary (propaganda, ideology, indoctrination, rearmament…).

High-level tip: The best answers explain how factors are connected — e.g. propaganda worked because it reinforced economic successes people could already see.

Aim for intro + 4–5 developed paragraphs + conclusion. Approx. 500–700 words. Pay attention to spelling and vocabulary.
📋 Mark Scheme — 16 marks + 4 SPaG
Level 4 — 13–16 marks
Sustained analysis of multiple factors. Reaches a clear, substantiated judgement. Propaganda AND other factors fully developed with specific evidence. Links between factors shown.
Level 3 — 9–12 marks
At least two factors developed with evidence. Some analysis and judgement. May not fully balance propaganda against other factors.
Level 2 — 5–8 marks
Some developed points but may be mostly descriptive. Limited range of factors or weak judgement.
Level 1 — 1–4 marks
Simple general points. Little specific knowledge. No real judgement.
FOR propaganda: Goebbels — Ministry of Propaganda 1933; control of press, radio, cinema, art; rallies (Nuremberg); cult of Hitler; targeted different groups with tailored messages.
AGAINST / other factors: Economic recovery — unemployment fell from 6m (1932) to near zero (1938); rearmament created jobs; Strength Through Joy holidays. Fear — Gestapo, SS, concentration camps silenced dissent (some "support" was compliance). Genuine ideology — antisemitism was widespread before Hitler; nationalism after Versailles. Traditional authority — conservatives, Church, army accepted Hitler as a bulwark against Communism.
SPaG Level descriptors: 4 = accurate spelling/punctuation throughout, varied vocabulary, clear paragraphing. 3 = mostly accurate with occasional errors. 2 = some errors but meaning clear. 1 = frequent errors impede meaning.

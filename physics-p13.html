P13.1 · Textbook pages 158–159

Magnetic fields

A magnet has a north pole and a south pole. Like poles repel, unlike poles attract. A plotting compass points along the magnetic field, which is strongest where its field lines are most closely spaced. Outside a bar magnet, field lines point from north to south.

Magnetic rules

North–north and south–south repel. North–south attract. A magnet has both poles.

Field lines

Field lines never cross. Outside the magnet they point from N to S. A compass needle aligns with the field.

Induced magnetism

Magnetic materials such as iron can become temporarily magnetised in a magnetic field; an unmagnetised iron object is attracted to either pole.

Explore a real bar magnet: follow the compass

Try it: choose a position around the magnet. The little compass will turn to show the direction of the field at that point. Notice how the field curves from the north pole towards the south pole.

1. Move the compass

Pick a position. It turns to align with the magnetic field.

2. Trace the lines

Lines travel from the N pole to the S pole outside the magnet.

3. Compare spacing

Closer lines near the poles indicate a stronger magnetic field.

Above the magnet, the compass points roughly towards the S pole.

GCSE takeaway: a field-line arrow shows the direction a north-seeking compass pole would point.

Predict: attract or repel?

Choose the poles facing each other. Predict what will happen before checking.

Choose both poles, then check.

Remember: induced magnetism is different — an initially unmagnetised iron bar is attracted to either pole.

P13.2 · Textbook pages 160–161

Magnetic fields of electric currents

A current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field of concentric circles around it. Reversing the current reverses the field direction. A solenoid (a long coil of insulated wire) produces a strong, almost uniform field inside it. With a soft iron core it forms an electromagnet.

Straight wire

Field lines form circles centred on the wire. Greater current makes a stronger field; the field weakens with distance.

Solenoid

Inside a long solenoid the magnetic field is strong, uniform and parallel to its axis. Outside, the pattern resembles a bar magnet.

Electromagnet

A coil around soft iron creates a strong magnet when current flows; the core loses most of its magnetism when current stops.

Interactive wire: reverse the current

Imagine looking down onto a vertical wire. • means current flows out of the page; × means current flows into the page. Change the current and watch the field arrows reverse.

4 A · out of the page → anticlockwise magnetic field.

GCSE takeaway: reversing current reverses the magnetic field. Increasing current strengthens the field.

Build an electromagnet: lift paper clips

Slide the current and choose a core. This is a qualitative model (not experimental lifting data).

Soft iron core + current = a stronger electromagnet.

Increase current

More current → stronger magnetic field.

Add soft iron

The field becomes much stronger.

Switch off

No current → most magnetism of the soft iron core disappears.

P13.3 · Textbook pages 162–163 · Higher tier

The motor effect

A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences a force unless it is parallel to the field. With a wire perpendicular to the field, the force is F = BIl, where B is magnetic flux density (T), I is current (A) and l is length inside the field (m). Fleming’s left-hand rule finds the force direction.

Making the force stronger

Increase the current, increase magnetic flux density or place a longer section of conductor in the field.

Change direction

Reverse either the current or field and the force reverses. Reverse both and force direction remains the same.

Motor rotation

Opposite sides of a current-carrying coil experience forces in opposite directions. A split-ring commutator reverses current every half-turn to maintain rotation.

Predict the motor-effect force

Field lines travel from N to S, left to right. Current is at right angles to the field. Try reversing the current to see what happens to the force on the wire.

Current out of page + field to the right → upward force.

Fleming’s left-hand rule: First finger = Field (N → S); seCond finger = Current (positive to negative); thuMb = Motion/force.

Motor-force calculator — Higher

Set the magnetic flux density, current and length of wire inside the field. The wire is perpendicular to the field.

F = 0.032 × 2.2 × 0.040 = 0.00282 N

Unit trap: 40 mm = 0.040 m. Always convert millimetres into metres before using F = BIl.

Why does a simple electric motor keep turning?

1. Current enters the coil

The magnetic field exerts forces in opposite directions on opposite sides of the coil.

2. The coil turns

The opposite forces produce a turning effect (torque).

3. Every half-turn

The split-ring commutator reverses the current through the coil, keeping the turning effect in the same direction.

Exam point: the split-ring commutator is essential for continuous rotation in a simple d.c. motor.

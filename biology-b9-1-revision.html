AQA GCSE Biology · B9 Respiration

B9.1 Aerobic respiration

The chemistry of aerobic respiration and why cellular respiration is so important.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Aerobic respiration is an exothermic reaction — glucose + oxygen → CO₂ + water. It happens continuously in every living cell and provides all the energy for life.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • The chemistry of aerobic respiration
  • Why cellular respiration is so important

The respiration equation

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (+ energy transferred to environment)
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O (+ energy transferred to environment)
⚠️ Study tip: Aerobic respiration is an exothermic reaction — it transfers energy TO the environment (opposite of endothermic photosynthesis). More energy is transferred when bonds form in the products than is taken in to break bonds in the reactants.

Where aerobic respiration takes place

🔋

Mitochondria

Most aerobic respiration reactions take place in the mitochondria — tiny rod-shaped organelles in almost all plant and animal cells. They have a folded inner membrane — large surface area for the enzymes involved.

The number of mitochondria in a cell shows how active it is — muscle cells have many; fat cells have very few.

🧫

Cytoplasm

Some reactions of respiration (especially the early stages) take place in the cytoplasm. This is also where enzymes are made. Anaerobic respiration also takes place here.

Why cellular respiration is so important

🧱

Building molecules

Energy is used for synthesis reactions — building up large molecules from smaller ones to make new cell material (e.g. proteins from amino acids, cellulose from glucose).

💪

Muscle contraction (animals)

Energy from respiration makes muscles contract. Muscles are working ALL the time — heart beating, breathing, stomach churning — even when you sleep.

🌡️

Maintaining body temperature

Mammals and birds maintain a constant internal body temperature. On cold days, energy from respiration keeps you warm. On hot days, you sweat and transfer energy to surroundings to keep cool.

🌱

Active transport (plants)

In plants, energy from respiration moves mineral ions (e.g. nitrates) from soil into root hair cells by active transport. Also converts sugars, nitrates, and nutrients into amino acids → proteins.

Energy needs

Average energy needs per day: teenage boys ≈ 11,510 kJ; teenage girls ≈ 8,830 kJ. Boys need more because:

  • Boys are generally larger than girls
  • Boys have more muscle cells — muscle cells have more mitochondria, require more fuel for aerobic respiration

Investigating aerobic respiration

To show that oxygen is taken up and carbon dioxide is released during aerobic respiration:

  • Limewater turns cloudy when CO₂ bubbles through it — the higher the CO₂ concentration, the faster it turns cloudy
  • Measure temperature rise — respiration is exothermic, so the surroundings get warmer
  • Deprive the organism of food or O₂ to observe reduced CO₂ production

For a planned investigation: design controls, measure O₂ consumption and CO₂ production, ensure an ethical approach (using living organisms).

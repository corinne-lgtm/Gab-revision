Food safety and the EHO

Learn → Quiz → Exam Practice. Your quiz score is saved on this device.

Lesson 1 of 6

How food causes ill health

Foodborne bacteria may include Salmonella, Campylobacter, E. coli and Listeria. Risks also come from viruses, allergens, intolerances, physical contaminants and chemicals.

Lesson 2 of 6

Preventing contamination

Keep raw and ready-to-eat foods separate; wash hands; clean and sanitise surfaces; control time and temperature; use safe storage and check suppliers. Allergen cross-contact needs separate precautions.

Lesson 3 of 6

Food safety control points

Identify where hazards can be prevented, eliminated or reduced: delivery checks, chilled storage, cooking, cooling and hot holding. Monitor controls, record results and act if limits are missed.

Lesson 4 of 6

EHO: inspections

An Environmental Health Officer protects public health by inspecting premises, checking hygiene and food handling, storage, cleaning, pest controls and staff practices; inspections can be unannounced.

Lesson 5 of 6

EHO: investigations and enforcement

EHOs investigate complaints and outbreaks; may take samples, photographs and statements, maintain records, advise on improvements and take enforcement action or provide evidence in court where needed.

Lesson 6 of 6

How to earn higher marks

For an explain question, connect responsibility → action → why it protects people. Use a realistic restaurant example, and give distinct, developed points instead of repeating “checks hygiene”.

Exam-style written questions

Write developed points using point → explain → link to the scenario. Model marking points are revealed after you try.

8 marks

Explain the role and responsibilities of an Environmental Health Officer when inspecting and investigating a restaurant.

Develop several distinct responsibilities with purpose: inspect storage and hygiene to check compliance; review practices/training and pest controls to identify risk; collect samples/photos/interviews to investigate complaints or outbreaks; give improvement advice and enforce requirements where necessary to protect public health. Link each point to an outcome.

Use these indicative points for self-assessment, not an automatic mark.

6 marks

Explain how a restaurant can prevent cross-contamination and allergen cross-contact.

Distinguish microbial cross-contamination from allergen cross-contact; use separate equipment/areas, cleaning, hand hygiene, segregated storage, ingredient checks and staff communication, with reasons.

Use these indicative points for self-assessment, not an automatic mark.

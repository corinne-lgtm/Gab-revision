AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

7.1 Work and bread

How did Nazi policies reduce unemployment in Germany?

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index Revision Questions
Hitler promised “work and bread”. Nazi policies reduced official unemployment, but some of the improvement was created by hiding unemployment and preparing Germany for war.
🎯

Objectives

  • Recall the main ways the Nazis reduced unemployment.
  • Explain how successful these policies were.
  • Understand why some unemployment figures were misleading.

Why was unemployment such a big issue?

📉

The problem

When Hitler came to power in 1933, over six million Germans were unemployed. The Nazis had promised voters that they would give Germans jobs and “work and bread”.

📊

Official improvement

Official unemployment fell sharply during the 1930s: from around 6.1 million in 1933 to very low levels by 1939.

🧠

Exam point

You need to explain both sides: the Nazis did create jobs, but they also changed the figures by excluding some groups from the statistics.

Main Nazi methods for reducing unemployment

🛠️

1. National Labour Service (RAD)

  • Men aged 18–25 had to spend six months in the RAD.
  • They did manual work such as digging drainage ditches, planting forests and working on farms.
  • They wore uniforms, lived in camps and received free meals and a small amount of money.
  • This reduced unemployment figures because these men were counted as working.
🛣️

2. Public work schemes

  • The Nazis ordered the building of autobahns, schools, hospitals and other public projects.
  • The autobahn network linked major towns and cities.
  • By 1938, about 3,800 km of autobahn had been built.
  • These schemes created jobs for construction workers and suppliers.
✈️

3. Rearmament

  • Hitler wanted Germany to become a strong military power again.
  • The Nazis built tanks, battleships, aircraft and weapons.
  • This created work in factories and gave big contracts to business owners.
  • It also broke the Treaty of Versailles, which had limited Germany’s armed forces.
🪖

4. Conscription

  • In 1935, Hitler introduced conscription.
  • All men aged 18–25 had to join the armed forces for at least two years.
  • The army grew from around 100,000 to about 1.4 million within five years.
  • This removed many young men from the unemployment figures.

Invisible unemployment

Why the figures looked better than reality

  • Women who gave up work to have a family were not counted as unemployed.
  • Jews and other groups removed from jobs were often not included in the figures.
  • Unmarried men under 25 in the RAD were counted as working.
  • People forced into the army by conscription were no longer counted as unemployed.

Why many Germans still supported the policy

  • Many people genuinely got jobs and wages again.
  • Families had more security than during the Depression.
  • Some Germans believed Hitler had kept his promise to provide “work and bread”.
  • Nazi propaganda made the employment policies look like a major success.

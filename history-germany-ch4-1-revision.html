AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

4.1 How did the Depression affect Germany?

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index 📚 Germany index ✏️ Quiz rapide
The Depression turned Germany from boom to crisis. Unemployment, hunger and anger helped people turn to radical parties, especially the Nazis and Communists.
🎯

Objectives

  • Discuss the causes and effects of the Depression in Germany.
  • Assess the impact of the Depression on Germans.
  • Explain how it affected support for extremist parties.

1. What caused the Depression?

🇺🇸

America had grown rich

After the First World War, America made and sold lots of goods such as cars and fridges. Many people had spare money and invested it in companies.

📉

Wall Street Crash, October 1929

Share prices collapsed. People rushed to sell shares, banks could not get loans repaid, and many Americans were ruined.

🏦

Loans were called back

American banks demanded repayment of the money they had lent to Germany. German banks and businesses struggled immediately.

2. The impact on Germany

1928
Before crash

Unemployment was already over 1 million.

1929
Crash

The Wall Street Crash hit Germany’s recovery.

1930
Worse

Unemployment rose to about 3 million.

1932
Peak crisis

Unemployment reached almost 6 million.

1933
Still severe

Nearly 5 million Germans were unemployed.

🏭

Factories closed

Foreign factories stopped buying German goods, so German factories reduced production or shut down.

🍞

Hunger and poverty

Many people lived on the streets, were unemployed, hungry and angry, and struggled to pay rent.

😡

Loss of trust

Many Germans blamed the Weimar government for failing to solve their problems.

3. Why did support grow for extremist parties?

Depression causes unemployment and hunger
➡️
People lose faith in moderate Weimar politicians
➡️
Radical parties promise clear, simple solutions
⚡

Nazis

Promised to solve unemployment, restore German pride, attack communism, and blame groups such as Jews and Weimar politicians for Germany’s problems.

🚩

Communists

Promised a workers’ revolution and appealed to some workers who felt capitalism had failed them.

4. Why did different people turn to Nazism?

These two examples are based on workers’ memories from the 1930s. You do not need the textbook beside you: the key ideas are explained here.

🍞

Example 1: desperation and hunger

One German worker described thousands of factories closing, hunger becoming part of daily life, and ordinary workers feeling abandoned. He said he had lost everything, so he joined the Nazi Party in 1930 because he hoped it would give him a better future.

What this shows: Some Germans supported the Nazis because the Depression made them desperate. They wanted jobs, food, security and change.
🚩

Example 2: fear of communism

Another worker, who had a more secure railway job, was less focused on hunger. He worried that Germany was being pulled towards communism and class conflict. He was attracted by the Nazi promise of national unity and a “community” without class struggle.

What this shows: Some Germans supported the Nazis because they feared communism and wanted order, unity and strong leadership.
Important: The same crisis could affect people differently. One person might support the Nazis because they were starving and unemployed; another might support them because they feared communism or wanted stronger leadership.

B4.3 The heart

The structure and functions of the heart, and ways of solving problems with blood supply and valves.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
The heart is a double pump — the right side sends blood to the lungs, the left pumps it around the whole body. Four chambers, four valves.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • Structure and functions of the heart
  • Ways of solving problems with blood supply and valves
❤️ ~70 beats/min. Walls almost entirely muscle, supplied by coronary arteries.

The four chambers

🔵

Right atrium

Receives deoxygenated blood from the body via the vena cava. Contracts → pushes blood into right ventricle.

🔷

Right ventricle

Pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs via the pulmonary artery. Thinner walls — only pumps to nearby lungs.

🔴

Left atrium

Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs via the pulmonary vein. Contracts → pushes blood into left ventricle.

❤️

Left ventricle

Pumps oxygenated blood around the whole body via the aorta. Thickest muscular wall — generates highest pressure to reach all organs.

⚠️ Atria fill from veins → contract together to fill ventricles → ventricles contract to pump blood OUT. Valves close to prevent backflow — this is the heartbeat sound.

Coronary heart disease (CHD)

Coronary arteries supply the heart muscle with oxygen and glucose. In CHD, fatty material builds up on the artery lining — narrowing the vessels. Reduced blood flow → less oxygen → pain, heart attack, or death.

Treatments for CHD

TreatmentHow it worksAdvantages ✅Disadvantages ❌
Stent Metal mesh inserted into narrowed artery. Balloon inflates to open artery and fix stent in place, then removed. Stent holds artery open permanently. No general anaesthetic. Quick recovery. Many release drugs to prevent clotting. Risk of blood clot around stent. Not suitable for very blocked arteries.
Bypass surgery Blocked coronary artery replaced with a section of vein from elsewhere (e.g. leg), re-routing blood around the blockage. Works for badly blocked arteries. Long-lasting solution. Expensive. General anaesthetic needed. Major surgery — longer recovery, higher risk.
Statins Drugs that reduce blood cholesterol levels, slowing the rate of fatty deposit build-up in coronary arteries. Non-invasive. Preventative. Easy daily tablet. Long-term medication. Possible side effects. Don't fix existing blockages.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Draw a flow chart showing blood passing through the heart [4 marks]
  • Explain the importance of: heart valves [2] / coronary arteries [2] / thickened left ventricle wall [3]
  • Why is blood in arteries bright red — but not in pulmonary arteries? [3 marks]
  • Describe what a stent is [2 marks]
  • Table: advantages and disadvantages of stents vs bypass surgery [4 marks]

Key: Left ventricle wall is THICKER because it pumps blood the full distance around the body — needs much higher pressure than the right ventricle (which only pumps to the lungs).

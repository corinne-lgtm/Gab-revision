B8.1 Photosynthesis

The raw materials and energy source for photosynthesis, that it is an endothermic reaction, and the equations that summarise it.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Photosynthesis converts CO₂ + water into glucose + oxygen using light energy — it is an endothermic reaction taking place in chloroplasts.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • Raw materials and energy source for photosynthesis
  • Photosynthesis is an endothermic reaction
  • The equations that summarise photosynthesis

The photosynthesis equation

carbon dioxide + water —light→ glucose + oxygen
6CO₂ + 6H₂O —light→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
⚠️ Study tip: Photosynthesis is endothermic — energy is transferred FROM the environment TO the chloroplasts by light. The energy input (breaking CO₂ and H₂O bonds) is greater than the energy output (forming glucose and O₂ bonds). The extra energy is stored in the glucose.

Where photosynthesis happens

🟢

Chloroplasts

Cells in algae and plant leaves contain chloroplasts filled with the green pigment chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs light energy to power the reaction.

💧

Raw materials

  • Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — from the air, through stomata
  • Water (H₂O) — absorbed from the soil through roots, carried up in the xylem
  • Light energy — from the sun, absorbed by chlorophyll
🍬

Products

  • Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) — used immediately for respiration, or converted to starch for storage
  • Oxygen (O₂) — released into the air as a by-product
💡 Every year plants produce about 368,000,000,000 tonnes of oxygen — so there is plenty to go around!

Path of a carbon atom — from CO₂ to starch

1
Carbon dioxide (CO₂) in the air enters the leaf through the stomata by diffusion down a concentration gradient
2
CO₂ diffuses into the mesophyll cells and into the chloroplasts
3
In the chloroplast, CO₂ reacts with water → glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) — the carbon is now part of the glucose molecule
4
Glucose molecules are joined together to form insoluble starch — stored in the leaf and elsewhere in the plant. The carbon atom is now part of the starch molecule.

Leaf adaptations for photosynthesis

📐

Broad and flat

Large surface area for maximum light absorption

📏

Thin

Short diffusion distances for CO₂ to reach cells — and for O₂ to leave

💧

Veins (xylem + phloem)

Xylem brings water to leaf cells; phloem removes glucose products

💨

Air spaces

Allow CO₂ to diffuse to cells and O₂ to diffuse out — through stomata

🔵

Guard cells + stomata

Open and close to regulate gas exchange and water loss

🟢

Chlorophyll in chloroplasts

Absorbs light energy — palisade cells near top, closest to light source

Algae and photosynthesis

Algae are aquatic — adapted to photosynthesise in water. They absorb CO₂ dissolved in the water around them. They have a large surface area. The oxygen they produce also dissolves in the water as it is released — vital for aquatic life.

To demonstrate that a plant is photosynthesising: use aquatic plants such as Cabomba or Elodea — you can see the oxygen bubbles they produce. The gas relights a glowing splint, confirming it is rich in oxygen.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • State where a plant gets CO₂ and water for photosynthesis [2] + where algae get CO₂, water, and light [3]
  • Describe the path taken by a carbon atom from CO₂ in the air to being part of a starch molecule in a plant [5 marks]
  • Explain why a leaf kept in the light for 24 hours tests blue-black for starch, but one kept in the dark for 24 hours has no effect on iodine [4 marks]
  • a) Give the word equation for photosynthesis [2] b) Explain why photosynthesis is an endothermic reaction [3 marks]

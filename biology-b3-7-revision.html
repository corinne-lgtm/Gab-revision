B3.7 Making digestion efficient

The roles of hydrochloric acid and bile in making digestion more efficient.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
The body produces hydrochloric acid and bile to create the optimum conditions for digestive enzymes to work as efficiently as possible.
🎯

Learning objective

  • The roles of hydrochloric acid and bile in making digestion more efficient

Hydrochloric acid in the stomach

🔴

What it does

  • Produced by glands in the stomach lining — the stomach produces around 3 litres of hydrochloric acid a day
  • Gives the stomach a low (acidic) pH ~2 — the optimum for pepsin (the stomach protease)
  • Allows pepsin to work efficiently to break down proteins
  • Kills most bacteria taken in with food
🛡️

Mucus protects the stomach

The stomach produces a thick layer of mucus that coats the stomach walls — protecting them from being digested by the acid and enzymes. If someone develops a stomach ulcer, the protective mucus is lost and the acid attacks the lining, causing pain.

Bile — two important functions

⚖️

Function 1: Neutralising acid

As food leaves the stomach, the acidic mixture moves into the small intestine. The liver makes an alkaline (green-yellow) liquid called bile. Bile is squirted through the bile duct onto the food in the duodenum.

Bile neutralises the acid — providing the alkaline conditions needed for pancreatic and small intestine enzymes (amylase, lipase, trypsin) to work efficiently at their optimum pH.

💧

Function 2: Emulsifying fats

Fats do not mix with watery digestive juices — they form large globules that are hard for lipase to break down. Bile emulsifies fat — physically breaking large fat droplets into much smaller droplets.

This gives a much larger surface area for lipase to work on — so lipase can break down fats into fatty acids and glycerol much more quickly.

⚠️ Bile is NOT an enzyme — it does not chemically break down fat molecules. It only physically breaks fat into smaller droplets (emulsification).

Bile — where it's made and stored

🟡

Made in the liver

Bile is produced continuously by the liver — in tiny bile ducts throughout the liver.

🫘

Stored in the gall bladder

Bile drains from the liver into the gall bladder where it is stored. When food enters the duodenum, bile is released through the bile duct into the small intestine.

🪨

Gall stones

Sometimes gall stones form in the gall bladder. These can block the gall bladder and bile ducts, causing pain and reducing the efficiency of digestion.

Summary: pH control throughout the gut

🔴

Stomach (pH ~2)

Hydrochloric acid → acidic conditions → optimum for pepsin (protease)

🟢

Small intestine (pH ~7–8)

Bile neutralises acid → alkaline conditions → optimum for amylase, lipase, trypsin

What is metabolism?

Metabolism is the sum of all the reactions that take place in a cell or the body of an organism. Energy transferred by respiration is used to power these continual enzyme-controlled processes that:

  • Make molecules (build up) — anabolic reactions
  • Break down molecules — catabolic reactions

Some energy simply heats the environment. But most is used to drive the metabolic reactions that enable growth, movement, and change.

Six key metabolic reactions

🍬

1. Glucose → starch / glycogen / cellulose

Conversion of glucose into complex carbohydrates for storage (starch/glycogen) or cell wall strengthening (cellulose).

🛢️

2. Glycerol + fatty acids → lipids

Formation of lipid molecules from one glycerol and three fatty acid molecules — for energy storage and cell membranes.

🧬

3. Glucose + nitrate ions → amino acids → proteins

Using glucose and nitrate ions to form amino acids, which are then assembled into proteins (enzymes, structural proteins).

⚡

4. Reactions of respiration

Glucose (+ O₂) → CO₂ + water + energy. The fundamental energy-releasing reactions happening in every living cell.

🌿

5. Reactions of photosynthesis

CO₂ + water → glucose + O₂ (in plants and algae only). Building glucose from inorganic raw materials using light energy.

🔄

6. Breakdown of excess proteins → urea

In the liver: excess amino acids are deaminated — the amino group is removed and converted to urea, which is excreted in urine via the kidneys.

Higher The role of the liver

The liver is a large (~1.5 kg), reddish-brown organ that carries out many different metabolic functions. Liver cells grow and regenerate rapidly. Its functions include:

  • Detoxifying poisonous substances — including ethanol from alcoholic drinks
  • Breaking down old, worn out red blood cells and storing the iron until it is needed to synthesise more blood cells
  • Passing breakdown products into the blood for excretion in urine via the kidneys
  • Breaking down excess amino acids → urea (deamination)
  • Converting lactic acid back to glucose after anaerobic respiration — repaying the oxygen debt

Higher Removing lactic acid — repaying the oxygen debt

Blood flowing through the muscles transports lactic acid to the liver where it is converted back to glucose. Once the oxygen debt has been repaid:

  • Lactic acid → glucose (in liver)
  • Glucose is then either broken down by aerobic respiration → CO₂ + water
  • Or converted to glycogen and stored in the liver until needed

This process requires oxygen — which is why heart rate and breathing rate stay elevated after exercise until the oxygen debt is fully repaid.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • What is metabolism? [1 mark]
  • Give four examples of metabolic reactions in the body [4 marks]
  • (Higher) The liver is an organ of respiration — discuss this statement [5 marks]

For the 5-mark discussion: The liver receives blood carrying lactic acid from muscles (via the hepatic portal vein) → converts lactic acid to glucose → glucose respired aerobically in liver cells → CO₂ + water. Also: liver itself carries out aerobic respiration to fuel its own metabolic processes. BUT the liver is not the site of anaerobic respiration (that's in muscles) — the liver is involved in the aftermath of anaerobic respiration. Award marks for nuance.

💡 Big Ideas
⚡
Motor Effect

A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field experiences a force — this is the motor effect.

🧲
Direction of Force

Use Fleming's left-hand rule to find the direction of the force on the conductor.

📐
Flux Density

Magnetic flux density B (measured in tesla, T) is a measure of the strength of a magnetic field.

🔄
Electric Motor

A coil in a magnetic field rotates continuously thanks to the motor effect and a split-ring commutator.

📐 The Key Formula
F = B I l
F — Force (newtons, N)
B — Magnetic flux density (tesla, T)
I — Current (amperes, A)
l — Length of conductor in field (metres, m)
📌 Key Condition
This formula applies when the wire is perpendicular to the magnetic field. If the wire is parallel to the field, the force is zero.

✋ Fleming's Left-Hand Rule
📌 How to use it
Hold your left hand with your First finger pointing in the direction of the magnetic Field, your seCond finger pointing in the direction of the Current — your thuMb then points in the direction of the Movement (force).
☝️ First finger
Points in the direction of the magnetic Field.
✌️ seCond finger
Points in the direction of the Current.
👍 thuMb
Points in the direction of the Movement (force).
✅ Exam tip
You must use your left hand — the right-hand rule is for generators (P13.4). Getting the wrong hand is a very common mistake in the exam.
⬆️ Increasing / Reversing the Force
🔼 Increase the force by…
  • Increasing the current
  • Using a stronger magnet (higher B)
  • Increasing the length of the conductor in the field
🔁 Reverse the force by…
  • Reversing the direction of the current
  • Reversing the direction of the magnetic field
  • (Doing both → force stays the same direction)
⚠️ Watch out
The force is greatest when the wire is perpendicular to the field, and zero when the wire is parallel to the field. This often appears as a 3-mark question.
🔄 The Electric Motor
🔄
How it works

A rectangular coil of wire sits inside a magnetic field. When current flows, the motor effect creates a force on each side of the coil — but the forces are in opposite directions on opposite sides, causing the coil to rotate.

🔌 Split-ring commutator
A metal ring split in two, fixed to the coil. It reverses the current direction every half-turn so the coil keeps spinning the same way.
🖊️ Graphite brushes
Press against the commutator to keep electrical contact as the coil spins. Graphite is used because it is a good conductor and very slippery — low friction.
⚡ Control speed
Increase the current → stronger force → faster rotation.
🔁 Reverse direction
Reverse the battery connections (reverse the current) → motor spins in the opposite direction.
✅ Exam tip
In a 3-mark "explain continuous rotation" question, always mention: (1) force on each side due to motor effect, (2) forces in opposite directions causing rotation, (3) split-ring commutator reverses current every half-turn so rotation continues.
🧮 Worked Example
📋 Exam-style question

A straight wire is placed in a magnetic field. The magnetic flux density is 0.032 T, the length of the conductor in the field is 40 mm, and the current is 2.2 A. Calculate the force on the conductor.

1
Convert units: 40 mm = 0.040 m
2
Write the formula: F = B × I × l
3
Substitute: F = 0.032 × 2.2 × 0.040
4
Calculate: F = 0.0028 N → Answer: 0.0028 N
✅ Exam tip
Always convert mm → m before substituting into F = BIl. Forgetting this conversion loses marks.

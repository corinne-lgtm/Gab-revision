AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

7.2 Did Hitler make Germany self-sufficient?

How Schacht and Goering tried to control the economy, build rearmament, reduce imports and prepare Germany for war.

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index 📚 Germany index ✏️ Quiz rapide
Hitler wanted Germany to become self-sufficient: able to make what it needed without relying on other countries. In reality, Nazi policy helped rearmament but Germany still depended on imported raw materials.
🎯

Objectives

  • Outline how Germany tried to become self-sufficient.
  • Evaluate whether Nazi economic policy was successful.
  • Assess how Nazi policies affected German farmers.

The economy under Schacht

🏦

Who was Schacht?

Hitler appointed Hjalmar Schacht, a respected banker, as Minister of Economics. His job was to rebuild Germany’s economy and find ways to pay for rearmament.

🤝

Trade deals

Schacht made deals with countries in South America and south-east Europe. Germany exchanged German-made goods for raw materials such as iron, rubber, steel and wood.

⚠️

Why Schacht lost influence

At first, production increased and unemployment fell. But Hitler wanted faster rearmament. Germany still needed raw materials from abroad, so Schacht was replaced by Hermann Goering.

The Four Year Plan under Goering

1936
Goering introduced the Four Year Plan
➡
Aim
prepare Germany for war within four years
➡
Method
increase military production and reduce imports

What increased?

  • Steel production
  • Explosives and chemicals
  • Textiles and uniforms
  • Aircraft, tanks, ships and guns

Jobs and contracts

Rearmament created thousands of jobs. Large businesses won government contracts, especially those making weapons and military equipment.

Limits

The plan did not fully succeed. Oil production and some other key targets were not met. Germany was still not ready for a long war by 1939.

Self-sufficiency: what did it mean?

🏭

Autarky

Self-sufficiency, also called autarky, meant Germany should produce its own food and raw materials instead of buying them from other countries.

This mattered because Hitler wanted Germany to survive a future war even if enemies stopped trade.

🧪

Substitutes

German scientists tried to develop artificial substitutes:

  • petrol from coal
  • rubber from coal
  • cotton from pulped wood
  • coffee from acorns
  • make-up from flour

“Guns or butter?”

🧈

What the phrase means

“Guns or butter?” means a government must choose between military spending and ordinary consumer goods. Nazi Germany prioritised guns: tanks, planes and weapons.

🖼️

Criticism from opponents

Some critics argued that the Nazis were sacrificing ordinary people’s living standards. The anti-Nazi artist John Heartfield mocked Goering’s claim that iron made a country strong while butter only made people fat.

📌

Exam meaning

For an exam answer, explain both sides: rearmament reduced unemployment and made Germany stronger, but it did not make Germany fully self-sufficient and it limited consumer choice.

Did the Nazis help farmers?

Why farmers mattered

About 30% of Germans worked in agriculture or forestry. Many farmers had supported the Nazis because they were struggling in the late 1920s and wanted protection.

What the Nazis offered

  • Guaranteed prices for farm produce
  • Protection from losing land if farmers fell into debt
  • Support for traditional rural family life
  • Attempts to make Germany produce more of its own food

Problems for farmers

Some laws forced farmers to divide land between children, which made farms too small to be profitable. Others resented state control, even if they liked the idea of family land being secure.

P9.2 Velocity & Acceleration

Speed in a direction, how quickly velocity changes, and the maths to describe it all.

⚡ Velocity 📈 Acceleration 🔢 a = Δv ÷ t 📉 Deceleration 📊 v–t Graphs

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Explain the difference between speed and velocity

  • Explain the difference between acceleration and deceleration
  • Interpret a velocity–time graph to find acceleration
  • Understand that a negative acceleration means slowing down

💡 The Big Ideas

🧭
Velocity
Speed in a specific direction — it's a vector quantity
📍
Displacement
Distance travelled in a given direction
⚡
Acceleration
Change in velocity per second (m/s²)
🛑
Deceleration
Negative acceleration — the object slows down

🧭 Speed vs Velocity

Velocity is speed in a given direction.

⚡ Speed (scalar)

  • Has magnitude (size) only
  • No direction needed
  • Example: 30 m/s
  • Can be the same for two objects moving in opposite directions

🧭 Velocity (vector)

  • Has magnitude and direction
  • Example: 30 m/s north
  • Two cars at 30 m/s in opposite directions have different velocities
  • On a roundabout, speed is constant but velocity changes continuously
🔬 Higher Tier
A car moving round a roundabout at constant speed still has a constantly changing velocity because its direction keeps changing. Velocity is never constant unless both speed and direction are fixed.

📐 The Acceleration Formula

Core Formula
a = Δv ÷ t
acceleration (m/s²) = change in velocity (m/s) ÷ time (s)
Expanded Form
a = (v − u) ÷ t
where v = final velocity, u = initial velocity, t = time taken
⭐ Exam Tip
The unit of acceleration is m/s² (metres per second squared). This means the change in speed (in m/s) that occurs every second. Don't confuse this with m/s!
💡 Remember
Δv means "change in velocity" = final velocity − initial velocity. Always subtract in this order!

🛑 Deceleration

⚠️ Key Point
Deceleration (negative acceleration) occurs when a driver brakes. The object slows down — velocity decreases over time.
✏️ Worked Example
A car moving at 28 m/s brakes and stops in 8.0 s. Calculate its deceleration.
Given: u = 28 m/s, v = 0 m/s, t = 8.0 s
Formula: a = (v − u) ÷ t
Substitute: a = (0 − 28) ÷ 8.0
Calculate: a = −28 ÷ 8.0 = −3.5 m/s²
Deceleration = 3.5 m/s²
⭐ Exam Tip
A minus value for acceleration means the object is slowing down. When talking about deceleration specifically, you do not need to include the minus sign — just state the value as positive.

📌 Key Points Summary

🧭 Velocity

Speed in a given direction. A vector quantity — has direction and magnitude.

⚡ Acceleration

Change in velocity per second. Unit: m/s².

🛑 Deceleration

When an object slows down — negative acceleration.

🔢 The Formula

a = (v − u) ÷ t — always subtract initial from final velocity!

📏 Scalar vs Vector

Speed = scalar (size only). Velocity = vector (size + direction).

⚠️ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out
Don't confuse speed and velocity. An object can change velocity without changing speed — if only its direction changes (e.g. circular motion).
⚠️ Watch Out
When calculating acceleration, always do v − u (final minus initial), not the other way round. Getting this backwards gives the wrong sign!
⚠️ Watch Out
The unit is m/s², not m/s. Every mark scheme will penalise a missing or wrong unit.

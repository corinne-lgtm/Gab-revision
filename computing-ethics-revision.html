1. Ethical Issues in Computing

Ethics is about what is morally right or wrong. In computing, ethical questions arise because technology can be used in ways that harm people even if they are not strictly illegal.

👁️ Surveillance

CCTV, cookies, social media tracking and smart devices collect data on people's behaviour, often without meaningful consent.

⚖️ Bias in algorithms

Machine learning trained on biased data can discriminate in hiring, credit scoring or facial recognition — especially against minority groups.

📰 Misinformation

Social media algorithms amplify viral content regardless of accuracy, spreading fake news at scale.

⚡ Exam tip When asked about ethical issues, always give a balanced argument — state the concern AND a counter-point or benefit. AQA rewards nuance.

2. Privacy & Data Collection

Every interaction with technology generates data. Companies collect it to improve services and sell targeted advertising, but this creates serious privacy risks.

✅ Benefits of data collection Personalised recommendations · Fraud detection · Medical research · Improved public services
⚠️ Risks of data collection Identity theft · Mass surveillance · Data breaches · Lack of informed consent · Profile manipulation

3. Computer Misuse Act 1990

The Computer Misuse Act 1990 (CMA) was the UK's first law specifically targeting cybercrime. It creates three main offences:

SectionOffenceExampleMax sentence
S.1Unauthorised access to computer materialGuessing someone's password to read their emails2 years
S.2Unauthorised access with intent to commit further offencesHacking a bank's system to steal money5 years
S.3Unauthorised modification of computer materialInstalling ransomware; deleting files10 years
🔑 Key point "Unauthorised" is the crucial word. Penetration testers have permission — they are not breaking the law. White-hat hackers who act outside their permission slip into illegality.

4. Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR

The Data Protection Act 2018 implements the EU's GDPR into UK law. It governs how organisations collect, store and use personal data.

📋 The 7 principles of UK GDPR — memorise these! Lawful, fair & transparent · Purpose limitation · Data minimisation · Accuracy · Storage limitation · Integrity & confidentiality · Accountability

Individual rights under GDPR

Rights you have as a data subject

  • Right to be informed
  • Right of access (Subject Access Request)
  • Right to rectification
  • Right to erasure ("right to be forgotten")
  • Right to restrict processing
  • Right to data portability

Obligations on organisations

  • Only collect data needed for stated purpose
  • Keep data secure and up-to-date
  • Appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) if processing at scale
  • Report breaches to ICO within 72 hours
  • Obtain valid consent before processing
🏛️ Regulator The ICO (Information Commissioner's Office) enforces the DPA. It can fine organisations up to £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover for serious breaches.

5. Copyright & Intellectual Property

Copyright is an automatic legal right that protects original creative works (code, images, music, text) from being copied or distributed without the creator's permission.

Licence typeWhat you can do
All rights reserved (commercial)Must pay / seek explicit permission
Creative Commons (CC-BY)Use freely but must credit the author
CC-BY-SAUse and adapt, but share-alike (same licence)
CC-BY-NCUse freely for non-commercial purposes only
Open source (MIT / GPL)Use, modify, redistribute — check licence terms
Public domainNo restrictions — copyright has expired
⚠️ Common mistake "Free to use" does not mean "copyright free." Images found via Google Images are usually copyrighted. Always check the licence.

6. Environmental Impact of Computing

🏭 Manufacture

  • Rare earth metals mined in harmful conditions
  • High energy & water use in semiconductor fabrication
  • Long supply chains with large carbon footprint

⚡ Use

  • Data centres use ~1–2% of global electricity
  • Cooling systems require huge amounts of water
  • Cryptocurrency mining is extremely energy-intensive
  • Streaming video is the biggest internet traffic source

🗑️ Disposal (e-waste)

  • 50 million tonnes of e-waste per year globally
  • Toxic chemicals (lead, mercury) leach into soil
  • Only ~20% is formally recycled
  • WEEE Directive regulates disposal in UK
♻️ Reducing impact Extending device lifespan · Renewable energy for data centres · Right to repair legislation · Responsible recycling · Efficient algorithm design (green computing)

7. The Digital Divide

The digital divide refers to the gap between those who have effective access to digital technology and those who do not. It exists at local, national and global levels.

🌍 Global causes Lack of infrastructure · Cost of devices and data · Censorship and government control · Limited electricity supply · Language barriers
🏠 Local (UK) causes Rural broadband gaps · Low income · Age (elderly less likely to be online) · Disability · Digital literacy skills

AQA GCSE History · Germany 1890–1945

3.4 Germany’s Golden Age

How Weimar Germany became culturally exciting in the late 1920s — and why some Germans hated it.

🏠 Main hub 📚 History index Revision Questions ✏️ Quiz rapide
The late 1920s brought a cultural boom in Germany — but many conservatives believed it showed moral decline.

Why was it called a “Golden Age”?

🎭 Freedom

After censorship under the Kaiser, people could experiment more freely.

🎨 Creativity

Art, design, music and film became modern and innovative.

🌍 Berlin

Berlin became a major European cultural centre.

Main cultural developments

🎬 Cinema

Films like Metropolis and stars like Marlene Dietrich made German cinema famous.

🎷 Nightlife

Cabarets, jazz clubs and experimental theatre flourished, especially in Berlin.

📚 Literature

Writers such as Erich Remarque explored war and modern life.

🏛️ Bauhaus

Modern architecture and design focused on simplicity and function.

🖼️ Art

Artists like Otto Dix showed social divisions and harsh realities.

Key individuals

Marlene Dietrich

Symbol of modern film, glamour and changing roles for women.

Otto Dix

Avant-garde artist who exposed inequality, war trauma and social contrasts.

Why did some Germans dislike Weimar culture?

Supporters believed:

  • Germany was modern and progressive.
  • Freedom encouraged creativity.
  • Berlin was exciting and international.

Critics believed:

  • Traditional values were being destroyed.
  • Nightlife was immoral.
  • Art was too experimental.
  • Culture showed weakness and decline.

Exam summary

Germany’s “Golden Age” was a period of major cultural growth in the late 1920s, with advances in film, art, literature and design. However, many conservative Germans rejected these changes, believing they undermined traditional values.

Big Ideas
📏
Speed
How far an object travels per second. Measured in metres per second (m/s).
📈
Constant Speed
Uniform motion — the same distance is covered every second. Shown as a straight line on a d–t graph.
🗻
Gradient = Speed
The steeper the line, the faster the object. A flat line means the object is stationary.
⏸️
Stationary
No movement at all. Shows as a completely horizontal (flat) section on the graph.
🧮
Average Speed
Total distance ÷ total time, even if the object was not moving at a constant speed throughout.
⚗️
The Speed Equation
The core equation
v = s ÷ t   |   s = v × t   |   t = s ÷ v
🔵 Speed (v)

Speed is measured in metres per second (m/s). It tells you how many metres an object travels every second.

v = s / t
🔵 Distance (s)

Distance is measured in metres (m). Rearrange: if you know speed and time, find distance using s = v × t.

🔵 Time (t)

Time is measured in seconds (s). Rearrange: if you know speed and distance, find time using t = s / v.

🎓
Exam tip: If time is given in minutes or hours, always convert to seconds first before substituting into the equation. 1 minute = 60 s, 1 hour = 3600 s.

Reading a Distance–Time Graph
The gradient of a line on a distance–time graph represents the object's speed.
1
Identify the type of line
Is it straight (constant speed), horizontal (stationary), or curved (changing speed)?
2
Pick two clear points on the line
Read off the distance values (y-axis) and time values (x-axis) at each point.
3
Calculate the gradient
Gradient = change in distance ÷ change in time = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)
4
State the speed with units
Always write the unit — m/s. This gradient value is the speed of the object.
📌
Study tip: When plotting points on a graph grid, make a clear point or a small cross — not a large blob. Accuracy matters in exam mark schemes!
🚛
Worked Example — Lorries X, Y, Z
Three lorries travel on the same motorway. Their distance–time graphs are plotted together. The steepest line = fastest lorry.

🥈 Medium

🥉 Slowest
📐 Calculating Y's speed from the graph

Y travels 30 000 m in 1250 s:

v = 30 000 ÷ 1250 = 24 m/s
⚠️ Z stopped for part of its journey (flat section from 500 s to 1000 s). This is visible as a horizontal segment on the graph — zero gradient, zero speed.
✅ To find Z's average speed over 1500 s: use total distance ÷ total time (including the stopped period).
⚖️
Stationary vs Moving at Constant Speed

✅ Moving at constant speed

  • Straight line sloping upwards
  • Gradient > 0
  • Equal distance in equal time intervals
  • Speed = gradient (m/s)

🛑 Stationary

  • Completely horizontal line
  • Gradient = 0
  • No distance gained over time
  • Speed = 0 m/s

Exam Tips
⏱️
Always convert time to seconds before using v = s / t. Examiners frequently give time in minutes to catch you out.
📐
Show your gradient working — pick two widely spaced points and show (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). Don't just state an answer.
📊
For "average speed" use total distance for entire journey ÷ total time for entire journey — including any stopped periods.
⚠️
Common mistake: Confusing a steeper gradient with a longer distance. A lorry that goes further is not necessarily faster — it may have had more time. Always compare gradients, not end distances.

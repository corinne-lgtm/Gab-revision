P1.1 · textbook pages 4–5

Changes in energy stores

Energy is stored in different ways and transferred by heating, by waves, by an electric current or when a force does work. GCSE answers should name the starting store, the transfer pathway and the final store.

Subtopic

What Gabriel needs to know

  • know the main energy stores
  • identify transfer pathways
  • describe changes for falling objects, torches and trains
  • avoid saying energy is “used up”
Study tip: Always write store → transfer → store. Do not just say “energy changed”.

Energy stores

Chemical, kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal/internal, magnetic, electrostatic and nuclear.

Transfer pathways

By heating, by waves, by an electric current, or mechanically when a force acts through a distance.

Typical examples

Torch: chemical → electrical → light + heating. Falling object: gravitational potential → kinetic → heating/sound on impact.

P1.2 · textbook pages 6–7

Conservation of energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. In a closed system the total energy remains constant, although energy can be transferred to less useful stores.

Subtopic

What Gabriel needs to know

  • state the conservation principle
  • apply it to a rollercoaster, pendulum or bungee jumper
  • describe closed systems
  • explain why total energy stays constant
Study tip: “Conserved” does not mean “all useful”; it means total energy stays the same.

Closed system

No energy is transferred into or out of the system, so the total energy before and after is the same.

Pendulum idea

As a pendulum falls, gravitational potential energy decreases and kinetic energy increases. On the way back up the reverse happens.

Bungee idea

GPE transfers to KE, then to elastic potential energy of the rope, with some energy dissipated to the surroundings.

P1.3 · textbook pages 8–9

Energy and work

When a force makes an object move through a distance in the direction of the force, work is done. Work done equals energy transferred.

Subtopic

What Gabriel needs to know

  • define work done
  • use W = F × s
  • recognise that one joule equals one newton metre
  • explain the effect of friction
Study tip: Use the distance moved in the direction of the force, not just any distance in the question.

Equation

W = F × s where W is in joules, F in newtons and s in metres.

Meaning

A force transfers energy to an object when it causes movement in the direction of the force.

Friction

Work against friction transfers energy to thermal stores of the surfaces rubbing and the surroundings.

P1.4 · textbook pages 10–11

Gravitational potential energy stores

Lifting an object transfers energy to its gravitational potential energy store. The change depends on mass, gravitational field strength and vertical height change.

Subtopic

What Gabriel needs to know

  • use ΔEₚ = m g Δh
  • use mass in kg and height in metres
  • know g ≈ 9.8 N/kg on Earth
  • relate lifting work to GPE gain
Study tip: A heavier object or a bigger vertical height change gives a bigger GPE increase.

Equation

ΔEₚ = m g Δh

Variables

m in kg, g in N/kg, Δh is vertical change in height in metres.

Common trap

On a slope, use the vertical height gained, not the distance moved along the slope.

P1.5 · textbook pages 12–13

Kinetic energy and elastic energy stores

Moving objects store kinetic energy. Stretched springs and elastic bands store elastic potential energy.

Subtopic

What Gabriel needs to know

  • use Eₖ = ½mv²
  • understand speed is squared
  • use Eₑ = ½ke² for a spring
  • link catapult and spring examples to energy stores
Study tip: Units matter: mass in kilograms, speed in m/s, extension in metres.

Kinetic energy

Eₖ = ½mv². If speed doubles, kinetic energy becomes four times greater.

Elastic energy

Eₑ = ½ke² where k is the spring constant and e is the extension.

Catapult idea

As a stretched elastic band is released, elastic energy transfers to kinetic energy.

P1.6 · textbook pages 14–15

Energy dissipation

Useful energy is transferred to the place we want it and in the form we want it. Dissipated energy spreads to the surroundings and becomes less useful.

Subtopic

What Gabriel needs to know

  • define useful and wasted energy
  • explain dissipation
  • describe friction examples
  • link heating and sound to less useful transfers
Study tip: Energy is not lost; it is transferred to less useful stores.

Useful energy

The intended transfer, for example heating water in a kettle or moving the blades of a fan.

Wasted energy

Energy transferred by an unwanted pathway, often to thermal stores of the surroundings or by sound.

Examples

Bike brakes, drills, running machines and gearboxes all dissipate energy due to friction.

P1.7 · textbook pages 16–17

Energy and efficiency

Efficiency compares useful output with total input. No device can be more than 100% efficient.

Subtopic

What Gabriel needs to know

  • calculate efficiency as a number or percentage
  • use useful output ÷ total input
  • calculate wasted energy
  • suggest ways to reduce unwanted transfers
Study tip: If your percentage is over 100, the calculation is wrong.

Equation

Efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input

Percentage form

Efficiency (%) = useful output ÷ total input × 100

Improving efficiency

Lubrication, insulation, reduced electrical resistance and streamlined shapes can reduce energy waste.

P1.8 · textbook pages 18–19

Electrical appliances

Appliances are designed for a particular purpose. Identify the useful energy transfer and the unwanted transfers for each device.

Subtopic

What Gabriel needs to know

  • compare useful and wasted outputs
  • choose an appliance for a job
  • use examples such as drill, bell, mixer and clockwork radio
  • describe clockwork radio energy changes
Study tip: State the output in terms of energy, not just “it works”.

Choosing an appliance

The best appliance provides the desired output with as little wasted energy as possible.

Clockwork radio

Winding it transfers energy to an elastic potential store in the spring, which later drives the generator.

Typical outputs

Drill → kinetic, bell → sound, kettle → heating water, lamp → light.

P1.9 · textbook pages 20–21

Energy and power

Power is the rate of energy transfer. A more powerful device transfers the same amount of energy in less time.

Subtopic

What Gabriel needs to know

  • use P = E ÷ t
  • know 1 W = 1 J/s and 1 kW = 1000 W
  • compare appliances by power rating
  • calculate wasted power
Study tip: Convert kilowatts to watts before calculations unless the question allows kW.

Equation

P = E ÷ t

Meaning

Power tells you how fast energy is transferred.

Efficiency and power

Efficiency = useful power out ÷ total power in × 100, and wasted power = total power in − useful power out.

Big Ideas

Waves transfer…

Energy and information — not matter. The medium itself doesn't travel.

Transverse

Oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer.

Longitudinal

Oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer.

Mechanical

Need a medium (substance) to travel through. Can be either type.

Waves transfer energy without transferring matter. The oscillations of a transverse wave are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer, while the oscillations of a longitudinal wave are parallel to the direction of energy transfer.

Transverse vs Longitudinal

⟺ Transverse

  • Oscillations perpendicular to energy transfer
  • Water ripples on a surface
  • All EM waves (light, radio, microwaves)
  • Waves on a rope moved up and down
  • Can travel through a vacuum

⟺ Longitudinal

  • Oscillations parallel to energy transfer
  • Sound waves in air
  • Slinky pushed in and out
  • Has compressions & rarefactions
  • Needs a medium to travel
Examples

🔊 Sound Waves

Longitudinal. Vibrations in air — compressions and rarefactions travel in the same direction as energy.

💡 Light & EM Waves

All transverse. Travel through a vacuum at 300,000 km/s. No medium needed.

🌊 Water Ripples

Transverse — the surface moves up and down while energy travels outward.

〰️ Slinky Spring

Move it side-to-side → transverse. Push and pull it → longitudinal (compressions visible).

⭐ Exam Tip
In the exam, always state the direction of oscillation relative to the direction of energy transfer. For a transverse wave: oscillations are perpendicular. For a longitudinal wave: oscillations are parallel. Learn these definitions exactly — they are 1-mark answers.
⚠ Common Mistake
Don't say "sound is transverse" or "light is longitudinal" — it's the other way round. Sound = longitudinal, light = transverse. Also remember: EM waves travel through a vacuum; mechanical waves do not.

🏢 Types of hospitality & catering provision

Provision is split by whether it makes a profit (commercial) or not, and whether it offers accommodation (residential).

Residential (offers accommodation)Non-residential (no accommodation)
Commercial (aims to profit)Hotels, motels, hostels, B&Bs, Airbnb, holiday parks, campsitesRestaurants, cafés, takeaways, fast food, pubs/bars, airlines, mobile food vans, vending machines
Non-commercial (no profit aim)Hospitals, hospices, care homes, armed forces, prisons, boarding schoolsSchool/college canteens, meals on wheels, subsidised workplace canteens, charity food providers

🍽️ Types of service

Table service

  • Plate: food plated in the kitchen, served by waiting staff
  • Silver: waiter transfers food from a serving dish to the customer's plate at the table
  • Banquet: large catered events (weddings, awards)
  • Family style: serving bowls placed on the table to share
  • Gueridon: food finished/cooked on a trolley in front of the customer

Counter service

  • Cafeteria: customers move along a counter with a tray
  • Fast food: menu behind the counter, quick, disposable packaging
  • Buffet: customers help themselves from a serving table

Personal & residential service

  • Tray/trolley, home delivery, takeaway
  • Residential: room types (single/double/family/suite), refreshments, leisure facilities, conference facilities

📑 Working conditions

Contract types

ContractKey feature
CasualCovers absent staff, no sick pay/holiday entitlement
Full-time (permanent)Fixed hours, sick pay & holiday entitlement
Part-time (permanent)Fixed days/hours, sick pay & holiday entitlement
SeasonalSet time-frame only (e.g. Christmas), no expectation of further work
Zero hoursNo fixed hours either way; no sick pay/holiday entitlement

Pay & benefits

Salary, holiday entitlement (28 days/year, pro-rata for part-time), pension, sickness pay, national minimum wage, tips, bonuses/rewards.

Working hours rules

  • Average max 48 hrs/week (over 17 weeks) — employees can opt out
  • Under-18s: max 8 hrs/day, 40 hrs/week
  • 6+ hour shift = right to a 20 minute break, plus at least 1 day off per week

📈 Factors affecting business success

FactorKey points
EconomyStrong economy → more spending; recession → people cut back on eating out/holidays. VAT is added to prices and goes to the government.
EnvironmentReduce/reuse/recycle, energy-efficient equipment, local & seasonal ingredients lower CO2, waste sorted/composted.
CostsLabour (wages, NI, pension), Material (ingredients, equipment, furnishings), Overheads (rent, rates, gas/electricity, insurance, training).
MediaPrint, broadcast, social media for advertising; customers increasingly book/order/pay/review via smartphone.
New technologyCashless/contactless payment, online booking, key cards, stock ordering software.

Profit formula (exam favourite!)

Gross Profit = Selling price − Ingredient cost. Ingredient cost should be no more than 30% of selling price.

Example: brownie costs £1.50 to make, sells for £4.50 → Gross profit = £3.00 → Gross Profit % = (3.00 ÷ 4.50) × 100 = 66.6%

Net Profit = what's left of gross profit once labour, material and overhead costs are all paid.

📱 Positive and negative uses of media

PositiveNegative
Free/cheap (social media), reaches a wide audience fast, attracts new customers, builds loyalty & personal connection, can target specific groups Print/broadcast advertising is expensive, a bad review can spread fast and damage reputation, can lead to loss of customers/closure

Types of media

  • Printed: magazines, newspapers, billboards, flyers, business cards
  • Broadcast: TV, radio
  • Internet: social media, websites (e.g. TripAdvisor), blogs, email
  • Competitive: competitions, deals, special offers, themed events

B5.7 Human defence responses

How your body stops pathogens getting in, and how white blood cells protect you from disease.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Your body has physical barriers to keep pathogens out, and white blood cells as a second line of defence if they get in.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How your body stops pathogens getting in
  • How white blood cells protect you from disease

First line of defence — keeping pathogens out

1
🧱 Skin — acts as a physical barrier. Prevents bacteria and viruses reaching tissues. If cut or damaged, platelets form a clot → scab seals the wound. Skin also produces antimicrobial secretions to destroy pathogenic bacteria. Healthy skin is also covered in harmless microorganisms that act as an extra barrier.
2
👃 Nose — mucus and hairs — nose is full of hairs and produces sticky mucus. Hairs and mucus trap particles (including pathogens) from the air. If you breathe in polluted air, mucus blown from your nose is blackened — showing the system works.
3
🫁 Trachea and bronchi — mucus + cilia — lining secretes sticky mucus that traps pathogens. Cilia (tiny hair-like projections on cells) beat to waft the mucus up to the back of the throat where it is swallowed.
4
🫁 Stomach — acid — stomach produces acid that destroys most microorganisms in the mucus you swallow and in your food and drink.

Second line of defence — white blood cells

If pathogens get past the first line of defence, white blood cells are waiting. They protect you in three ways:

Role of white blood cellHow it protects you
🍽️ Ingesting microorganisms (phagocytosis) Some white blood cells engulf (take in) pathogens, digesting and destroying them so they cannot make you ill.
💉 Producing antibodies Some white blood cells produce special chemicals called antibodies that target specific pathogens (matching the antigen on the pathogen's surface). You need a unique antibody for each type of pathogen. Once produced, the same antibodies can be made very quickly if that pathogen appears again — stopping you getting the disease twice.
🧪 Producing antitoxins Some white blood cells produce antitoxins that counteract (cancel out) the toxins released by bacteria, preventing them from damaging body cells.
💡 Green mucus during a cold: Some white blood cells contain green enzymes. When you have a cold, these cells destroy cold viruses and bacteria trapped in nasal mucus. The dead white blood cells and bacteria make the mucus look green.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • State how each action prevents spread of disease: washing hands [1]; throwing away tissues [1]; sewage not getting into drinking water [1]
  • Explain why blood not clotting properly increases risk of infections [2]
  • Explain why fewer white blood cells increases risk of infections [3]
  • Discuss how white blood cells help prevent suffering from communicable diseases [6 marks]

Key for 6-mark question: Cover all three WBC roles: phagocytosis (ingesting and destroying pathogens) + antibodies (specific to each antigen, memory means quicker response) + antitoxins (counteract bacterial toxins).

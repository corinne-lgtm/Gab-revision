B8.2 The rate of photosynthesis

Which factors limit the rate of photosynthesis in plants.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
The rate of photosynthesis is limited by whichever factor is in shortest supply — light, temperature, CO₂, or chlorophyll.
🎯

Learning objective

  • Which factors limit the rate of photosynthesis in plants
💡 A limiting factor is any factor that is in short supply and prevents photosynthesis going faster, even if other conditions are ideal.

The four limiting factors

💡

Light intensity

Most obvious factor. More light → faster photosynthesis. No light → photosynthesis stops completely. For most plants, brighter = faster.

Graph shape: Rises steeply, then levels off (limited by another factor — CO₂ or temperature).

🌡️

Temperature

As temperature rises, rate increases — reactions speed up. BUT photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes. Above 40–50°C enzymes are denatured → rate falls sharply.

Graph shape: Rises, reaches a peak, then falls sharply at high temperatures.

💨

CO₂ concentration

CO₂ is needed to make glucose. Atmosphere is only ~0.04% CO₂ — often the most common limiting factor on a sunny day. Increasing CO₂ → faster photosynthesis.

Graph shape: Rises, then levels off when CO₂ is no longer the limiting factor (light or temperature becomes limiting).

🟢

Chlorophyll levels

If chlorophyll is limited (e.g. variegated leaves, magnesium deficiency), less light energy can be absorbed → slower photosynthesis. Without magnesium, chlorophyll cannot be made.

CO₂ levels around a plant — day and night

On a sunny day, CO₂ concentration is often the most common limiting factor.

  • During the day: As light and temperature increase, most CO₂ around the plant gets used up — CO₂ becomes limiting
  • At night: The plant respires but doesn't photosynthesise → CO₂ levels rise again

In a science lab or greenhouse, CO₂ levels can be increased artificially — then CO₂ is no longer limiting and the rate increases further with light and temperature.

In a garden, woodland, or field, any one of the three factors might be limiting at any time.

Light intensity and the inverse square law (Higher)

Light intensity is not linearly related to distance — it follows the inverse square law:

Light intensity ∝ 1 / distance²

If you double the distance between the light and plant, light intensity falls to ¼ (not ½):

At distance 2: 1/2² = 1/4

This means moving a lamp slightly further away has a much bigger effect than you might expect.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • State the three main limiting factors [3 marks]
  • Figure 1 graph: explain what is happening between points a and b [2] and between points b and c [2]
  • Figure 2: explain why it is a different shape to Figures 1 and 3 [4 marks]
  • Explain in terms of limiting factors why tropical rainforest plants are so much bigger than UK woodland plants, and why both are bigger than Arctic tundra plants [6 marks]

Key for 6-mark question: Tropical = high light + high temp + high CO₂ → all factors optimal → fastest rate → biggest plants. UK = moderate light + moderate temp. Arctic = low light + low temp → most limiting → slowest rate → smallest plants.

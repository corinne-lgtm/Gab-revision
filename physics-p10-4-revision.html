What is momentum?
Definition

Momentum is a property of any moving object. The greater the mass and speed, the more momentum it has.

Vector quantity

Momentum has both size and direction — it is a vector. Direction matters in calculations!

Unit

The unit of momentum is kg m/s (kilogram metres per second).

Real life

A rugby player with lots of momentum is very hard to stop. More mass or speed = more momentum.

The Momentum Equation
p = m × v
p = momentum (kg m/s)  ·  m = mass (kg)  ·  v = velocity (m/s)
Conservation of Momentum
The Law

In a closed system, the total momentum before an event is equal to the total momentum after the event.

Closed system

A system where no external forces act on the objects — so momentum is conserved.

When does it apply?

Any collision or explosion — as long as no external resultant force acts on the system.

💡
Exam Tip

Always write: "total momentum before = total momentum after" before setting up your calculation. This shows the examiner you know the law.

Collisions vs Explosions
Collisions
  • Objects come together or bounce apart
  • Total momentum conserved
  • Objects may stick together (velocity of A halved when mass doubles)
  • Example: trolley A hits stationary trolley B
Explosions
  • Objects push apart from rest
  • Both start with zero momentum
  • They recoil in opposite directions with equal and opposite momentum
  • Example: two trolleys pushed apart by a spring
⚠️
Watch out — direction!

If objects move in opposite directions, one momentum is positive and the other is negative. Always assign a positive direction first, then use signs consistently.

Worked Examples
Example 1 — Calculate momentum
A sprinter of mass 50 kg runs at 10 m/s. What is their momentum?
1

Write the formula: p = m × v

2

Substitute: p = 50 × 10

p = 500 kg m/s
Example 2 — Conservation in a collision
Trolley A (2 kg, 3 m/s) hits stationary trolley B (2 kg). They stick together. What is their combined velocity?
1

Total momentum before = 2 × 3 + 2 × 0 = 6 kg m/s

2

Total mass after = 2 + 2 = 4 kg

3

6 = 4 × v → v = 6 ÷ 4

v = 1.5 m/s
Example 3 — Explosion (ice skaters)
An 80 kg skater and a 60 kg skater push apart from rest. The 60 kg skater moves away at 2.0 m/s. Find the velocity of the 80 kg skater.
1

Total momentum before = 0 (both at rest)

2

Momentum after: 80v + 60 × 2.0 = 0

3

80v = −120 → v = −120 ÷ 80

v = −1.5 m/s (opposite direction to the 60 kg skater)

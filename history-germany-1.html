1.1A
Government, industry and socialism

What was Germany like before the First World War?

Germany was a young, rapidly industrialising nation. It had an elected Reichstag, but the Kaiser and the traditional elites still held enormous power.

A new Germany

Before 1871, Germany was a collection of separate states. Prussia was the strongest. After wars against neighbouring powers, the states united and the King of Prussia became German Emperor, or Kaiser.

Prussian militarism

Prussia believed a strong army was essential. When Germany unified, Prussian generals, officers and traditions shaped the new German army, with the Kaiser as supreme commander.

The Second Reich

The German Empire created in 1871 was known as the Second Reich. Germany was a federation of states, each sending representatives to the Bundesrat.

Who really held power?

Government under the Kaiser

Key tension

Democracy?

Men elected the Reichstag, but the Kaiser could ignore ministers' advice and retained great personal power.

Key biography

Kaiser Wilhelm II (reigned 1888–1918)

Wilhelm became Kaiser in 1888 aged 29. He was energetic, ambitious and fascinated by the military. He wanted Germany to become a great world power and strongly supported a larger army and navy.

Industrial power

Germany grows rapidly

Germany's industrialisation accelerated under Wilhelm II. By 1913, Germany was producing more iron and steel and as much coal as Britain. German electrical and chemical industries were also highly successful.

Cause → consequence

Industrialisation changed politics too

Industry expands→More factory workers→Poor pay & conditions→Support for socialism / SPD grows

The Social Democratic Party (SPD) argued that power and wealth should be shared more equally. By around 1914, about one German in three voted for it. This worried the Kaiser and conservative elites.

1.1B
A place in the sun

Weltpolitik, empire and the navy

Wilhelm wanted Germany to act like a global great power. That meant colonies, a larger navy and much greater military spending.

Weltpolitik

Germany wanted greater influence overseas. This new global policy was called Weltpolitik. Germany joined other European powers in building an overseas empire, mainly in Africa.

“A place in the sun”

In 1897, Bernhard von Bülow argued that Germany did not want to push other nations aside, but demanded its own “place in the sun”.

Protecting the empire

Wilhelm believed a powerful navy would help Germany gain and defend colonies. He admired Britain's huge navy and wanted Germany to rival it.

Naval Laws

A series of Naval Laws between 1898 and 1912 rapidly expanded the German navy. Wilhelm also enlarged the army.

The price

Expansion cost huge sums. Taxes were raised and money was borrowed, leaving Germany with long-term debt. Social Democrats criticised spending on battleships while workers faced difficult living conditions.

Exam link: Wilhelm's governments faced pressure from two directions: a growing socialist movement demanding reform, and the financial/political consequences of an expensive world-power policy.

1 · Describe two problems faced by Kaiser Wilhelm II's governments in ruling Germany up to 1914.

4 marks

How to tackle this 4-mark question

  • Choose two different problems.
  • Identify each problem clearly.
  • Add one accurate piece of supporting detail to each.
  • Keep it focused: you are describing two problems, not writing a long essay.
Useful structure: “One problem was… This was a problem because…” then repeat for a second, different problem.
Revision marking points — based on the chapter practice question

Relevant problems include the growth of socialism and SPD support, workers' dissatisfaction with pay and conditions, tension between elected politics and the Kaiser's personal power, and the cost of military/naval expansion. Credit two clearly described, distinct problems with accurate supporting detail.

2 · Explain why support for socialism grew in Germany before 1914.

8 marks

Writing tip

  • Start with a clear reason.
  • Add precise knowledge about industrialisation and workers.
  • Explain the chain: why did this make socialism more attractive?
  • Develop a second reason rather than listing facts.
Revision marking points — original revision question

A strong answer could connect rapid industrialisation with the growth of the urban working class, poor wages and working conditions, and the SPD's socialist promise of a fairer distribution of wealth and political/social reform.

3 · Explain why Kaiser Wilhelm II wanted Germany to build a larger navy.

6 marks

Build a causal explanation

  • Give a reason, not just a fact about ships.
  • Use the ideas of Weltpolitik, empire and great-power status.
  • Explain how a navy helped Wilhelm pursue those aims.
Revision marking points — original revision question

Relevant knowledge includes Wilhelm's admiration of Britain's navy, his desire for Germany to become a major world power, Weltpolitik, acquiring/protecting colonies, and the belief that a large navy would give Germany greater international influence.

4 · Give one feature of the Reichstag before the First World War.

4 marks

Short-answer tip

Name one specific feature and support it with accurate detail. Don't drift into everything you know about German government.

Revision marking points — original revision question

Relevant knowledge includes that the Reichstag was elected by men over 25 and discussed and voted on laws drawn up by the Kaiser and his ministers. Its existence did not remove the Kaiser's considerable personal power.

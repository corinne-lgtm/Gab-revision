The big ideas

📡
Radio waves
Different wavelengths carry different amounts of info over different ranges
🛰️
Microwaves
Used for satellites — spread out less and travel between Earth and space
🌊
Carrier waves
Waves that carry audio signals by varying their amplitude or frequency
💡
Optical fibres
Thin glass fibres using total internal reflection to send light signals

Radio wavelengths — who uses what?

Wavelength Used by Why?
< 1 m (microwaves) Satellite phone, satellite TV links Travel between satellites and Earth; spread out less than radio waves
< ~1 m (short radio) TV masts (terrestrial TV) Carry more info; range limited to local area around transmitter
~1 m to 100 m Local radio stations & emergency services Range limited to area around transmitter — good for local coverage
> 100 m (long radio) National & international radio Much longer range than shorter-wavelength waves

How wavelength affects wave behaviour

📶 Information capacity

Shorter wavelength → more information carried per wave

📏 Range

Shorter wavelength → shorter range (more absorption by atmosphere)

🔊 Spreading

Shorter wavelength → spreads out less → signal stays stronger

Key idea: A carrier wave is an electromagnetic wave used to carry audio information. The audio signal modulates the carrier wave by varying its amplitude (AM) or frequency (FM). The receiver separates the audio signal from the carrier and sends it to the loudspeaker — which produces sound waves matching the original microphone input.

Optical fibres vs radio waves

📡 Radio / Microwaves
  • Signals travel through air
  • Can be intercepted
  • Spread out over distance
  • Longer wavelength → less info
💡 Optical Fibres
  • Light / infrared stays inside fibre
  • More secure — signal can't escape
  • Carry much more information
  • Light has very short wavelength → more pulses

🔬 What are optical fibres?

Very thin glass fibres. Light (or infrared) enters the fibre and is totally internally reflected along its length — it cannot escape through the glass surface.

🔁 Why can't light escape?

When light hits the glass-air boundary at a large angle, it reflects back inside rather than refracting out. This is called total internal reflection.

Key formula

v = f × λ
v = wave speed (m/s) — for EM waves in air: 3 × 10⁸ m/s
f = frequency (Hz)
λ = wavelength (m)

Rearrange: λ = v / f  |  f = v / λ

Mobile phones and safety

Mobile phones send radio signals to the nearest phone mast. If the phone is close to the brain, electromagnetic radiation might affect brain tissue. Children may be more at risk because they have thinner skulls. A UK government report (2000) recommended limiting children's mobile phone use. More research is still needed — there is no definitive conclusion.

Exam tips

💡 Exam Tip 1
For a question about why microwaves are used for satellite TV (not radio waves), say: microwaves can travel through the atmosphere between the satellite and Earth, AND they spread out less so the signal doesn't weaken as much.
💡 Exam Tip 2
For optical fibre security: the signal stays inside the fibre and cannot be picked up outside — unlike radio waves which travel through open air and can be intercepted.
💡 Exam Tip 3
When calculating wavelength: always use v = 3 × 10⁸ m/s for EM waves in air. Convert MHz to Hz first (× 10⁶).
⚠️ Common mistake
Don't confuse carrier waves with the audio signal. The audio signal modulates the carrier — it rides on top of it. The carrier itself is what actually travels through the air as a radio wave.

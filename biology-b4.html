B4.1

The blood

Blood is a tissue: plasma carries red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets around the body.

Plasma

Pale yellow liquid that transports blood cells, carbon dioxide, urea and soluble products of digestion.

Red blood cells

Biconcave, packed with haemoglobin and no nucleus. Haemoglobin binds oxygen reversibly.

Defence + clotting

White blood cells defend against pathogens. Platelets are cell fragments that start blood clotting.

Explore the components of blood

GCSE trap: plasma is the liquid part; red blood cells are suspended in it. Blood is not simply “a red liquid”.
B4.2

The blood vessels

Structure is linked to function: arteries withstand high pressure, veins return blood at lower pressure, and capillaries exchange substances.

B4.3

The heart

The heart is a muscular organ forming two pumps in a double circulatory system.

Right side

Receives deoxygenated blood from the vena cava. The right ventricle pumps it to the lungs through the pulmonary artery.

Left side

Receives oxygenated blood from the pulmonary vein. The left ventricle pumps it around the body through the aorta.

Coronary arteries

Supply the heart muscle itself with oxygenated blood. The left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall because it generates higher pressure.

Follow one red blood cell through the heart

AQA scope: know aorta, vena cava, pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein and coronary arteries. Names of individual heart valves are not required.
B4.4

Helping the heart

Cardiovascular treatments must be evaluated by weighing benefits, limitations and risks.

Stents + statins

Stents hold narrowed coronary arteries open. Statins lower blood cholesterol and slow fatty deposit build-up.

Faulty valves + pacemakers

Faulty valves can be replaced biologically or mechanically. Artificial pacemakers correct irregular heart rhythms.

Heart failure

A donor heart may be transplanted. Artificial hearts may support a patient while waiting for a transplant or allow the heart to rest.

B4.5

Breathing and gas exchange

Ventilation maintains steep concentration gradients so oxygen diffuses into blood and carbon dioxide diffuses out.

Breathing in

Diaphragm contracts and flattens; ribcage moves up and out; thorax volume increases; pressure falls; air enters.

Breathing out

Diaphragm relaxes and domes; ribcage moves down and in; thorax volume decreases; pressure rises; air leaves.

Alveoli

Very large surface area, moist surface, one-cell-thick walls and rich capillary supply give rapid gas exchange.

Ventilation + alveolar gas exchange

Approximate air composition: oxygen falls from about 20% inhaled to about 16% exhaled; carbon dioxide rises from about 0.04% to about 4%; nitrogen stays about 80%.
B4.6

Tissues and organs in plants

Plant organs contain specialised tissues arranged to carry out particular functions.

Leaf tissues

Epidermis protects; palisade mesophyll contains many chloroplasts; spongy mesophyll has air spaces; guard cells control stomata.

Transport tissues

Xylem carries water and mineral ions. Phloem carries dissolved sugars.

Meristems

Growing tips of roots and shoots contain rapidly dividing cells that can differentiate into other plant cell types.

B4.8

Evaporation and transpiration

Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from leaves, mainly through open stomata.

1

Water enters root hair cells from soil.

2

Water moves up xylem in the transpiration stream.

3

Water evaporates from mesophyll cell surfaces and diffuses out through stomata.

Factors affecting transpiration

Temperature, humidity, air movement and light intensity can all change transpiration rate.

Apparatus technique

Potometer

A potometer measures water uptake, which gives an estimate of transpiration rate because most water taken up is lost by transpiration. It does not directly measure water loss.

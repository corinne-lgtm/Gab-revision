AQA GCSE Biology · B5 Communicable diseases

B5.6 Diseases caused by fungi and protists

Examples of plant diseases caused by fungi (rose black spot) and animal diseases caused by protists (malaria).

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Fungi damage plants by reducing photosynthesis. Protist diseases (like malaria) use a vector to transfer the pathogen — targeting the vector is key to control.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • Plant diseases caused by fungi: rose black spot
  • Animal diseases caused by protists: malaria
  • How spread of these diseases can be reduced/prevented

🌹 Rose black spot (fungal disease — plants)

🔬

What it is

A fungal disease of rose leaves. Causes purple or black spots on leaves. Leaves turn yellow and drop early.

Weakens the plant because it reduces the area of leaves available for photosynthesis → plant cannot flower well.

💨

How it spreads

Fungal spores spread in the environment by wind and by water splashes (rain splashing from leaf to leaf). Spores can stay dormant over winter on dead leaves and rose stems.

🛡️

Prevention and control

  • Remove and burn affected leaves and stems
  • Apply chemical fungicides
  • Breed resistant rose varieties — but disease cannot be completely prevented or cured

🦟 Malaria (protist disease — humans)

Malaria is caused by protist parasites that live and feed on other living organisms. It is spread by the bite of the female Anopheles mosquito — which acts as a vector.

Symptoms: Recurrent episodes of fever and shaking (as protists burst out of red blood cells). Affects the liver, damages red blood cells. Can be fatal — weakens the person over time even if it does not kill them immediately.

Global scale: Several hundred million cases per year; ~660,000 deaths annually.

Life cycle of the malaria protist

In the human body

Protists reproduce asexually → travel around circulatory system → infect liver cells → infect and burst out of red blood cells → causing fever and shaking

In the mosquito

Protists reproduce sexually → female mosquito feeds on 2 human blood meals before laying eggs → passes protists into human bloodstream when biting

Preventing malaria — targeting the vector

🛏️

Insecticide-treated nets

Prevent mosquitoes biting humans at night. Insecticide kills any mosquito that touches the net. Can reduce malaria incidence by up to 75% in some areas.

🪲

Insecticides (homes/offices)

Kill mosquitoes in buildings where people live and sleep.

💧

Remove standing water

Mosquitoes breed in still water — draining or treating standing water prevents larvae developing into adult mosquitoes.

💊

Antimalarial drugs

Travellers can take chemoprophylaxis (antimalarial drugs) before/during travel to kill protists if bitten. Also used for treatment if diagnosed early.

⚠️ Protists are becoming resistant to some of the most commonly used antimalarial medicines — this is a growing problem for control.

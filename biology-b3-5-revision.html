B3.5 Factors affecting enzyme action

How temperature and pH affect enzyme action, and why different enzymes work best at different conditions.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Temperature and pH both affect the shape of an enzyme's active site — changing them too much denatures the enzyme.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How temperature and pH affect enzyme action
  • Why different enzymes work best at different temperatures and pH values

Effect of temperature on enzyme action

🌡️

Low temperature

As temperature increases, molecules move faster — more collisions between enzyme and substrate → rate of reaction increases.

✅

Optimum temperature

The temperature at which the enzyme works fastest. Most human enzymes have an optimum of ~37°C (body temperature). At this point the active site and substrate collide most effectively.

🔥

Above optimum → denaturation

Above the optimum, high temperatures cause the amino acid chains to unravel — the shape of the active site changes. The substrate can no longer fit. The enzyme is denatured and stops working. The rate drops dramatically.

Reading the temperature graph (Figure 1): Rate rises as temperature increases → peaks at optimum (~37–40°C for most enzymes) → drops sharply as enzyme is denatured above optimum. The drop is rapid, not gradual — denaturation is irreversible.
⚠️ Study tip: Enzymes are not "killed" — they are molecules, not living things. Always say they are denatured. At 41°C, human enzymes begin to denature — this is why a dangerously high fever can be fatal.

Effect of pH on enzyme action

⚗️

How pH affects enzymes

The shape of an enzyme's active site is held in place by forces between parts of the protein molecule. A change in pH affects these forces — changing the shape of the molecule. If the shape of the active site changes, the substrate no longer fits and the enzyme stops working.

🎯

Optimum pH

Each enzyme has an optimum pH at which it works fastest. Different enzymes work best at different pH values:

  • Pepsin (stomach protease) — optimum pH ~2 (acidic)
  • Amylase (saliva) — optimum pH ~7 (neutral)
  • Trypsin (pancreas) — optimum pH ~8 (alkaline)
❌

Wrong pH → stops working

A pH that is too far from the optimum changes the shape of the active site — the enzyme is denatured (or its activity is greatly reduced). The substrate no longer fits.

Different enzymes, different optima

Not all enzymes work best at 37°C or pH 7. Extremophiles are organisms that live in extreme environments — their enzymes are adapted for those conditions:

  • Bacteria in hot springs survive at 80°C+ — their enzymes have a high optimum temperature
  • Bacteria in very cold, deep seas have enzymes that work effectively at 0°C
  • Enzymes from extremophile bacteria are used in PCR (polymerase chain reaction) for DNA fingerprinting and genome analysis

Fever — why it's dangerous

🤒

When you get an infectious disease, your body temperature rises (fever). This is a defence mechanism — many microorganisms cannot reproduce at high temperatures. However, if the body temperature rises too high (above ~41°C), human enzymes begin to denature — the reactions needed to keep the body alive slow down dangerously. This is why doctors try to bring down a very high fever.

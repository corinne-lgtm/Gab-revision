Key Facts & Formulas

Gradient → Acceleration

Core Principle

The gradient (slope) of a velocity–time graph equals the acceleration of the object. A steeper line means a larger acceleration.

acceleration = Δvelocity ÷ Δtime

a = (v − u) / t  ·  units: m/s²

📏A straight line on a v-t graph means constant acceleration. The increase in velocity is the same every second.
➡A horizontal line means the velocity is not changing → zero acceleration.
↘A line going downward (negative gradient) means the object is decelerating.

Area → Distance (Higher ⬆)

Higher Content

The area under a velocity–time graph represents the distance travelled (displacement) in the direction of motion.

🟨Rectangle area = base × height → distance at constant velocity
🔺Triangle area = ½ × base × height → distance while accelerating/decelerating

Braking — Worked Example

A car travels at 20 m/s for 10 s, then brakes to a stop in 5 s. Find the distances.

1

Distance at constant velocity (rectangle):

d = v × t = 20 × 10 = 200 m

This is the yellow shaded rectangle on Figure 2 (0 s to 10 s)

2

Distance while braking (triangle):

d = ½ × base × height = ½ × 5 × 20 = 50 m

This is the purple shaded triangle (10 s to 15 s)

3

Total distance = 200 + 50 = 250 m

⚠ Exam Tip

Always check what shape the area is: flat section = rectangle, sloping section = triangle. Don't forget the ½ for triangles!

🔄
Unit Conversion: km/h → m/s
Key fact

Because 1000 m = 1 km and 3600 s = 1 hour, a speed of 1 km/h = 0.278 m/s.

m/s = km/h ÷ 3.6

Always convert km/h → m/s before using graphs or equations

⚠If speed/velocity is given in km/h, you must convert to m/s before plotting on a v-t graph or using in a formula.
⚖️
Positive vs Negative Gradient

✅ Positive gradient

  • Line slopes upward
  • Velocity increasing
  • Object accelerating
  • a is a positive value

❌ Negative gradient

  • Line slopes downward
  • Velocity decreasing
  • Object decelerating
  • a is a negative value
📏Always draw graphs with a ruler for straight-line graphs in the exam.

Key Vocabulary
Velocity Speed in a given direction (vector), m/s
Acceleration Rate of change of velocity, m/s². Gradient of v-t graph.
Deceleration Negative acceleration — velocity is decreasing
Displacement Distance in a given direction — area under v-t graph
Gradient Slope of a line = Δy / Δx = Δv / Δt
Motion sensor Device linked to a computer to record changing velocity
km/h → m/s Divide by 3.6 (e.g. 1 km/h = 0.278 m/s)
Area (triangle) ½ × base × height → distance during acceleration/decel

B12.1

Types of reproduction

Learn

Asexual reproduction

One parent, no fusion of gametes, mitosis only. Offspring are genetically identical clones unless a mutation occurs.

Sexual reproduction

Male and female gametes fuse at fertilisation. Genetic information from two parents is combined, creating variation.

Gametes

Human sperm and egg cells contain 23 chromosomes. Fertilisation restores the full 46 chromosomes.

B12.2

Cell division in sexual reproduction

Learn

Meiosis

DNA is copied once, then the cell divides twice to form four genetically different gametes with half the chromosome number.

Fertilisation

Two haploid gametes fuse to form a diploid zygote. In humans, 23 chromosomes from each parent combine to make 46.

Variation

Gametes produced by meiosis contain different combinations of genetic information, increasing variation in offspring.

B12.3

DNA and the genome

Learn

DNA

DNA is a polymer made of two strands forming a double helix. It carries inherited information.

Genes and chromosomes

A gene is a section of DNA that codes for a particular sequence of amino acids to make a protein. Chromosomes contain many genes.

The genome

The genome is all of an organism's genetic material. Studying genomes helps scientists understand inherited disorders, disease risk and human migration.

B12.4 · Higher tier

Inheritance in action

Learn

Alleles

Alleles are different forms of a gene. A dominant allele is expressed with one copy; a recessive allele is expressed only when two copies are present.

Genotype and phenotype

Genotype describes the allele combination, such as BB, Bb or bb. Phenotype describes the observable characteristic.

More about genetics

Learn

Ratios and probability

Punnett squares predict expected genotype and phenotype ratios. Actual small families may not match the expected ratio exactly because fertilisation is random.

Sex determination

Females are XX and males are XY. Eggs carry X; sperm carry X or Y, giving an approximately 50:50 chance of XX or XY.

Family trees

Pedigree diagrams can be used to track an inherited characteristic and infer possible genotypes.

B12.6 · Higher tier

Inherited disorders

Learn

Polydactyly

The common form is caused by a dominant allele. A heterozygous affected parent crossed with an unaffected parent gives a 50% chance of an affected child.

Cystic fibrosis

Cystic fibrosis is caused by a recessive allele. Two unaffected carriers can have an affected child.

Carriers

A carrier has one recessive disease allele and one normal dominant allele, so does not show the recessive disorder.

B12.7 · Higher tier

Screening for genetic disorders

Learn

Amniocentesis

Usually carried out later than CVS. A sample of amniotic fluid containing fetal cells is removed and tested.

Chorionic villus sampling

CVS samples cells from the developing placenta and can be performed earlier in pregnancy.

Benefits and concerns

Screening can identify serious inherited disorders, but invasive tests carry a miscarriage risk and screening can raise ethical, social and economic issues.

B4.5 Breathing and gas exchange

The structure of the human gas exchange system and how gases are exchanged in the alveoli.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
The lungs ventilate to maintain steep concentration gradients so O₂ diffuses into the blood and CO₂ diffuses out — through adapted alveoli.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • The structure of the human gas exchange system
  • How gases are exchanged in the alveoli

Structure of the gas exchange system

👃

Nose / Mouth

Air enters here. Warmed, moistened, and filtered before reaching the lungs.

🫁

Trachea (windpipe)

The main airway from mouth/nose down to the lungs. Supported by rings of cartilage.

🌿

Bronchi → Bronchioles

The trachea splits into two bronchi (one per lung). These branch further into smaller bronchioles.

🫧

Alveoli (air sacs)

Tiny air sacs at the end of bronchioles where gas exchange takes place. Surrounded by capillaries.

💪

Diaphragm

A strong sheet of muscle below the lungs, separating chest from abdomen. Contracts and relaxes to change chest volume → ventilates the lungs.

🦴

Intercostal muscles + ribs

Muscles between the ribs that contract/relax to move the ribcage up and out / down and in — changing chest volume.

Breathing in and out — ventilation

💨 Breathing IN (inhalation)

  1. Intercostal muscles contract → ribs move up and out
  2. Diaphragm contracts → flattens downwards
  3. Volume of chest cavity increases
  4. Pressure inside chest decreases (lower than outside)
  5. Air is drawn into the lungs

💨 Breathing OUT (exhalation)

  1. Intercostal muscles relax → ribs move down and in
  2. Diaphragm relaxes → domes upwards
  3. Volume of chest cavity decreases
  4. Pressure inside chest increases (higher than outside)
  5. Air is forced out of the lungs
💡 Key principle: Air flows from HIGH pressure → LOW pressure. Lungs change pressure by changing volume — this is called ventilation.

Composition of inhaled vs exhaled air

GasBreathed IN (%)Breathed OUT (%)Change
Nitrogen~80~80No change — not used
Oxygen~20~16⬇️ Reduced — O₂ absorbed into blood
Carbon dioxide0.04~4⬆️ Increased — CO₂ released from blood
⚠️ Common misconception: We breathe in ~20% O₂ and breathe out ~16% — not all of it. We also breathe out ~4% CO₂ (not pure CO₂). Both oxygen AND nitrogen AND CO₂ are present in exhaled air.

Adaptations of the alveoli for gas exchange

📐

Very large surface area

Millions of alveoli provide a huge total surface area — equivalent to 10–15 tennis tables if spread flat. More area = faster rate of diffusion.

📏

One cell thick walls

The wall of each alveolus is only one cell thick — the shortest possible diffusion distance between air and blood.

🩸

Rich blood supply

Each alveolus is surrounded by a dense network of capillaries. Blood constantly carries O₂ away and brings CO₂ — maintaining steep concentration gradients in both directions.

💨

Ventilation maintains gradient

Breathing continuously replaces stale air with fresh air — keeping the concentration of O₂ high and CO₂ low in the alveoli so diffusion continues at maximum rate.

Exam tip

Common questions:
  • Describe how air is moved in and out of the lungs [3 marks]
  • Describe what is meant by gaseous exchange [1] + explain why it is important [2]
  • Draw a bar chart of air composition [3 marks]
  • People say "we breathe in O₂ and breathe out CO₂" — explain why this is wrong [2 marks]
  • Describe adaptations of the gas exchange system + explain how they make it efficient [6 marks]

Key answer for "why wrong": Exhaled air still contains ~16% O₂ and ~80% N₂ — it is not pure CO₂. Inhaled air also contains CO₂ (0.04%). We don't use up all the oxygen we breathe in.

B3.6 How the digestive system works

How food is digested in the body and the roles played by the different digestive enzymes.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Digestive enzymes break down large insoluble molecules into small soluble molecules that can be absorbed into the blood across the wall of the small intestine.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How food is digested in the body
  • The roles of the different digestive enzymes

The three digestive enzymes

Enzyme Where made What it catalyses Where it works Optimum pH
🍞 Amylase
(carbohydrase)		 Salivary glands, pancreas Breaks down starch → simple sugars (glucose) Mouth, small intestine ~7 (neutral/slightly alkaline)
🥩 Protease Stomach, pancreas, small intestine Breaks down proteins → amino acids Stomach (pepsin), small intestine ~2 (pepsin) / ~8 (trypsin)
🧈 Lipase Pancreas, small intestine Breaks down lipids → fatty acids + glycerol Small intestine ~7–8
💡 Remember: enzymes from the pancreas are passed into the small intestine — the pancreas does NOT digest food itself. Digestive enzymes work outside cells, unlike most enzymes which work inside cells.

Digesting carbohydrates — amylase

Starch is one of the most common carbohydrates in food (bread, potatoes, pasta). It is broken down into simple sugars by amylase.

  • Amylase is produced in salivary glands — digestion of starch begins in the mouth
  • Amylase is also made in the pancreas — the enzymes flow into the small intestine where most starch digestion is completed
  • No digestion takes place inside the pancreas

Digesting proteins — proteases

Proteins (meat, fish, cheese) are broken down into amino acids by protease enzymes.

  • Proteases are produced by the stomach (pepsin — works at pH ~2), pancreas, and small intestine
  • Breakdown of proteins takes place in the stomach and small intestine
  • The stomach also produces hydrochloric acid — providing the low pH needed for pepsin to work

Digesting fats — lipases

Lipids (fats and oils) are broken down into fatty acids and glycerol by lipase enzymes.

  • Lipases are produced by the pancreas and small intestine
  • Breakdown takes place in the small intestine
  • Bile (produced by the liver) emulsifies fats — breaks large fat droplets into small droplets, increasing surface area for lipase to work on

pH in the digestive system

🔴

Stomach — acidic (pH ~2)

Stomach glands produce hydrochloric acid — providing the optimum pH for pepsin (protease) and killing harmful bacteria in food.

🟢

Mouth — slightly alkaline (~7)

Saliva is slightly alkaline — ideal pH for salivary amylase to begin breaking down starch.

🔵

Small intestine — alkaline (~7–8)

Bile from the liver is alkaline — it neutralises the stomach acid as food enters the small intestine, providing the right pH for pancreatic enzymes (amylase, lipase, trypsin).

Question 1 — Wave Properties
4 marks

(a) Describe two features of a transverse wave that distinguish it from a longitudinal wave. [2 marks]

💡 How to Answer — "Describe two features" For each feature, name it AND briefly explain it. Don't just write one word. Compare directly: "In a transverse wave, particles oscillate perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer, whereas in a longitudinal wave they oscillate parallel." Give a specific example of each type.

(b) Describe two features of all electromagnetic waves. [2 marks]

(a) Any two from:

  • Particles oscillate perpendicular to the direction of wave travel/energy transfer ✓
  • Examples include light, water waves, seismic S-waves ✓
  • Can be polarised (longitudinal waves cannot be polarised) ✓
  • In longitudinal waves, particles oscillate parallel to direction of travel (making them different) ✓

(b) Any two from:

  • All travel at 3 × 10⁸ m/s in a vacuum / speed of light ✓
  • All are transverse waves ✓
  • All can travel through a vacuum / do not need a medium ✓
  • All transfer energy ✓

A student uses a ripple tank to investigate wave speed. They measure the frequency and wavelength of waves and record the following data.

Frequency (Hz)Wavelength (cm)Wavelength (m)Wave speed (m/s)

103.00.030

(a) Complete the wave speed column for all four rows and state what you conclude. [3 marks]

v = f × λ
💡 How to Answer — Data Analysis Questions Complete the table first showing full calculation for at least one row. Then look for a pattern. State your conclusion clearly: "The wave speed is constant/the same for all frequencies" is the key conclusion here. Relate this to the physics: changing frequency changes wavelength inversely, keeping speed constant.

(b) The student says frequency and wavelength are "inversely proportional." Explain what this means and how the data supports it. [3 marks]

(a)

  • v = 5 × 0.060 = 0.30 m/s; v = 10 × 0.030 = 0.30 m/s; etc. (all 0.30 m/s) ✓
  • All wave speeds are 0.30 m/s ✓
  • Conclusion: wave speed is constant regardless of frequency ✓

(b)

  • "Inversely proportional" means as one quantity doubles, the other halves ✓
  • Evidence: when frequency doubled from 5 to 10 Hz, wavelength halved from 6 cm to 3 cm ✓
  • This is because wave speed = f × λ is constant, so f × λ = constant → f ∝ 1/λ ✓
Question 3 — Refraction
4 marks

A light ray passes from air into glass at an angle of incidence of 50°. The angle of refraction is 31°.

(a) Explain why the light ray changes direction when it enters the glass. [2 marks]

💡 How to Answer — Refraction Explain Questions Two key points: (1) the wave changes speed when crossing the boundary, (2) this change in speed causes a change in direction (bending). Direction: air → denser medium = slows down = bends TOWARDS normal. Always measure angles from the normal, not the surface.

(b) State what happens to the frequency and wavelength of the light as it enters the glass. [2 marks]

(a)

  • Light slows down when it enters glass (denser medium) ✓
  • The change in speed causes the wave to change direction / bend towards the normal ✓

(b)

  • Frequency: stays the same / does not change ✓
  • Wavelength: decreases (because v = fλ, and v decreases while f stays constant) ✓

v = fλ → λ = v ÷ f

(b) Give one use of each of the following types of EM radiation and state one potential danger: (i) UV, (ii) X-rays, (iii) gamma rays. [4 marks — any 4 correct points]

Question 5 — The Motor Effect
4 marks

(a) Describe what is meant by the motor effect and state the equation for the force on a current-carrying conductor. [2 marks]

F = BIl
💡 How to Answer — Motor Effect Questions Motor effect: a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences a force. Use Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: thuMb = Motion (force), First finger = Field (B), seCond finger = Current (I). For the equation F = BIl: B in tesla, I in amps, l in metres, F in newtons.

(b) A wire of length 0.3 m carries a current of 4 A. The magnetic flux density is 0.05 T. Calculate the force on the wire. [2 marks]

(a)

  • A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force ✓
  • F = BIl (force = magnetic flux density × current × length) ✓

(b)

  • F = BIl = 0.05 × 4 × 0.3 ✓
  • F = 0.06 N ✓
Question 6 — Transformers and National Grid
4 marks

A transformer has 500 primary turns and 50 secondary turns. The primary voltage is 230 V.

(a) Calculate the secondary voltage and state whether this is a step-up or step-down transformer. [3 marks]

Vp/Vs = np/ns
💡 How to Answer — Transformer Questions Cross-multiply: Vp × ns = Vs × np → rearrange for the unknown. Step-up: more secondary turns → higher secondary voltage. Step-down: fewer secondary turns → lower secondary voltage. Ideal transformer: Vp × Ip = Vs × Is (power in = power out).

(b) Explain why step-up transformers are used when electricity enters the National Grid. [1 mark]

(a)

  • Vs = Vp × (ns/np) = 230 × (50/500) ✓
  • Vs = 230 × 0.1 = 23 V ✓
  • This is a step-DOWN transformer (secondary voltage lower than primary) ✓

(b) Step-up transformers increase voltage → reduce current → less energy wasted as heat in cables (P = I²R) ✓

Question 7 — Electromagnetic Induction (8 marks)
8 marks

Describe and explain how an alternating current (AC) generator works. In your answer, include the role of electromagnetic induction, how the output varies with time, and how the output can be increased.

💡 How to Answer — 8-Mark Level-of-Response Questions These are marked in levels (not per point). For Level 3 (7–8 marks): detailed, coherent, accurate explanation covering ALL aspects. Structure: (1) setup, (2) how it produces EMF, (3) how it produces AC, (4) how to increase output. Use technical terms: EMF, flux, induced current, slip rings, armature. Write at least 6–8 sentences.
Level Descriptors:
Level 3 (7–8): Detailed and coherent — describes setup clearly, explains induction, explains AC generation, and gives ways to increase output with scientific justification.
Level 2 (4–6): Describes how the generator produces an EMF and gives some detail about AC output, but may miss some steps or lack full explanation.
Level 1 (1–3): Basic statement that a rotating coil produces electricity, or mentions magnets/coils but without linking to induction.

Indicative content (must cover to reach Level 3):

  • Setup: A coil of wire is placed between the poles of a magnet (or a magnet rotates inside a coil) ✓
  • Induction: When the coil rotates, the magnetic flux through the coil changes ✓
  • This changing flux induces an EMF (electromagnetic induction / Faraday's law) ✓
  • The induced EMF drives a current in the circuit ✓
  • AC production: As the coil rotates, the direction of the induced EMF reverses every half turn ✓
  • This produces an alternating current (AC) — current reverses direction regularly ✓
  • Slip rings and brushes allow the current to flow to the external circuit while the coil rotates ✓
  • The output on an oscilloscope shows a sinusoidal (wave) pattern ✓
  • Increasing output: Use a stronger magnet / more turns of wire / rotate faster / use a larger coil — all increase the rate of change of flux, increasing the induced EMF ✓
Question 8 — Evaluate: Risks and Benefits of EM Radiation (8 marks)
8 marks

Evaluate the risks and benefits of using X-rays in medicine and the use of mobile phones (which emit microwaves) in everyday life. In your answer, consider the evidence for and against their use and reach a conclusion.

💡 How to Answer — "Evaluate" Questions "Evaluate" means: consider evidence FOR and AGAINST, weigh them up, and reach a justified conclusion. Structure: (1) Benefits with evidence. (2) Risks with evidence. (3) Factors that affect risk (e.g. dose, frequency). (4) Your conclusion — is the benefit worth the risk? Use qualifiers like "the evidence suggests", "on balance". This is a Level of Response question.
Level Descriptors:
Level 3 (7–8): Balanced evaluation — clear benefits and risks for both X-rays and microwaves, evidence cited, factors affecting risk discussed, coherent conclusion reached.
Level 2 (4–6): Benefits and risks given for at least one type of radiation, some evidence, partial conclusion.
Level 1 (1–3): States some uses or dangers without evaluating or reaching a conclusion.

Indicative content for Level 3:

  • X-ray benefits: essential for diagnosing bone fractures, detecting tumours, dental checks; alternative to invasive surgery; CT scans give 3D images ✓
  • X-ray risks: ionising radiation — can cause DNA mutations and cancer; high dose can damage cells; workers are exposed regularly → precautions needed (lead aprons, limited exposure) ✓
  • Risk factors: risk depends on dose — a chest X-ray gives very low dose; equivalent to a few days of background radiation ✓
  • Microwave (mobile) benefits: essential communication tool; 4G/5G enables emergency services; improves quality of life ✓
  • Microwave risks: microwaves are NON-ionising — cannot cause mutations directly; some studies suggest heating effect at high intensity; current evidence does not conclusively prove harm from normal phone use ✓
  • Conclusion: On balance, X-ray use in medicine is justified because benefits (life-saving diagnosis) outweigh small risk when doses are controlled. Mobile phone risk remains uncertain but current evidence suggests low risk at typical usage levels — precautionary approach reasonable ✓

Question 9 — Wave Behaviour: Diffraction
6 marks

Explain what diffraction is and describe how the degree of diffraction depends on wavelength and gap size. Include in your answer why radio waves can diffract around buildings but light cannot.

💡 How to Answer — Diffraction Explanation Questions Define diffraction first (spreading of waves at a gap/edge). State the rule: diffraction is greatest when wavelength ≈ gap size. Compare: radio waves (λ = metres) can diffract around buildings (metres wide); visible light (λ ≈ 500 nm) cannot diffract around buildings because λ << gap. Use examples to support each point.
  • Diffraction: the spreading out of waves when they pass through a gap or around an obstacle ✓
  • Greatest diffraction when wavelength is approximately equal to the gap size ✓
  • If gap is much larger than wavelength, very little diffraction occurs ✓
  • Radio waves have wavelengths of metres to kilometres ✓
  • This is comparable to the size of buildings/gaps in cities → radio waves diffract around them ✓
  • Visible light has wavelength ~400–700 nm (nanometres) — much smaller than buildings → no significant diffraction → cannot bend around buildings ✓
Question 10 — Ultrasound and Sonar
6 marks

A ship uses ultrasound sonar to measure the depth of the ocean. A pulse is sent down and returns after 0.24 s. The speed of sound in seawater is 1500 m/s.

Calculate the depth of the ocean. Explain why ultrasound is used instead of audible sound and why light cannot be used instead.

💡 How to Answer — Sonar Calculations Remember: the pulse travels DOWN and UP, so total distance = 2 × depth. Time = 0.24 s for the full journey. Use d = v × t, then divide by 2. Common error: forgetting to halve. For the explanation: ultrasound has short wavelength (high frequency) → less diffraction → sharper images. Light is absorbed by water. Ultrasound reflects off boundaries between materials.
d = v × t  |  depth = total distance ÷ 2
  • Total distance = v × t = 1500 × 0.24 = 360 m ✓
  • Depth = 360 ÷ 2 = 180 m (pulse travels to bottom and back) ✓
  • Ultrasound used because: ultrasound reflects well off boundaries (seabed) / it can penetrate water well ✓
  • Ultrasound is above 20,000 Hz → shorter wavelength → less diffraction → more precise readings ✓
  • Light cannot be used: light is absorbed rapidly by seawater and does not penetrate to significant depths ✓
  • Audible sound would work but gives less precise readings due to longer wavelength / more diffraction ✓

Question 12 — Electromagnetic Induction and Lenz's Law
6 marks

A bar magnet is pushed into a coil connected to a galvanometer. Describe what happens and explain how the size and direction of the induced current can be changed. Use scientific terms in your answer.

💡 How to Answer — Induction Description Questions Cover: (1) What you observe (galvanometer deflects). (2) Why (changing B-field induces EMF → current). (3) Factors affecting SIZE of EMF. (4) Factors affecting DIRECTION of current (Lenz's law: induced current opposes the change). Direction reversed by: reversing magnet, reversing poles, pulling magnet out.
  • As the magnet moves into the coil, the magnetic flux through the coil increases ✓
  • This changing flux induces an EMF (electromotive force) in the coil ✓
  • The induced EMF drives a current — galvanometer deflects ✓
  • Size increased by: moving magnet faster, using a stronger magnet, using more turns of wire ✓
  • Direction reversed by: pulling the magnet out instead, or reversing the poles (N→S) of the magnet ✓
  • The induced current always acts to oppose the change that caused it (Lenz's law) ✓
Question 13 — Evaluate: The National Grid (8 marks)
8 marks

Evaluate the design of the National Grid, including the use of step-up and step-down transformers and the use of high voltages for transmission. Consider the advantages of the current system and any limitations or alternatives.

💡 How to Answer — 8-Mark Evaluate (National Grid) Structure: (1) Purpose of step-up transformers at power stations. (2) Why high voltage reduces energy loss (use P = I²R). (3) Role of step-down transformers at homes. (4) Limitations: cost of infrastructure, dangers of high voltage, electromagnetic interference. (5) Alternatives: superconducting cables, local generation. Conclusion: overall verdict on the system's effectiveness.
Level Descriptors:
Level 3 (7–8): Full evaluation — explains how the system works using physics (P = I²R), gives advantages of high-voltage transmission, discusses limitations, and reaches a justified conclusion.
Level 2 (4–6): Explains high voltage reduces energy loss and describes role of transformers, but limited discussion of limitations or alternatives.
Level 1 (1–3): Basic statement that transformers change voltage or that high voltage reduces current.

Indicative content for Level 3:

  • Step-up transformers increase voltage from ~25,000 V to ~400,000 V when electricity leaves power stations ✓
  • High voltage means lower current for same power (P = IV) ✓
  • Less current means less energy wasted as heat in cables (P = I²R — power loss ∝ I²) ✓
  • Step-down transformers reduce voltage to 230 V for homes — safe for domestic use ✓
  • Advantage: very efficient over long distances; without high voltage, enormous energy losses would occur ✓
  • Limitation: very high voltages are dangerous — require extensive insulation and safety precautions ✓
  • Limitation: infrastructure is expensive to build and maintain; pylons affect landscapes ✓
  • Alternative: superconducting cables would have zero resistance → no heat loss, but very expensive to cool ✓
  • Alternative: local/distributed generation (solar, wind near homes) would reduce need for long-distance transmission ✓
  • Conclusion: the National Grid is a well-designed and effective system — high-voltage transmission greatly reduces energy losses; improvements possible but current system is justified given scale and efficiency ✓
Question 14 — Seismic Waves and the Earth's Structure
6 marks

Explain how seismic P-waves and S-waves are used to provide evidence for the Earth's internal structure. Include the properties of each type of wave and what their behaviour tells us about the Earth's interior.

💡 How to Answer — Seismic Wave Questions P-waves: longitudinal, can travel through solids AND liquids. S-waves: transverse, can ONLY travel through solids. Key evidence: S-waves don't reach the other side of Earth → outer core must be liquid. P-waves refract at boundaries → Earth has layers of different density. This links to the structure: inner core (solid), outer core (liquid), mantle, crust.
  • P-waves are longitudinal; S-waves are transverse ✓
  • P-waves can travel through solids AND liquids ✓
  • S-waves can only travel through solids — cannot pass through liquids ✓
  • S-waves are detected on one side of Earth but NOT on the opposite side (shadow zone) ✓
  • This tells us the outer core is LIQUID — S-waves cannot pass through it ✓
  • P-waves refract (change direction) as they pass through layers of different density, providing evidence for the layered internal structure of the Earth ✓
Question 15 — Evaluate: Benefits and Risks of Ionising Radiation in Medicine (8 marks)
8 marks

Evaluate the use of ionising radiation (including X-rays and gamma rays) in medicine. Consider the benefits to patients, the risks to patients and healthcare workers, and the precautions taken to reduce risk. Reach a justified conclusion.

💡 How to Answer — Medical Radiation Evaluate Questions This is a balanced evaluation. Benefits: diagnosis, cancer treatment (radiotherapy), sterilising equipment. Risks: ionising radiation causes DNA damage, mutations, cancer. Precautions: lead aprons, limiting exposure time, keeping distance, using minimum effective dose. Conclusion: benefits usually outweigh risks when precautions are followed — back this up with evidence.
Level Descriptors:
Level 3 (7–8): Comprehensive — specific benefits and risks given with explanations, precautions discussed with scientific reasoning, balanced conclusion reached with justification.
Level 2 (4–6): Benefits and risks listed with some detail, some precautions, limited conclusion.
Level 1 (1–3): States that radiation can cause cancer or is used in medicine, without development.

Indicative content for Level 3:

  • X-ray benefits: can image internal structures (bones, lungs, tumours) non-invasively; essential for fracture diagnosis ✓
  • Gamma ray benefits: used in radiotherapy to destroy cancer cells; can be targeted using rotating beam to minimise damage to surrounding tissue ✓
  • Gamma ray benefits: sterilise medical equipment (kill bacteria) — essential for safe surgery ✓
  • Risks to patients: ionising radiation can damage DNA → mutations → risk of cancer developing later ✓
  • Risks to workers: healthcare professionals receive repeated small doses over career — cumulative dose can be significant ✓
  • Precautions: lead aprons absorb X-rays, protecting areas not being imaged; minimum effective dose used ✓
  • Precautions: workers leave room during imaging; dosimeters worn to monitor exposure; exposure time minimised ✓
  • Conclusion: The benefits of ionising radiation in medicine generally outweigh the risks when precautions are correctly applied — cancer treatment and diagnosis save many more lives than the small cancer risk from exposure; risk-benefit analysis is always made before treatment ✓

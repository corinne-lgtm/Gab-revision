B5.2 Pathogens and disease

What pathogens are, how they cause disease, and how they are spread.

📖 Revision ✏️ Questions
Pathogens are microorganisms that cause disease — they enter the body and reproduce rapidly, producing toxins or damaging cells directly.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • What pathogens are
  • How they cause disease
  • How pathogens are spread

What are pathogens?

Pathogens are microorganisms that cause communicable (infectious) diseases. They include:

🦠 Bacteria — single-celled living organisms
🔴 Viruses — even smaller than bacteria
🍄 Fungi — common in plant diseases
🔵 Protists — single-celled organisms (e.g. malaria)
💡 Most bacteria are harmless or beneficial (e.g. making yoghurt, cheese, treating sewage, decomposers). Scientists estimate most people have 1–2 kg of bacteria in their gut! Pathogenic bacteria are the minority.

Bacteria vs Viruses — differences

🦠

Bacteria

  • Single-celled living organisms
  • Much smaller than animal/plant cells
  • Reproduce by binary fission (splitting in two)
  • Can live outside cells
  • May produce toxins (poisons) that affect the body
  • May also directly damage cells
  • Example diseases: tuberculosis, tetanus, salmonella, chlamydia
🔴

Viruses

  • Not cells — even smaller than bacteria
  • Usually have regular shapes
  • Take over host cells to reproduce — live and replicate inside cells
  • Burst out of cells (cell lysis) — causing cell damage and death
  • Cause diseases in every type of living organism
  • Example diseases: flu, HIV, measles, COVID-19

How pathogens cause disease

☠️

Toxins

Bacteria may produce toxins (poisons) that damage body cells and cause symptoms like high temperature, headaches, and rashes.

💥

Cell damage

Both bacteria and viruses can directly damage cells. Viruses replicate inside cells and burst out — destroying them. This triggers the immune response.

🤒

Symptoms

Common symptoms — high temperature, headaches, rashes — are mostly caused by the body's immune response to the damage and toxins, not always the pathogen itself.

How pathogens are spread

💨

By air (droplet infection)

When you cough, sneeze, or talk, you expel tiny droplets full of pathogens. Others breathe them in. Droplets travel at up to 100 miles per hour when you sneeze.

Examples: flu, tuberculosis, common cold.

🤝

By direct contact

Diseases spread by skin contact, sexual contact, cuts/scratches, or needle sharing. Animals can act as vectors carrying pathogens between hosts.

Examples: HIV, hepatitis, syphilis, chlamydia, rabies. Plant diseases: direct contact of infected plant material.

💧

By water

Fungal spores in water, or drinking/eating contaminated food or water (with sewage). The pathogen enters the body through the digestive system.

Examples: cholera, diarrhoeal diseases, salmonella.

💡 Lifestyle factors affect spread — overcrowded conditions with no sewage system mean infectious diseases spread much more rapidly.

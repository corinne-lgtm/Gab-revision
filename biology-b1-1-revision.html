Units — getting the scale right

📏

The scale of units

  • 1 km = 1000 m
  • 1 m = 100 cm
  • 1 cm = 10 mm
  • 1 mm = 1000 micrometres (μm)
  • 1 μm = 1000 nanometres (nm)
  • 1 nm = 0.000 000 001 m = 1 × 10⁻⁹ m
🔬

Why does scale matter?

Cells are measured in micrometres (μm). Cell structures (organelles) are even smaller — measured in nanometres (nm). You need to be comfortable converting between these units in exam calculations.

Light microscope vs Electron microscope

Feature 🔬 Light microscope ⚡ Electron microscope
Uses Beam of light Beam of electrons
Max magnification ×2,000 ×2,000,000
Resolving power ~200 nm ~0.2 nm (TEM) / ~0.2 nm (SEM)
Can magnify live specimens? ✅ Yes ❌ No
Cost & size Cheap, portable Very expensive, large, special conditions needed
Image type Colour, 2D TEM = 2D high magnification; SEM = 3D dramatic
Developed Mid-17th century 1930s
Key word — Resolving power: the ability to distinguish two separate points. Higher resolving power = more detail visible.

The magnification formula

magnification = size of image ÷ size of real object You can rearrange: size of real object = image size ÷ magnification  |  image size = magnification × real size

📐 How to calculate magnification

Multiply the eyepiece lens magnification by the objective lens magnification.

Example: eyepiece ×4, objective ×10 → total = ×40

🧮 Worked example

Image measures 5 mm, magnification is ×1000.

Real size = 5 ÷ 1000 = 0.005 mm = 5 μm

⚠️ Watch out!

Always check your units. Convert everything to the same unit before calculating. Exam questions often mix mm and μm — read carefully!

Key points to remember

💡

Light microscopes

  • Magnify up to ×2,000
  • Resolving power ~200 nm
  • Can view live specimens
  • Cheap and widely available
⚡

Electron microscopes

  • Magnify up to ×2,000,000
  • Resolving power ~0.2 nm
  • Show subcellular structures
  • Cannot view live specimens

Exam tip

Study tip: Make sure you can work out the magnification, the size of a cell, or the size of the image depending on the information you are given. Practice rearranging the formula in all three ways.

Common question types:

  • Give one advantage/disadvantage of a light microscope [2 marks]
  • Calculate real size from image size and magnification [3 marks]
  • Evaluate the use of electron vs light microscopes [6 marks]

B1.3 Eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells

The differences between eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells, how bacteria compare to animal and plant cells, and orders of magnitude.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Eukaryotic cells have DNA in a nucleus.
Prokaryotic cells have DNA free in the cytoplasm — no nucleus.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • Similarities and differences between eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells
  • How bacteria compare to animal and plant cells
  • Size, scale and orders of magnitude calculations

Eukaryotic cells

Animal cells, plant cells, fungi and protists are all eukaryotic.

  • All have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, and genetic material (DNA)
  • DNA is contained in chromosomes inside a nucleus
  • Typical size: 10–100 μm

Prokaryotic cells — bacteria

🦠

What bacteria have

  • Cytoplasm
  • Cell membrane
  • Cell wall (NOT cellulose — different material)
  • Genetic material — single DNA loop, free in cytoplasm (no nucleus)
  • Ribosomes (smaller than in eukaryotes)
  • Plasmids — extra small rings of DNA (not always present)
  • Flagella — long protein strand for movement (not always present)
  • Slime capsule — protective layer (not always present)
❌

What bacteria DON'T have

  • No nucleus — DNA is free in cytoplasm
  • No mitochondria
  • No chloroplasts
  • No permanent vacuole
  • No cellulose cell wall
Key: The bacterial chromosome is a single DNA loop found free in the cytoplasm — not enclosed in a nucleus.

Orders of magnitude

Used to compare sizes using powers of 10. Divide the bigger number by the smaller.

Result < 10 → same order of magnitude
Result ~10 → 1 order of magnitude bigger (10¹)
Result ~100 → 2 orders of magnitude bigger (10²)
Example:
Small animal cell = 10 μm
Large plant cell = 100 μm
100 ÷ 10 = 10 → plant cell is 1 order of magnitude (10¹) bigger
⚠️ Bacteria are 1–2 orders of magnitude smaller than eukaryotic cells. You could fit hundreds of thousands of bacteria onto a full stop.

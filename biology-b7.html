B7.1

Non-communicable diseases

Learn

What is a non-communicable disease?

Non-communicable diseases are not caused by pathogens and cannot be passed from one person to another. They develop because of a combination of genetic, lifestyle and environmental factors.

Risk factors

A risk factor is something linked with an increased chance of developing a disease. Examples include smoking, alcohol, diet, obesity, lack of exercise and exposure to carcinogens such as ionising radiation.

Correlation and causation

A correlation means two factors change together. It does not prove that one causes the other. A causal mechanism explains how one factor produces a biological change that leads to disease.

A correlation shows that two variables are associated. It does not prove that one causes the other.

B7.2

Cancer

Learn

Tumour formation

Cancer begins when control of the cell cycle is lost and cells divide uncontrollably, forming a tumour.

Benign and malignant tumours

Benign tumours remain in one place and do not invade other tissues. Malignant tumours invade nearby tissues and can spread in the blood or lymph to form secondary tumours.

Causes and treatment

Risk factors include smoking, some viruses such as HPV, ionising radiation and genetic factors. Treatments include radiotherapy and chemotherapy; both aim to destroy cancer cells but may also damage healthy cells.

A tumour forms when cells divide uncontrollably.

B7.3

Smoking and the risk of disease

Learn

Nicotine and carbon monoxide

Nicotine is addictive and affects the cardiovascular system. Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin and reduces the blood’s oxygen-carrying capacity.

Tar and carcinogens

Tar damages cilia in the airways, increasing mucus build-up and the risk of bronchitis and infection. Tar also contains carcinogens that increase the risk of lung cancer.

Smoking and pregnancy

Reduced oxygen supply can affect the fetus, increasing the risk of premature birth, low birthweight and stillbirth.

Nicotine is addictive and increases cardiovascular strain.

B7.4

Diet, exercise, and disease

Learn

Energy balance and obesity

If energy intake is greater than energy use over time, the excess is stored as fat. Obesity increases the risk of several non-communicable diseases.

Exercise and cardiovascular health

Regular exercise strengthens the heart, can lower blood pressure, improves blood lipid balance and reduces the risk of cardiovascular disease.

Obesity and type 2 diabetes

Being overweight or obese is a strong risk factor for type 2 diabetes, in which blood glucose regulation is impaired because insulin production or response is insufficient.

If energy intake stays above energy use, excess energy is stored as fat.

B7.5

Alcohol and other carcinogens

Learn

Alcohol and health

Alcohol affects the nervous system and can damage the liver and brain. Long-term heavy drinking can cause cirrhosis and increases the risk of liver cancer.

Alcohol and pregnancy

Alcohol crosses the placenta. Drinking during pregnancy increases the risk of miscarriage, premature birth, low birthweight and fetal alcohol spectrum disorders.

Ionising radiation

Ionising radiation can damage DNA and cause mutations, increasing cancer risk. Sources include radioactive materials, X-rays and some environmental exposures.

Long-term heavy alcohol use can damage the liver and brain and increase cancer risk.

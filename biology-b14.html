B14.1

Evidence for evolution

Learn

Fossils

Fossils are preserved remains or traces of organisms from the past. They provide snapshots of life millions of years ago.

How fossils form

Decay may be prevented, hard parts may be replaced by minerals, moulds and casts can form, and traces such as footprints may be preserved.

Incomplete record

Many organisms never fossilised, many fossils were destroyed by geological processes, and many have not yet been discovered.

B14.2

Fossils and extinction

Learn

Fossil record

Comparing fossils of different ages reveals changes in body form and lifestyle over long periods.

Extinction

Extinction is the permanent loss of a species. Environmental change, predators, diseases and competition can all contribute.

Horse evolution

The horse fossil record shows increasing body size and changes in feet from several toes to a single hoof.

Horse fossil sequence

Move from older small multi-toed forms to the modern horse.

B14.3

More about extinction

Learn

Environmental change

Long-term changes in climate and habitat can remove the conditions a species needs to survive and reproduce.

Mass extinctions

Mass extinctions affect many groups over a relatively short geological interval and can follow catastrophic events.

Dinosaur extinction

Evidence supports a major asteroid impact about 65 million years ago, but ancient events are reconstructed from multiple, incomplete lines of evidence.

B14.4

Antibiotic resistant bacteria

Learn

Mutation

A random mutation may make one bacterium resistant to an antibiotic.

Selection

The antibiotic kills susceptible bacteria. Resistant bacteria survive, reproduce and pass on resistance.

Reducing resistance

Avoid unnecessary prescriptions, complete prescribed courses and use strong infection-control measures.

B14.5

Classification

Learn

Linnaean hierarchy

Kingdom → phylum → class → order → family → genus → species.

Species

A species is a group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring.

Binomial naming

Scientific names use genus and species, for example Homo sapiens.

B14.6

New systems of classification

Learn

Three domains

Carl Woese used biochemical and molecular evidence to propose Archaea, Bacteria and Eukaryota.

Modern evidence

Microscopy, cell biochemistry and DNA comparisons can reveal evolutionary relationships invisible from appearance alone.

Evolutionary trees

Organisms sharing a more recent common ancestor are more closely related.

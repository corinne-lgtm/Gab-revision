Section A · Cells and Organisation · B1–B3
1
Describe two features of… · 4 marks
Describe two ways in which a red blood cell is adapted for its function of carrying oxygen.
4 marks

Give Feature/Adaptation 1, then explain how it helps the cell carry oxygen. Do the same for Feature/Adaptation 2. Keep them separate — 2 marks per feature (1 for naming it, 1 for the explanation).

  • Think about: shape, nucleus, haemoglobin, size, surface area
  • Don't just name the feature — always say how it helps
Structure: "One adaptation is [feature]. This means that [how it helps carry oxygen]."

Any two of the following (1 mark feature + 1 mark explanation):

  • Biconcave disc shape → increases surface area for oxygen to diffuse in and out quickly
  • No nucleus → more space inside the cell for haemoglobin, so more oxygen can be carried
  • Contains haemoglobin → the protein that binds to oxygen in the lungs and releases it in the tissues
  • Very small and flexible → can squeeze through narrow capillaries to reach all body tissues
  • Large number produced → ensures a constant supply for oxygen transport
2
Describe two features of… · 4 marks
Describe two features of mitosis and explain why it is important for the growth and repair of organisms.
4 marks

Give two separate features of mitosis (e.g. what it produces, chromosome number). For the importance, make sure you link it to growth or repair specifically.

Features (any two, 1 mark each):

  • Mitosis produces two daughter cells from one parent cell
  • The daughter cells are genetically identical to each other and to the parent
  • Each daughter cell has the same number of chromosomes as the parent (46 in humans)
  • The cell's DNA is copied before division so each daughter cell receives a full set

Importance (1 mark for each valid use):

  • Growth — produces new cells to increase the size of an organism
  • Repair — replaces damaged or dead cells (e.g. skin healing after a cut)
  • Asexual reproduction — some organisms reproduce by mitosis alone

  • Above 37°C the rate decreases ✓

  • Above the optimum, the enzyme's active site changes shape / the enzyme is denatured ✓
  • The substrate can no longer fit into the active site, so the enzyme-substrate complex cannot form ✓
Section B · Disease and the Body's Defences · B4–B6
4
Explain · 6 marks
Explain how the body's immune system responds when a new pathogen enters the body for the first time, and why a second infection by the same pathogen is usually defeated much more quickly.
6 marks

This question has two parts — make sure you answer both. For the first infection, describe the steps of the immune response in order. For the second infection, explain the role of memory cells. Use the correct terminology: antigens, antibodies, lymphocytes, phagocytes, memory cells.

  • Don't mix up phagocytes (engulf) and lymphocytes (produce antibodies)
  • Antibodies are specific — each antibody only fits one antigen
  • Memory cells allow a faster and larger response second time

First infection (up to 4 marks):

  • Pathogen enters the body and has antigens on its surface ✓
  • Phagocytes engulf and destroy some pathogens (non-specific) ✓
  • Lymphocytes recognise the specific antigen and produce specific antibodies ✓
  • Antibodies bind to antigens on the pathogen, neutralising it or marking it for destruction ✓
  • This takes time — the person may feel ill during this period ✓

Second infection (up to 2 marks):

  • Memory cells remain in the body after the first infection ✓
  • On second exposure, memory cells rapidly divide and produce a large quantity of antibodies very quickly ✓
  • The pathogen is destroyed before symptoms develop / the person does not become ill ✓
5
Explain · 8 marks
Explain why the development and testing of new drugs is a long and expensive process. In your answer, refer to the different stages of drug trials.
8 marks

For an 8-mark question, aim for at least 4 developed points. Don't just list the stages — for each one, explain why it is done and what could go wrong if it were skipped. Good structure: toxicity → efficacy → dosage → safety → large trials → cost/time reasons.

  • The stages: cell/tissue → animal → small human → large double-blind → peer review
  • Include why each stage is needed (ethical, safety, efficacy)
  • Mention the double-blind trial and placebo — and explain what they do
LevelMarksDescription
Level 37–8Detailed, well-organised explanation covering at least 3 stages with clear reasoning for why each is needed. Mentions double-blind/placebo and overall cost/time reasons. Logical structure throughout.
Level 24–6Describes at least 2 stages with some explanation of purpose. May mention safety/efficacy but lacks full development. Some structure.
Level 11–3Limited description of one or two stages. Little explanation of why stages are needed. Largely descriptive.

Key points to include:

  • Stage 1 — Preclinical (cells/tissues/computer models): Test if drug is toxic to cells at all before using living animals
  • Stage 2 — Animal testing: Test on living systems (whole organisms) to check for toxicity, side effects, and dosage in a full biological system. Required by law before human trials.
  • Stage 3 — Small human trials (Phase 1): Test on small number of healthy volunteers to check safety and find safe dose range
  • Stage 4 — Large clinical trials (Phase 3): Tested on hundreds/thousands of patients. Double-blind: neither patient nor doctor knows who gets drug/placebo → removes bias. Placebo controls for psychological effect.
  • Why long/expensive: Each stage takes years; failures at any stage waste investment; ethical approvals needed; large numbers of participants; peer review required before approval

This section contains seven short-answer questions (6 marks each). Answer all seven. These cover the full range of B1–B5 topics.
42 marks total

6a. Explain how the structure of a villus in the small intestine is adapted to absorb digested food efficiently. [6 marks]

Think about: surface area, thin walls, capillary network, lacteal, microvilli. For each feature, say how it increases absorption — don't just list them.

  • Villi have a large surface area → more absorption per unit time ✓
  • Microvilli on the epithelial cells further increase surface area ✓
  • Wall is only one cell thick → short diffusion distance for molecules ✓
  • Rich network of capillaries → blood carries absorbed sugars and amino acids away quickly, maintaining concentration gradient ✓
  • Lacteals absorb fatty acids and glycerol (into lymph) ✓
  • Good blood supply maintains a steep concentration gradient for diffusion ✓

6b. A patient has a blocked coronary artery. Explain what causes coronary heart disease and describe two treatments available. [6 marks]

Cause: fatty deposits → narrowing → reduced blood flow → heart muscle starved of oxygen. Treatments: stents, statins (or bypass surgery). Explain how each works — not just the name.

Cause (up to 2 marks):

  • Fatty deposits (atherosclerosis) build up in the walls of the coronary arteries ✓
  • This narrows the artery, reducing blood flow to the heart muscle ✓
  • The heart muscle is deprived of oxygen and glucose → heart attack may occur ✓

Treatments (1 mark name + 1 mark explanation, for two treatments):

  • Stents — a mesh tube inserted into the narrowed artery to hold it open and restore blood flow ✓
  • Statins — drugs that reduce blood cholesterol levels, slowing the rate of fatty deposit buildup ✓
  • Bypass surgery — a healthy blood vessel is used to create a new route around the blocked section ✓

6c. Describe the differences between active transport and diffusion. [6 marks]

A comparison question — use a structured approach. For each difference, state what happens in diffusion AND what happens in active transport. Cover: energy, direction, concentration gradient, carrier proteins.

  • Energy: Diffusion requires NO energy (passive); active transport requires energy (ATP) from respiration ✓
  • Direction: Diffusion moves substances DOWN a concentration gradient (high → low); active transport moves AGAINST the gradient (low → high) ✓
  • Carrier proteins: Active transport uses carrier proteins in the membrane; diffusion may or may not (facilitated diffusion does) ✓
  • Example of active transport: Absorption of glucose from the gut into blood when gut concentration is lower than blood ✓
  • Example of diffusion: Oxygen moving from lungs into the blood ✓
  • Active transport is a faster process than unaided diffusion when against the gradient ✓

6d. Explain the ethical issues surrounding the use of embryonic stem cells in medicine. [6 marks]

Present both sides — arguments for AND against. The marker wants to see you engage with the ethics, not just state facts. Use phrases like "Some people argue… however, others believe…"

Arguments FOR use (any 3):

  • Embryonic stem cells are pluripotent — can differentiate into almost any cell type → could treat many diseases ✓
  • Could be used to grow replacement tissues/organs (e.g. insulin-producing cells for diabetes) ✓
  • Embryos used are typically from IVF clinics and would otherwise be destroyed ✓
  • Research could save millions of lives in the future ✓

Arguments AGAINST use (any 3):

  • Some believe life begins at fertilisation → destroying an embryo is morally equivalent to ending a life ✓
  • Religious objections — many faiths oppose the destruction of embryos ✓
  • Concerns about commercialisation — could lead to embryos being "harvested" ✓
  • Adult stem cells or induced pluripotent stem cells may be alternatives that avoid the ethical problem ✓

6e. Explain how vaccination prevents the spread of a communicable disease through a population. [6 marks]

Cover: how a vaccine works (antigens → antibodies → memory cells), AND how vaccinating many people creates herd immunity. Include the idea that unvaccinated people are also protected.

  • Vaccine contains dead/weakened pathogen or its antigens ✓
  • The immune system produces antibodies specific to those antigens ✓
  • Memory cells are produced → on future exposure, rapid large antibody response → person doesn't become ill ✓
  • If large proportion of population is vaccinated → herd immunity ✓
  • Pathogen cannot spread easily because most potential hosts are immune ✓
  • Even unvaccinated/vulnerable individuals (e.g. babies, immunosuppressed) are protected because the disease cannot spread ✓

6f. A scientist is investigating osmosis in potato cells. Describe a method she could use and state what results she would expect. [6 marks]

This is a required practical style question. Describe the actual method step-by-step: cut cylinders, measure mass, place in different concentrations, record mass change. Then predict results using osmosis theory.

Method (any 4):

  • Cut potato cylinders of equal length/mass using a cork borer and scalpel ✓
  • Prepare a range of sugar/salt solution concentrations (e.g. 0%, 0.25%, 0.5%, 0.75%, 1.0%) ✓
  • Dry the cylinders and record their initial mass ✓
  • Place one cylinder in each concentration for the same length of time (e.g. 30 min) ✓
  • Remove, dry, and record final mass; calculate percentage change in mass ✓

Expected results (any 2):

  • In distilled water / very dilute solution → potato gains mass (water enters by osmosis from high to low water potential) ✓
  • In very concentrated solution → potato loses mass (water leaves cells by osmosis) ✓
  • At the isotonic point → no change in mass (water potential of solution = water potential of cells) ✓

6g. Evaluate the use of statins in reducing the risk of cardiovascular disease. Include both benefits and risks in your answer. [6 marks]

"Evaluate" means give a balanced answer AND a conclusion. Present benefits, then risks/limitations, then state your overall view. Don't just list — weigh the evidence.

Benefits (any 3):

  • Statins reduce blood LDL cholesterol levels ✓
  • Lower cholesterol slows or prevents atherosclerosis (fatty deposit build-up in arteries) ✓
  • Reduces risk of heart attack and stroke ✓
  • Large clinical studies show statins significantly reduce cardiovascular mortality ✓
  • Can be taken as a daily tablet — convenient ✓

Risks/limitations (any 2):

  • Side effects include muscle pain (myopathy), liver damage, and increased risk of type 2 diabetes ✓
  • Must be taken long-term — risk returns if stopped ✓
  • Some patients do not respond to statins ✓
  • Controversy about who should be prescribed them — some argue healthy lifestyle changes are preferable ✓

Conclusion (1 mark):

  • A balanced judgement — e.g. "Overall, statins are beneficial for high-risk patients as the reduction in cardiovascular events outweighs the risk of side effects for most people" ✓

Volume and Surface Area for Prisms
Question 1[5 marks]

A triangular prism has a cross-section which is a right-angled triangle with base 6 cm and height 8 cm. The length of the prism is 12 cm.

(a) [2 marks]
Work out the volume of the prism.
(b) [3 marks]
Work out the total surface area of the prism. (First find the hypotenuse of the triangular cross-section.)

Mark Scheme — Q1 [5 marks]

  • [1] (a) Cross-sectional area = ½ × 6 × 8 = 24 cm²
  • [1] (a) Volume = 24 × 12 = 288 cm³
  • [1] (b) Hypotenuse = √(6²+8²) = √100 = 10 cm
  • [1] (b) Surface area = 2×(24) + (6+8+10)×12 = 48 + 288
  • [1] (b) = 336 cm²
Converting Units (Areas and Volumes)
Question 2[4 marks]
(a) [2 marks]
Convert 3.2 m² into cm².
(b) [2 marks]
Convert 540 000 cm³ into m³.

Mark Scheme — Q2 [4 marks]

  • [1] (a) Recognises 1 m² = 10 000 cm² (i.e. 100² cm²)
  • [1] (a) 3.2 × 10 000 = 32 000 cm²
  • [1] (b) Recognises 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ (i.e. 100³ cm³)
  • [1] (b) 540 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = 0.54 m³
Volume of Cones, Pyramids and Spheres Grade 7+
Question 3[6 marks]
(a) [2 marks]
Find the volume of a sphere with radius 6 cm. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (V = 4/3 πr³)
(b) [2 marks]
Find the volume of a cone with base radius 4 cm and height 9 cm. Leave your answer in terms of π. (V = 1/3 πr²h)
(c) [2 marks]
A square-based pyramid has base side 5 cm and height 12 cm. Find its volume. (V = 1/3 × base area × height)

Mark Scheme — Q3 [6 marks]

  • [1] (a) V = 4/3 × π × 6³ = 4/3 × π × 216 = 288π
  • [1] (a) = 905 cm³ (3 s.f.)
  • [1] (b) V = 1/3 × π × 4² × 9 = 1/3 × π × 16 × 9
  • [1] (b) = 48π cm³
  • [1] (c) Base area = 5² = 25 cm²
  • [1] (c) V = 1/3 × 25 × 12 = 100 cm³
Sectors Grade 7+
Question 4[5 marks]

A sector of a circle has radius 9 cm and angle 120°.

(a) [2 marks]
Find the arc length of the sector. Leave your answer in terms of π.
(b) [3 marks]
Find the area of the sector, to 1 decimal place. (Use π = 3.14159)

Mark Scheme — Q4 [5 marks]

  • [1] (a) Arc length = (120/360) × 2π × 9
  • [1] (a) = 6π cm
  • [1] (b) Area = (120/360) × π × 9²
  • [1] (b) = (1/3) × π × 81 = 27π
  • [1] (b) = 84.8 cm² (1 d.p.)
Angles and Shape Equations
Question 5[4 marks]

A quadrilateral has angles 2x°, 3x°, 4x° and (x + 30)°. Find the value of x, and hence find the size of each angle.

Mark Scheme — Q5 [4 marks]

  • [1] Sets up equation using angle sum of a quadrilateral = 360°: 2x+3x+4x+(x+30) = 360
  • [1] Simplifies: 10x + 30 = 360 → 10x = 330
  • [1] x = 33
  • [1] Angles: 66°, 99°, 132°, 63° (sum checks to 360°)
Angles in Polygons
Question 6[5 marks]

A regular nonagon has 9 sides.

(a) [2 marks]
Find the sum of the interior angles of the nonagon. (Use (n−2) × 180°)
(b) [1 mark]
Find the size of each interior angle.
(c) [2 marks]
Find the size of each exterior angle.

Mark Scheme — Q6 [5 marks]

  • [1] (a) (9−2) × 180
  • [1] (a) = 1260°
  • [1] (b) 1260 ÷ 9 = 140°
  • [1] (c) Uses 360 ÷ 9, or 180 − 140
  • [1] (c) = 40°
Angles in Parallel Lines
Question 7[4 marks]

Two parallel lines are cut by a single transversal. One of the angles formed is 65°. Angle a is co-interior to the 65° angle, and angle b is alternate to the 65° angle.

Find angle a and angle b, giving a geometric reason for each.

Mark Scheme — Q7 [4 marks]

  • [1] a = 180 − 65
  • [1] a = 115° — co-interior (allied) angles sum to 180°
  • [1] b = 65°
  • [1] Correct reason: alternate angles are equal

B1.7 Osmosis

How osmosis differs from diffusion, the meaning of isotonic/hypertonic/hypotonic, and why osmosis is important in animal cells.

🏠 Main hub 📚 Biology ⚡ Quick check
Osmosis is the movement of water from a dilute solution to a concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane.
🎯

Learning objectives

  • How osmosis differs from diffusion
  • Why osmosis is important in animal cells

Osmosis vs Diffusion

💧

Osmosis

  • Only water molecules move
  • Through a partially permeable membrane
  • From dilute solution (high water concentration) to concentrated solution (low water concentration)
  • A special type of diffusion
⚡

Diffusion

  • Any soluble, small particles can move
  • Through any permeable barrier or freely in solution/gas
  • From high concentration to low concentration
  • No membrane needed
🧱

Partially permeable membrane

The cell membrane only lets some types of particles through. Water molecules are small enough to pass through. Large molecules like glucose cannot.

The cytoplasm is a fairly concentrated solution of salts and sugars — so water can move in or out.

⚠️ Study tip: When writing about osmosis, always specify whether it is the concentration of water or of solutes you are referring to. A dilute solution has a high concentration of water and a low concentration of solute.

Isotonic, Hypotonic and Hypertonic

Isotonic
Solution outside = same concentration as inside the cell
😊
Cell stays normal — no net movement of water
Hypotonic
Solution outside = lower concentration than inside the cell
💥
Water moves INTO cell → cell swells and may BURST
Hypertonic
Solution outside = higher concentration than inside the cell
🥀
Water moves OUT of cell → cell SHRIVELS
💡 Memory trick: HYPOtonic = below → water goes IN (cell inflates). HYPERtonic = above → water goes OUT (cell shrivels).

Osmosis in animal cells

🔄

If cell uses water

The cytoplasm becomes more concentrated. The surrounding fluid becomes hypotonic to the cell, so water moves into the cell by osmosis — restoring the balance.

💧

If cell gains water

The cytoplasm becomes more dilute. The surrounding fluid becomes hypertonic to the cell, so water moves out of the cell by osmosis — restoring the balance.

⚠️

If balance is lost

  • Too hypotonic outside: too much water enters → cell swells and bursts
  • Too hypertonic outside: too much water leaves → cytoplasm becomes too concentrated → cell shrivels and dies
⚖️

Why balance matters

The concentration of the solute surrounding body cells must be kept as constant as possible. Osmosis can cause big problems if the concentration outside the cell changes dramatically.

Dilute vs Concentrated — key reminder

💧 Dilute solution

  • High concentration of water (solvent)
  • Low concentration of solute (e.g. sugar)
  • Water moves out of this solution by osmosis

🍬 Concentrated solution

  • Low concentration of water
  • High concentration of solute
  • Water moves into this solution by osmosis
